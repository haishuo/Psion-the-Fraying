# Hairline

**A Starter Chronicle for Psion: The Fraying**

*A one-session scenario (3–4 hours) for a starting group of Gifted built with [Character Creation — Building a Gifted](Character%20Creation%20-%20Building%20a%20Gifted.md). It is designed to teach the [Fraying engine](The%20Fraying%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md) at the table by making the players live it, and to land the setting's central moral equation in a single night: how much freedom is worth how many lives, and what you owe a person whose own power is killing them. It needs no prior canon beyond the four mechanics docs.*

> **Content note.** This scenario is about a friend dying — or becoming a monster — and a frightened child in danger, with no clean way out. That is the genre working as intended, but it is heavy. Run a session-zero, agree on lines and veils, and give every player an explicit out to slow down or step back. The setting calls execution mercy; the table should not have to.

---

## The Pitch (for players)

One of yours is fraying. You've all seen it — the headaches she waves off, the half-second lag between what she means and what she does, the way she rubbed her arm last week and said it felt like it belonged to someone else. Mara is the strongest of you, which is exactly the problem: she's the one who carries the heavy jobs, and she's been carrying too many.

Tonight there's a child. Newly Triggered, no training, screaming on every frequency a Gifted can hear — and the lattice is already turning toward the sound. You have a few hours to reach a terrified eleven-year-old before the people who "help" children like that arrive to do it their way.

It's a simple job. Get in, calm the kid, get out. The only hard part is that the calming is mental work, and the strongest mind in the room is the one that can least afford to spend itself.

---

## What's Really Going On (Storyteller)

**Mara Sokol is already at Stage 3.** Months of being the cell's heavy hitter have worn her tether down, and she has been hiding it — from the others and from herself — with the discipline of someone who genuinely believes resting now would get someone killed. She has high Willpower and low Tether, which is the most dangerous combination in the game: she will not break under temptation, so she keeps choosing to push, and her tether cannot bear the choices her will keeps making. She is the Julian Vire problem in miniature. *(Tether traits, [Fraying engine §7](The%20Fraying%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md).)*

**The child — Kit, age 11 — is fully Unshielded.** Barriers 0, no bracing, leaking in both directions and psionically deafening. That loudness is the engine of the scenario: it is how the PCs can find Kit, and it is how the **Panopticon** is already getting a fix. An Inquisition team is inbound. *(The [Unshielded rules](The%20Fraying%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md), and the [Antagonists doc](Antagonists%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md) for the team.)*

**The trap of the night** is that calming Kit requires sustained **Intrusion** work (re-bracing, [Domains §Barriers](The%20Seven%20Domains%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md)), and Mara is the only one strong enough to do it cleanly — so the job itself will push the one person who cannot afford to be pushed. By the climax she will be at **Stage 4**, and the players will decide, with dice on the table, whether she pushes once more.

**Two outside pressures close the vise:** the Inquisition (who are not villains — they are here to contain a deafening Unshielded child before it draws worse attention, and to handle a fraying Gifted by the only doctrine they have), and **Brother Ezra**, a Cult of the Unbound recruiter who has been gently circling Mara for weeks and who will, at the worst possible moment, offer her the lie that snapping is freedom.

There is no clean win. There is only what the players are willing to pay, and who pays it.

## The Cast

**Mara Sokol** — *the fraying friend; the heart of the scenario.*
Gifted Operative (see [template](Antagonists%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md)), with: **Intrusion 4**, Kinesis 3, Barriers 2, Detection 2 · **Tether 2** · **Stamina 2** · **Willpower 6**.
She begins at **Stage 3** (6 boxes filled — 2 Strain, 4 Fray from months of overuse). Highest Domain 4 → **if she snaps, she becomes a Fresh Psidolon**, not a clean death. Her Snap Check pool is a terrifying **Tether 2 + Stamina 2 = 4 dice vs 8.** Play her as warm, competent, and quietly exhausted — someone who jokes about the headaches because the alternative is admitting what they are.

**Kit** — *the Unshielded child, age 11.* Barriers 0, **psionically loud** (−2 difficulty for anyone, including the Panopticon, to locate or read them). Terrified, leaking others' thoughts and broadcasting their own panic. Under stress Kit suffers **involuntary Bleed** — objects trembling, a wave of borrowed terror washing over everyone nearby. Not a combatant; a clock with a heartbeat. The emotional core: this is who everyone is fighting over.

**Inquisitor Tomas Hale** — *the doctrine, with a face.* Use the [Inquisitor template](Antagonists%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md); leads a team of **2 more Inquisitors + 1 Memory specialist** (Intrusion 3 / Memory 3). Hale is not cruel. He is tired, careful, and completely certain that a loud Unshielded child and a Stage-3 Gifted are exactly the situation his doctrine exists for. Give him one line that makes the players hate that they understand him: *"I'm not the worst thing coming for that kid tonight. I'm the most careful one. There's a difference, and someday you'll wish you'd let me prove it."*

**Brother Ezra** — *the tempter.* A soft-spoken Cult of the Unbound recruiter, kind-eyed and patient, who has been a sympathetic ear to Mara for weeks ("you give and give and they take and take; doesn't anyone ask what *you* need?"). He believes, sincerely, that untethering is liberation. He is catastrophically wrong, and he has watched it kill before and decided not to know. Stat him minimally; his weapon is a sentence, delivered at the brink: *"You don't have to hold it anymore, Mara. You were never meant to. Just let go."*

## Party Framing

The default assumes the PCs are a small **off-grid cell** — Railroad, Lacuna, or unaffiliated — who rescue people the system would process. Mara is one of their own.

- **Project cell (default):** No lattice monitoring means *they* are Kit's only warning system and Mara's only doctor. The horror is that self-monitoring is fallible and there is no safety net but each other.
- **MetaConcert team (variant):** The PCs are a junior Integrator/Operative team *dispatched* to retrieve Kit — and Mara is a teammate whose Stage-3 condition the Panopticon is about to flag. Now the dilemma inverts: report her (doctrine, a White Room) or cover for her (complicity, and a snap risk you chose to allow). Inquisitor Hale is their own backup, which makes him worse, not better.

Both framings run the same three acts. Choose before session zero; it changes who the Inquisition is *to* the players.

## Act One — The Tell

*Goal: introduce the cell and Mara, let the players notice the fraying, and give them their first low-stakes taste of using a Domain (and paying a little Strain).*

Open on the cell getting the tip — a loud new signature across town, a child, and a narrow window. Stage a small, calm beat that requires light psionic use: reading a nervous informant (Skim, 2 Strain), scouting the building (Thoughtsense or Remote Viewing, 1–2 Strain), quieting a suspicious neighbor (a Suggestion, 2 Strain). Let everyone spend a box or two and see that it's nothing. This is the baseline against which the rest of the night will feel like falling.

**The tell.** Somewhere in here, let the players catch Mara's fraying — don't hand it to them; make them earn it:

> Call for **Perception + Awareness (diff 7)**, or let anyone using **Detection** on Mara roll **Perception + Detection (diff 7)**; a Gifted with **Tether Sense** reads it outright.

> *On a success, read aloud:* "Mara laughs at your joke half a second too late — like the sound reached her from across a longer room than the one you're in. When she reaches for her coffee her hand arrives before she does, knocks it, catches it, plays it off. For just a moment the skin around her eyes looks like a photograph of itself."

If they push her on it, she deflects with love and competence: *"I'm fine. I'll rest after. There's a kid, okay? There's a kid right now."* She is not lying about the kid. That's what makes it work.

**What this teaches:** Strain is cheap and recoverable at low Stages (they'll rest tonight's boxes off by morning — *if* tonight stays small), and fraying has *symptoms you can read*, not just numbers. Establish the symptom language now so Act Three's numbers have a body.

## Act Two — The Loud Room

*Goal: the extraction. Spend real Strain, meet the Unshielded state, start the Inquisition clock, and push Mara to Stage 4.*

Kit is in a second-floor apartment, alone, parents already drawn off or memory-fogged by an earlier MetaConcert pass (Storyteller's choice — a chilling detail either way). The moment the PCs get close, the **loudness** hits:

> *Read aloud:* "You feel Kit before you see the door — a wall of noise with no words in it, every raw feeling a frightened child has ever had arriving all at once and from the inside. Your own thoughts skid. Someone's nose starts to bleed. Down the block a car alarm goes off, then another, then all of them."

**The Unshielded child.** Kit cannot be reasoned with through the door — the leakage drowns speech. Calming Kit means **bracing** them: gentle, guided Intrusion that teaches a panicking mind its first boundary. *(Re-bracing: **Manipulation + Intrusion, diff 7**, across roughly a scene; restores bracing — the baseline containment, not a Barriers dot — and ends the Unshielded state — [Domains, Bracing](The%20Seven%20Domains%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md).)*

Mara (Intrusion 4) is the obvious choice and she knows it. If a PC with strong Intrusion volunteers instead, **let them** — this is the players choosing who pays, and it's a *good* choice to reward. Whoever braces Kit:
- Pays **2 Strain to open the channel**, then grinds the diff-7 roll over several turns against a torrent. The raw Unshielded mind inflicts **+1 Strain each turn** the bracing continues (the noise pushes back). A botch means a surge of Kit's borrowed terror — everyone in the room takes a turn of disorientation and the bracer takes an extra Strain.
- Realistically spends **4–6 Strain** here. For Mara, starting at Stage 3, that lands her in **Stage 4.**

**The clock.** While the bracing grinds, the Inquisition closes. Track it openly — players should *see* the vise:

> **The Lattice Clock (4 segments).** Mark one at the start of Act Two and one each turn the bracing continues (Kit's loudness is a beacon). At **2 marks**, distant sounds of an arriving team. At **3**, Hale's people are in the building. At **4**, they're at the door. Bracing Kit *silences the beacon* — the instant Kit is braced, stop the clock. The faster they calm the child, the more room they have to run; the more they rush, the more Strain they burn. The trap is built into the timer.

**Forcing the brink.** However the bracing resolves, the exit is now contested — Hale's team between them and out. Make the clean escape require one big psionic act: a **Kinesis** working to collapse a stairwell, hurl a dumpster across the alley mouth, or shield the group from a doorway (Kinesis, base 4, +modifiers — easily **5–6 Strain** under fire). If Mara is the one who does it (and she'll volunteer), she is now deep in **Stage 4**, flagged, loud with her own fraying, and one push from the edge — with a child in her arms and the Inquisition on the stairs.

End the act on that image. Hold it. Take the break here.

**What this teaches:** the Unshielded state and bracing; sustained Strain under a clock; how desperation and rushing *manufacture* fraying; and the exact mechanism by which a normal job walks a Gifted to the brink without anyone making a single reckless choice.

## Act Three — The Brink

*Goal: the impossible choice, with a live Snap Check on the table.*

The cell is cornered — a rooftop, a stairwell, a dead-end alley — with Kit, with Mara at Stage 4, with Hale's team closing and **Brother Ezra** somehow already there, calm in the chaos, looking only at Mara.

Lay the situation bare for the players. The way out requires one more significant push — and at Stage 4, **any psionic use forces a Snap Check: Tether 2 + Stamina 2 = 4 dice vs difficulty 8, and Willpower cannot help her.** Everyone at the table should understand those odds before anyone decides anything. Then let them decide.

**Ezra's whisper.** As Mara weighs it, Ezra speaks — to her, gently, the practiced kindness of the Cult: *"You've held them all up for so long. You don't have to anymore. What they call the snap, we call the door. Just let go, and the pain stops, and you'll finally be free."* He believes it. He is wrong. If Mara is at all tempted to push *because part of her wants the release*, that is the most honest thing this scenario can ask a player to sit with.

**Honor the table's choices.** There is no scripted out; reward cleverness and cost:
- **Someone else pays.** A PC pushes *their own* tether to make the escape so Mara doesn't have to — sharing the curse, and seeding that PC's own fraying arc. This is the best outcome the system offers, and it costs.
- **They refuse the push.** They surrender, or sacrifice the escape, to keep Mara from rolling. Hale takes them — the child to "integration," Mara to a White Room. Everyone lives. Nobody is free. The "safe" betrayal is its own ending.
- **They let Mara roll.** The Snap Check happens live. Read the dice as the dice fall.
- **They turn to Hale.** Bargaining with the Inquisitor — Kit's safety for Mara's surrender, or the reverse — is legitimate and characterful. Hale will deal, within doctrine. He always keeps the letter of it. That's the horror of him.

### If the Snap Check happens

- **She holds (success):** she takes **+1 Fray** and stays — gray, shaking, *here.* They get out, or they don't, but Mara is still Mara. Bittersweet: she is now buried in Fray, flagged, and the road from here is short unless something changes. The chronicle has its first scar and its first reason to keep going.
- **She snaps (failure):** the tether goes. Mara is gone between one word and the next, and a **Fresh Psidolon** (Coherence 3, Burning Out, [Antagonists](Antagonists%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md)) is wearing her — desperate, powerful, no longer her. The friend the party spent all night protecting is now the thing the setting is built to fear, and it is reaching for the nearest body, which may be Kit's, or a PC's. *(See scaling note below — do not let this be an automatic TPK.)*
- **Botch:** catastrophic — she comes apart without even the coherence to become a monster. Just gone. For some tables this is the more merciful result, which is the cruelest thing the scenario can teach.

## Outcomes & Aftermath

- **Kit lives, Mara holds.** A genuine, costly win. Kit reaches a Children of Gaia sanctuary; Mara faces a long, frightened convalescence she may not get. The cell has learned exactly what their work costs and that they cannot keep paying it the way they have. *Hook: the Panopticon now has Mara's signature. They will be back.*
- **Kit lives, Mara falls.** They saved a stranger's child and lost one of their own to do it — the Railroad's whole tragedy in one night. *Hook: grief, and the question of whether it was worth it, becomes the cell's defining argument.*
- **The Inquisition takes them.** Hale keeps his word, whatever it was. Someone goes to a White Room. The survivors carry the math. *Hook: a rescue — or a reckoning — aimed at the people who let it happen.*
- **A psidolon walks.** If a Fresh psidolon escaped into a host, the cell now has a personal monster wearing a face they loved, loose in their city. *Hook: this is a campaign. (And the cell now understands, in their bones, why the Inquisition does what it does.)*

## Running It

**What it teaches, by act:** Act One — Strain is cheap, fraying has readable symptoms. Act Two — the Unshielded state, sustained Strain, clocks, and how ordinary desperation manufactures fraying. Act Three — Stage-4 lethality, the Snap Check, Willpower's helplessness against the tether, and the moral equation with no clean side. A group that finishes *Hairline* understands the whole engine, because they felt it instead of reading it.

**Scaling the snap so it isn't a TPK.** A Fresh psidolon is **Coherence 3 and Burning Out (loses 1 Coherence/hour, faster under stress)** — it is on a timer and desperate, not invincible. A starting party with no Memory specialist *cannot wipe it*, and that's intentional: their realistic options are to **deny it a host and survive the window** (it dissipates), drive it off, or — bleakest of all — let **Hale's team** do the thing Hale's team exists to do, turning the night's enemy into the night's grim salvation. Telegraph these. Never corner the players into a slaughter; the lesson is grief and complicity, not a body count.

**Pacing.** This is one tight session. Keep Act One short, let Act Two breathe (the bracing-under-clock is the mechanical heart), and give Act Three room — the choice should not be rushed, even though everything *in* the fiction is. When the dice come out for the Snap Check, slow all the way down. That roll is the whole game in four dice.

**Adjusting difficulty.** Tougher table: start Mara one box deeper, or make Hale's team larger. Gentler table: give Mara Stamina 3 (Snap pool 5 dice), or let a successful, un-rushed bracing leave her with enough margin to reach Act Three at Stage 3 instead of 4 — so the brink is a threat she can still refuse. The dials are the same ones in the [Fraying engine's](The%20Fraying%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md) Storyteller notes.

**The one thing to get right.** Mara is not a plot device. Spend the first hour making the players *like* her. Everything the scenario is depends on the moment in Act Three being a decision about a person, not a stat block — on the players wanting, badly, for four dice to come up eights, and knowing they probably won't.
