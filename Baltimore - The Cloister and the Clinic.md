# Baltimore: The Cloister and the Clinic

**The Default Chronicle Setting for Psion: The Fraying**

*Every game needs a place to start. Psion's is Baltimore — because no American city embodies the setting's themes more completely, and few embody them by accident. This is the cloister and the clinic made real: the city that invented the American research university, a temple of elite medicine with a documented history of research done on the unconsenting, one of the most surveilled cities in the country, and a place where gleaming institutions stand a few blocks from some of the nation's hardest poverty. It is Edgar Allan Poe's city. It was always going to be ours.*

> **A note before the city — read it.** This setting borrows real institutions and a real city, and some of what makes Baltimore *fit* is real harm done to real people: research conducted without consent, decades of surveillance and disinvestment, communities treated as material. Henrietta Lacks was a real woman; the cells taken from her without her knowledge are real; her family's grief is real and recent. Use this history as *atmosphere and resonance* — the true thing the fiction stands beside — and never as a plot trophy, a villain's set-piece, or a character to fictionalize. The horror here is effective precisely because it rhymes with something that actually happened. Treat what actually happened with respect. (See *Running Baltimore*, below.)

## Why Baltimore

**The first research university.** Johns Hopkins opened in 1876 as the first American university built on the German research model — the prototype every American research university copied. To the Gifted, this is not trivia; it is lineage. **Praxis** and American academic medicine grew up as twins, and Baltimore is where that twinship is oldest and deepest. The Baltimore Seat is a Praxis stronghold — one of the founding chapters of the MetaConcert in North America, and still one of its most important. If the American Gifted have a spiritual capital, it is here, in the place that taught the country how to turn a university into an instrument.

**The clinic with a past.** Johns Hopkins Hospital is one of the great hospitals of the world, and its real history includes the kind of research the MetaConcert embodies — work done on bodies and minds whose owners never agreed and were never told. The fiction does not have to manufacture a consent-violating research institution for the Gifted to hide inside. Baltimore handed it one. The Phipps Psychiatric Clinic — among the first psychiatric units inside a general hospital — is a particular gift: an entire tradition of the institutional management of "disordered" minds, the perfect cover for everything the MetaConcert does to the Unshielded, the frayed, and the inconvenient.

**The watched city.** Baltimore has been a live testbed for mass surveillance — a dense municipal camera network, predictive policing, and, for several months in 2016, a private aircraft secretly photographing the entire city from the sky. The **Panopticon** does not need to build its apparatus here. It only had to plug into the one the city already had, and let the cameras and the records and the quiet aerial eye carry the weight its lattice cannot.

**The divided map.** And Baltimore is a city of brutal proximity — endowed chairs and marble hospital lobbies a few blocks from disinvested neighborhoods that have been surveilled, policed, and abandoned in turn. The MetaConcert's elite institutional power and the Projects' work among the people that power discards are not abstractions here. They are *addresses,* a ten-minute walk apart.

## The Two Poles: Homewood and East Baltimore

The Baltimore Seat is organized, like the city, around two campuses that are nothing alike.

**Homewood — the Cloister.** The undergraduate campus, red brick and Georgian quads in leafy North Baltimore, ringed by Charles Village row houses. This is where the power *surfaces* — in students, far from home, sleepless and stressed and primed for the trauma that pulls the trigger. Homewood is the recruiting ground: the **Integrators** work it like a sinister honor society, and **Project Railroad** works it right back, both racing to reach a newly Triggered nineteen-year-old before the other does. It is also where dissent lives — **Heterodox** faculty teaching in the same departments as the orthodoxy they've broken with, arguing the framework is wrong from inside the institution that built it.

**East Baltimore — the Clinic.** The medical campus: the hospital, the dome, the School of Medicine, the research towers — and, hidden among them, the things the Seat does not advertise. The local **White Room** is here, behind a door in a restricted research wing or a locked Phipps ward, where a frayed Gifted brought in for "treatment" raises no questions in a building full of them. **Praxis's** real laboratories are here; so, it is whispered, are the ones that aren't on any floor plan. And the medical campus sits in the middle of neighborhoods the institution has loomed over for a century — which is exactly where the **Projects**, the **Cult**, and the desperate all are. The clinic gleams. The blocks around it do not. Both belong to the story.

**The rest of the board.** The University of Maryland's own medical campus and law school sit downtown — a second academic-medical pole, and contested ground. The **Peabody** conservatory in genteel Mount Vernon, and the marble-stepped gentility around it, is where the Seat's senior figures keep their respectable social lives. The **Inner Harbor's** restaurants and hotels are neutral ground where the **Operatives** meet Baltimore's Kindred. And out past the Beltway, the university's classified applied-research arm does the kind of work that has no public clearance — the obvious front for Praxis projects that officially do not exist.

## The Seat: The MetaConcert in Baltimore

Power here wears a faculty ID. The Baltimore Seat runs on appointments, directorships, grant committees, and hospital privileges — and the people who hold them.

**Dr. Vivian Ashford-Cole** *(Praxis — the Seat's effective head).* An endowed professor and department chair in the neurosciences, internationally published, beloved by her graduate students, and the coldest mind in the city. She runs the Baltimore Seat the way she runs her lab: by data, by procedure, and without the comfort of guilt. She is the smallpox-repository argument made flesh — *you cannot defeat what you refuse to study* — and the persistent rumor among the few who know her is that somewhere under the East Baltimore campus, in a room with no number, she keeps a **specimen.** Whether it is true is the kind of question that gets people Sterilized for asking. *(Stat as a senior Praxis figure: Detection/Memory/Intrusion high, Tether 3, formidable Willpower.)*

**Dr. Asha Iyer** *(Inquisition — the White Room).* An attending psychiatrist at Phipps, gentle with her living patients, who also runs the Seat's White Room and has performed more terminations than anyone in the city. She is on her last permitted rotation; everyone can see what the work has done to her, and no one says so. She believes, completely, that she is the most merciful person in the building. *(Inquisitor template; carries Deadening and Mind Barbs; Tether 3.)*

**"Wren"** *(Panopticon — the watcher).* No one uses the analyst's name. They work from an unremarkable office wired into both the lattice and the city's real surveillance — the cameras, the license readers, the data brokers, and the institutional memory of a place that has always watched its poor. Wren is how a flag becomes an address in Baltimore, and they are very, very good at it. *(Panopticon Analyst template: Detection 5, Trace, never seen in the field.)*

**Dean Walter Adler** *(Integrators — the recruiter).* The warm, rumpled dean-of-students figure every undergraduate trusts, which is the entire point. When a student Triggers, Adler is the kind hand on the shoulder, the discreet referral to "a program that can help" — the smile at the front of the trap. He genuinely believes he is saving them. He is usually not wrong, which is what makes him unbearable. *(Integrator: Intrusion-focused, the bracing specialist.)*

Beneath them: a Maintainer who keeps the Seat's binding constructs and the White Room's isolation; a small Preserver staff buried in the medical library and the archives; and Operatives who manage the wary arrangement with Baltimore's Kindred and the city's other powers.

## The Resistance: The Projects in Baltimore

The same two campuses, read from the other side.

**Theo Calloway** *(Railroad — the conductor).* A former Integrator who could not keep doing to children what Adler does, Calloway runs Baltimore's Railroad out of East Baltimore — intercepting newly Triggered students and frayed runaways before the Seat reaches them, and moving them out to Children of Gaia sanctuary in the Maryland hills along the Patapsco. He knows Adler personally. They trained together. Neither has forgiven the other for the choice the other made. *(Gifted Operative; Intrusion/Detection; carries the weight of every extraction that failed.)*

**"Static"** *(Lacuna — the privacy engineer).* In the most surveilled city in the country, someone has to build the spaces the Panopticon and the cameras both cannot see. Static is a counter-surveillance obsessive — half privacy-tech, half psionic dead-zone craft — who makes the Railroad's safe houses possible and lives in terror of the day Wren finally notices the *shape* of all the places that have gone quiet. *(Detection/Barriers; the *Off the Lattice* and *Quiet Mind* Merits.)*

**Dr. Elaine Voss** *(Heterodox — the apostate scholar).* Once Praxis, now the most dangerous kind of dissident: a tenured professor in Ashford-Cole's own department who argues, in seminars and unpublishable papers, that the Domain framework is *wrong* — incomplete, self-serving, a cage the MetaConcert mistook for a map. She is watched constantly and Sterilized never, because removing a tenured Hopkins professor cleanly is harder than letting her talk. The academic schism between Praxis and Heterodox, in Baltimore, is a faculty feud between two people who share a hallway. *(Praxis-trained; the academic legitimacy is her armor.)*

**Brother Ezra Pell** *(the Cult of the Unbound).* The Cult works the margins the institutions abandoned — the support groups, the shelters, the frayed and the failing and the desperate who slipped through every net. Ezra is soft-spoken, genuinely kind, and catastrophically wrong, offering the snap as a door and "transcendence" to people the MetaConcert would simply Sterilize. He has watched it kill before. He keeps preaching. *(He is the tempter at the brink; see the [Cult](The%20Cult%20of%20the%20Unbound.md).)*

## Flashpoints

Ready-to-run pressure. Each is a chronicle's worth of trouble.

- **The Tap.** A brilliant Homewood sophomore Triggers during finals — loud, Unshielded, terrified. Adler is already drafting the referral; Calloway already has a car. The party has hours, and whichever way they move, they make an enemy who used to be a friend. *(This is [Hairline](Adventure%20-%20Hairline.md), with the city's names attached.)*
- **The Room With No Number.** Someone the party trusts goes into the East Baltimore campus "for tests" and comes out *calmer.* The question of what Ashford-Cole keeps down there — a Cognitive Reconstruction patient, a Sterilization no one logged, or the specimen the rumors insist on — is the kind of question that ends careers and lives.
- **The Eye.** Static finds proof that Wren has tapped the city's real surveillance apparatus and routed it through the lattice — a panopticon over Baltimore that sees the living and the Gifted both. Exposing it could break the Seat. Reaching the proof means going somewhere the Eye is already looking.
- **The Apostate's Problem.** Voss has found something — real evidence the framework is wrong, the kind Heterodox has chased for a generation — and the only reason she's still breathing is that no one's sure how much she's written down. The party can protect her, debunk her, deliver her, or finish what she started. Praxis is watching all four options.
- **The Wake.** A psidolon is hunting in East Baltimore, and the dead it has left behind — the wraiths of its consumed hosts — are trying, in the only ways the dead can, to point the living at it. The Inquisition will not work with the Shadowlands. The party might have to. *(See [The Dead and the Untethered](Psidolons%20-%20The%20Untethered.md).)*

## Running Baltimore

**Use the geography.** The two-campus split does your thematic work for free. Keep asking which pole a scene belongs to — the cloister or the clinic — and let the ten-minute walk between the gleaming institution and the abandoned block do what no amount of exposition can.

**Power wears a lanyard.** Resist the urge to make the MetaConcert look like a conspiracy from a film. In Baltimore it looks like a faculty meeting, a grant decision, a referral to a specialist, a kindly dean. The horror is that it is *respectable,* and that everyone in the building thinks they are helping. Your scariest villain is a beloved professor with tenure and a clean conscience.

**Honor the real city — both parts.** This is the important one. Baltimore is a real place full of real people, and the same gothic resonance that makes it perfect for this game comes from real disinvestment, real surveillance, and real medical harm. Do not turn its struggling neighborhoods into a horror-movie backdrop or its residents into scenery. Do not fictionalize real victims of medical exploitation, and do not let the fiction's invented horrors imply the real ones were anything less than real. The Projects work among the marginalized because the setting is *about* who institutions discard — play that with the weight it deserves, not as local color. When in doubt, the rule is the rule for the whole game: it should hurt because it's true, not because it's lurid. Talk to your table; agree on the lines; mean it.

**And let it breathe.** Baltimore is also crab houses and marble stoops and the Ravens and a particular gallows humor and a fierce local love. A city that is *only* grim is a lie about Baltimore, and a chronicle that is only grim burns its players out. Let there be charm in Charm City. It makes the dark land harder.
