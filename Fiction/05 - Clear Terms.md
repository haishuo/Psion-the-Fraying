# Clear Terms

*The fifth of the* Memories *— illustrative fiction for Psion: The Fraying, not rules. Marcus Thiel, in his own voice. See [the cycle index](00%20-%20Marcus%20Thiel%20-%20Story%20Arc.md).*

---

After Doreen, I went looking for people who would never offer me coffee.

I do not mean that unkindly. I mean that the warmth of the strays had shown me a thing about myself I did not like: that I could want it, and that wanting it was a door, and that I had spent two years making certain I had no doors. So I went where there was no warmth to flinch from. I went to the dead.

The Kindred had use for me, and I for them. I can do things to a memory that none of their Disciplines can manage — lift a witness's whole evening out clean, find the one buried hour in a man's head that a Prince needs and his rivals would kill for, close a breach in their precious secrecy without leaving a body to explain. I did not charge in blood, and I did not charge in loyalty, which made me strange to them and valuable in the same breath. I charged what a working man charges — money, mostly, because a body has to eat and the rent does not care what you are mourning. But with the ones who knew things, I would set the fee aside for something I wanted more than money: *tell me where it has been. Anything you have heard, from anywhere, about a thing that wears a face that is not its own.*

The one I dealt with most went by Cherub. He had not been beautiful in five hundred years and had a sense of humor about it that almost none of his kind ever manage — the name was the whole joke, and he told it on himself every time he said it, which is the only sort of vanity I have ever trusted. He kept his accounts the way some men keep a faith: every favor in, every favor out, ruled and dated in a ledger I was never shown and never once doubted. With Cherub you always knew exactly where you stood. He would tell you to your face that he would sell you the instant your price was met — and because he said it plainly, you also knew he would not sell you a moment before.

That was the thing I had not expected to find restful, and did. With Doreen there was always the danger of the open door. With Cherub there was no door at all — only a counter, a price, and a handshake that meant precisely what it said and not one thing more. A transaction keeps no ledger of the heart. I could stand in that cold little court beneath the city and breathe in a way I never could beside a warm fire, because nothing in it wanted anything from me that I had not already decided to sell.

I came to prefer the company of monsters who knew they were monsters. Cherub never once told me he was helping me. He never called the leash a hand. The institution that raised me lied with every kindness it ever did me; Cherub lied only in his name, and his name said the lie out loud, on purpose, so that no one could be fooled by it. A creature that is honest about being a liar is a kind of honest man. I have trusted far worse for far less.

I was never careless, understand. The Camarilla and the people I ran from keep an arrangement — the dead make their messes, my old masters tidy them away, and one clean hand washes the other — and a man like me is exactly the kind of loose thread that arrangement exists to notice and snip. So I gave clear terms and I kept them, to the letter, and not a single drop past the letter, and I always knew where the door was even in the rooms that had none. Clear terms cut both ways. They were how I stayed useful. They were also how I stayed alive.

Whatever sunless place I had spent the night working in, I would come up before dawn, find somewhere quiet, and do the one thing all the rest of it is for. I keep her where the cold cannot reach — which is only to say where nothing can. The Kindred live in a permanent winter and call it survival; I understood them better than they ever guessed. I have a room like theirs. I simply keep one warm thing in mine, and I would burn the rest of myself to the ground before I let it go out.

I built a whole life out of clean transactions in those years, and I believed — correctly — that it was the safest way for a man like me to live. Nothing owed that could not be paid. Nothing given that could not be itemized. No one ever close enough to cost me anything I had not already priced.

I was right about all of it.

Right up until the transaction that refused to stay one.
