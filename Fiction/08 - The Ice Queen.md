# The Ice Queen

*The eighth of the* Memories *— illustrative fiction for Psion: The Fraying, not rules. Marcus Thiel, in his own voice. See [the cycle index](00%20-%20Marcus%20Thiel%20-%20Story%20Arc.md).*

---

The work came to me the way Kindred work always came — through a chain of favors with a monster at every link — and at the end of this one was a Ventrue who needed a mess made to disappear.

I have cleaned up after a great many of the dead, and they run to type: the panicked young ones, the bored old ones, the theatrical ones who want you to admire the mess before you erase it. She was none of these. She was the coldest thing I had ever stood in a room with, and I have stood in rooms with the Inquisition.

Isolde. Four centuries dead, born in a French village she would not name and I would not learn of for years. She received me the way a great house receives a plumber — with the precise, frictionless courtesy that is its own kind of insult, the courtesy that has already measured exactly what you are worth and found the sum very small. She did not dislike me. Dislike would have required noticing me. She had a face built for centuries of getting her way and a stillness that made the air feel watched. When she described the mess — a feeding gone wrong, two witnesses, a body that needed a story — she spoke of it the way a banker speaks of a rounding error. *Control,* I would much later hear her call it — the only mercy her kind has to give: keep your grip, and the mortal wakes forgetting; lose it, and there is a body. She spoke as one who had never lost hers. It would be a while before I learned about the night she had. That first night I thought only: *ice, top to bottom. The Ice Queen.*

The job itself was clean enough. I lifted the evening out of two frightened people and left them a gentler lie to keep — a gas leak, a bad fall, something with no fangs in it — and I closed the breach without leaving a body to explain. I gave her clear terms, the way I gave everyone clear terms, and I did the work well, because work done well is the only signature I allow myself. She paid me a flat, fair fee — in money, the way one pays a tradesman, because rent is rent — and then, because she was the kind who heard things, I pressed for the part I actually wanted: a scrap of what she had heard, traded flat across the table, about a thing somewhere that wore a face not its own. It was thin. I took it. They were all thin in those days; I was building a picture out of thin things.

Here is the part I did not understand until much later, and it shames me a little to set down. She looked straight through me — the janitor, the useful function, the man you need not remember — and I let her, because being unremembered is the whole of my craft and there is a cold comfort in being seen as exactly the nothing you have worked to become. But I did the very same to her. I, who can hold a stranger entire in a single glance, never once turned that on Isolde. I had no reason to. She was a client. You do not read the furniture. I had spent years making a refuge out of clean transactions for precisely this reason — a transaction lets you not see the person on the far side of it — and so I stood in a room with her and aimed my one real gift at the wallpaper.

Not long before, by a different water, I had met a girl I *could not* read, and it had felt like being handed something. Now I stood across from a woman I simply *would not,* and felt nothing at all, and called it professionalism. I have turned that pairing over many times since. The mind I couldn't open, I truly met. The mind I could have opened with a thought, I left shut, because opening it was not in the contract. My power has never once made me less alone. It is my refusals that did the damage.

This became an arrangement. She had messes; I made them vanish; she paid in cold scraps and colder courtesy, and I banked it and asked nothing more, because nothing more was on offer and nothing more was safe. I cleaned up after her three times before either of us saw the other at all.

One of those three jobs I would not understand for a long time. She handed it to me as a stale witness — a memory gone soft that she wanted put to sleep for good — and I filed it like any other and forgot it, until the day I set it beside the map I keep of the thing I hunt and saw whose worst night I had buried for her, and for how flat a fee. But that is a later memory. This one is only the ice.

That is not a love story. It is not any kind of story yet. It is only the account of two careful, frozen people who came within a hair of letting the most important person each would ever meet walk back out the door as a client and a contractor — paid in full, owing nothing, strangers.

We very nearly managed it.
