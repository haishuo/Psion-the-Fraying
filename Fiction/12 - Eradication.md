# Eradication

*The twelfth and last of the* Memories *— illustrative fiction for Psion: The Fraying, not rules. Marcus Thiel, in his own voice. See [the cycle index](00%20-%20Marcus%20Thiel%20-%20Story%20Arc.md).*

---

I will not tell you where the room was. It does not matter, and some things I still keep. What matters is that after ten years I walked into a room and my sister's face turned to look at me — grown the way she never got to grow — and smiled with a mouth that had not been hers since she was eight.

Isolde stood at one door and Eve at the other: the two walls in the world it could not climb. I had explained it to them a dozen times and they had understood it the first. It survives by leaping, and there was no one in that room for it to leap into. One of them was dead. One of them was impossible. And the third — me — was the mind it had wanted from the very start, the one door still open, which is exactly why I had to be the one to go in, and why they were there to hold the rest of the world shut while I did.

I will not describe the unmaking in detail, because it was the worst work of my life and because some of it is in the room I do not open. I will say what it is, plainly, the way I have learned to say hard things: you go in, and you take the thing apart. A psidolon *is* its memories. So you reach into the accumulated years of it and you begin, carefully, to erase — every face, every name, every stolen hour, peeled off a layer at a time until there is not enough left to hold together. It is precisely what they did to my mother. I have never once pretended otherwise. I did it on my sister's face.

It fought. Of course it fought; everything fights at the end, even a thing with no one home to do the fighting. And when it understood what was happening — when enough of it was gone that the rest could feel the edge coming — it did the only thing it has ever done. It lunged. It reached for the nearest mind to climb into, the way it reached for Isolde by the harbor eleven years ago, the way it reaches for anything as it drowns.

It reached for me.

It had wanted me since I was twelve — the good house, the strong host, the one that got away. And in that last moment it threw everything it had left against the walls of me, to climb in and wear me out into the world and leave Elena's body on the floor and begin again.

It could not get in.

It could not get in when I was twelve and untrained and broken with grief, and it could not get in now, when I am the most walled mind either of us had ever met. Isolde had a fang bared and Eve had put herself bodily between me and the far door, and in the end neither of them was needed, because the oldest fact of my life turned out to be the one that saved it: I am a locked house. It broke against me like water on stone, and fell back, and I closed the last of it in my hands.

Here is the thing no one warned me of. To erase all of it I had to go *through* all of it — down past every face, backward through every theft, all the way to the bottom, to the first memory, the one beneath the years. To the person it had been before it was the thing.

And it had been a person. That is the part I will carry a long time.

At the very bottom, before everything else, there was a young man in a circle of gentle people who called each other Brother and Sister — who spoke of a cocoon and an emergence, who told him the fraying tearing him apart was not death but a *becoming,* that he was a caterpillar and had only to be brave enough to break. They cupped his terror in their hands and called it grace. And he chose it. He walked into the unmaking on purpose, believing he would come out the other side with wings.

The thing that took my sister was not an accident. It was not a Gifted who slipped. It was *made* — deliberately, tenderly, by people who do it on purpose and call it liberation.

I have spent ten years keeping one memory safe from erasure. At the very end, with the last of the creature coming apart in my hands, I did it again. I reached past everything I was destroying, and I lifted one thing out, and I kept it: the circle. The Brother who smiled. The exact shape of the lie that built the thing that wore Elena.

Then I finished it. And it was gone — all of it, every face but the one I took, unmade and scattered past any cohering, the way you put out a fire by taking away the fuel.

The body did not die. It breathes, somewhere safe now, vacant, nobody home — Elena's grown face with no one behind it, which is a grief of its own and not the one I came for. I did not get my sister back. I never could; it was never on the table; you do not unspeak a death.

But I lived. I had braced for ten years not to, and I did — and that means the rope did not let go, which means *she* did not let go, somewhere on the far side, still held, still mine, no longer worn by anything at all. The thing is off her at last. Her face is her own again, even with no one wearing it. And I am alive to keep her — and, Vale tells me, alive now with the hunt no longer eating every hour, perhaps to do more than keep her. Perhaps to reach her. We will see. I have time I never expected to have. It is the strangest gift I have ever been handed.

So that is the end of the thing I have been writing toward my whole remembered life. The creature is unmade. I survived it. I even, in the only way that was ever possible, kept her.

I should feel finished.

I am holding a memory I took out of a dying monster, and in it a circle of kind people are making another one — right now, tonight, somewhere — smiling while they do it. There is no one in a psidolon to hate; I have always known that; it is what let me keep my hands clean for ten years. *You do not hate a virus. You eradicate it.*

But the Cult are not a virus. The Cult are people. Deluded, gentle, certain people, with souls — people who could be argued with, or wept over, or — and here is the new thing, the thing whose shape I do not yet know myself — *stopped.*

Eradication is the right word for what I did in that room. It is the only word I have ever needed.

I do not yet know the word for what the Cult are. I am afraid I am going to learn it the way I have learned everything that ever mattered: too late, and from the inside.

That is where the record stops, because that is where I am. The rest of it has not happened yet.

But I have the memory. And I have, for the first time in my life, a reason to still be here when it does.
