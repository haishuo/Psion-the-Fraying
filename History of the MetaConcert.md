# History of the MetaConcert

**A Timeline**

*A compiled timeline. The Circuit documents each carry their own founding context; this lays the whole sequence out in order and cross-links back to them for detail on any single beat.*

---

## I. The Age of Heroes (deep past — no fixed date, deliberately unpinned)

No Inquisition. No Praxis. No MetaConcert. Just a scattering of powerful, mostly solitary Gifted — "an age of titans who answered to no one," remembered where remembered at all as wizards, oracles, monsters, saints. Because no one hunted them, psidolons ran rampant: a psidolon could wear a life for decades, even a throne. Some of history's most famous sudden personality-replacements — rulers and beloved figures alike, turned overnight into strangers — may have been psidolons wearing a crown, though the setting is careful never to claim this of any specific ordinary tragedy.

The emblem of the era is **the sealing of the Interred**: thirteen of the mightiest Gifted who ever lived, most of whom despised each other, joined hands exactly once — because the alternative was worse — to contain a psidolon they could not kill, then scattered again. Not an institution. A one-time armistice among rivals. *(See [The Interred](The%20Interred.md).)*

## I-b. Before the Domains — what psionics was without a system

It is worth stopping on a question the Domain taxonomy makes easy to forget: **the titans had no taxonomy.** Nobody in that age said *Memory Domain*. Nobody had heard of Intrusion, or Barriers, or bracing. Praxis had not been founded, would not be founded for an age, and the seven categories every trained Gifted now thinks in did not exist as thoughts anyone could have.

They did all of it anyway.

**Systematization is not the same as invention, and Praxis has spent five hundred years quietly enjoying the confusion.** What the scholars did was not discover that a mind could reach into another mind. Human beings had been doing that, badly and brilliantly and by accident, for as long as there had been tethers strong enough to stand on. What Praxis did was work out the *shape* underneath all that idiosyncratic practice, name it, and make it **teachable** — which is precisely what they did much later and much more visibly with [the Fugue](Combat%20and%20Conflict%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md), codifying a thing that was already real, already dangerous, and already happening, because a thing that cannot be taught dies with whoever worked it out.

Before that, psionics was a craft in the way any craft is before anyone writes the book: **by trial, by error, by figuring it out.** Some practitioners were superb. Most muddled. A few were geniuses whose methods died with them, unrecorded and unrepeatable, because there was no vocabulary in which to leave a note. Two masters in that age could have practised for forty years each and possessed no common word for a single thing either of them did.

**And some of them reached further than a modern Gifted ever will.** This is not romanticism about a lost golden age; it is what you should expect. A pre-taxonomic practitioner had no doctrine informing them that Control requires access first, that Kinesis is beneath a professional, that the art has seven parts and no more. They did not know which things were supposed to be impossible, and so a certain number of them did those things. The taxonomy that makes a modern Gifted competent also tells them where the walls are, and some of those walls were built by the surveyors.

**They also had no idea what any of it cost.** There was no Strain economy, no doctrine of consonance, no notion that forcing an unwilling mind is the specific act that eats you. There was only the work, and the sense — arriving far too late — that something was wrong.

**Which is the whole explanation for why the world was once full of psidolons.** It has never needed a stranger cause than this. An age of practitioners doing enormous, unrepeatable, poorly-understood things, with no model of the price, will produce untetherings at a rate no later century has approached. The titans were not merely powerful. They were **uninsured**, and the monsters that stalked that age were, very largely, *the previous generation of its heroes.* *(See [Psidolons — When the World Was Full of Them](Psidolons%20-%20The%20Untethered.md).)*

**So Praxis's real gift was never power. It was survival rate.** The Domain system trades ceiling for old age. It is a cage — [Heterodox](Project%20Heterodox.md) is entirely correct about that, and correct that the categories a Gifted thinks in are the categories a Gifted is limited to — and it is also the harness that stopped the species killing itself one virtuoso at a time. Both things are true, which is why the argument has never been settled and never will be.

It also means the MetaConcert's founding claim is not propaganda. Systematization genuinely saved them. Everything monstrous the institution later built stands on a foundation that was, at the beginning, entirely sound. That is not a defence of what came after. It is the reason what came after was possible.

*(One living case is often mistaken for this, and is not. [Eve](Eve.md) reaches without training, but she does not reach without **intent** the way the titans did — she does not try things, practise, or figure anything out. Her power surfaces in response to need. That is not the pre-taxonomic craftsman; that is the **Unshielded**, running on a tether that does not pay for it. See "What She Is.")*

> **A note for Storytellers: this is history, not a character option.** Pre-taxonomic practice belongs to the deep past and to the rare, isolated, never-Integrated figure a chronicle might encounter — the feral talent in a mountain village, a [Cult of the Unbound](The%20Cult%20of%20the%20Unbound.md) adept who rejects Praxis's categories on principle, something very old that has been practising alone since before there was anyone to tell it not to. Such a figure has **no Domain ratings** and is adjudicated entirely by the Storyteller: capable of one or two genuinely startling things, incapable of most ordinary ones, and possessing no reliable sense of what any of it is doing to them. They are not a build. They are a *weather event*, and the correct player response to meeting one is not to fight it but to work out what it wants.

## II. Two Convictions, No Names (still deep past — the organizing impulse begins)

What ended the age of titans was *organization*, and organization began as two convictions, not two institutions:

- **The hunters' conviction:** these things ran wild for want of anyone whose job it was to end them.
- **The scholars' conviction:** these things had never been properly understood.

The hunters became the first true order the Gifted ever forged — not a name yet, just *the ones who burned*. The scholars gathered in the great houses of learning of the old world, the **University of Sankoré at Timbuktu** chief among them — also not a name yet, just *the ones who kept*. Both went by informal, shifting designations for centuries. Neither was called what we call them now. *(See [Psidolons — When the World Was Full of Them](Psidolons%20-%20The%20Untethered.md).)*

## III. The Hunters Become "the Inquisition" (~1478–1530s)

Centuries into their existence, still unnamed, the order of hunters embedded itself within the Catholic Church's apparatus under **Tomás de Torquemada** — using the real Spanish Inquisition's institutional reach, cross-border authority, and legal cover to hunt psidolons and eliminate compromised Gifted under the pretext of heresy trials. Torquemada himself was a senior member of the order, not a churchman who merely shared their methods.

The infiltration ran so deep, and lasted so long, that when the operation finally ended, the order never took the mask off. It kept the name. To this day, the Inquisition wears the Spanish Inquisition the way a psidolon wears a host — and, like a psidolon that overstays, it never got its own name back. *(See [The Inquisition Circuit](The%20Inquisition%20Circuit.md), "The Spanish Inquisition.")*

## IV. The Scholars Become "Praxis" (~1550s — the coffeehouses of Constantinople)

The scholars' order waited far longer for its name — not at the hour history handed the hunters theirs, but generations afterward, and **not in Europe.** While the hunters were burying themselves in Catholic Spain, the scholars had simply gone where the learning was, as they always had: Sankoré, then Cairo, then Constantinople, following the houses of study the way water follows a channel. The two orders had not merely diverged in method. By this point they were not living in the same world, and had not been for a long time.

They took their name in the **coffeehouses of Constantinople**, which were new then and already notorious — rooms that had existed barely a generation, where men sat up all night arguing and the authorities kept trying to close the doors on the grounds that nothing good is decided after dark. It was the first institution in the world built for an argument to outlast the night. The order that had been having one argument for six hundred years walked in and recognized the house.

There they finally sat down to name the conviction they had always acted on: that understanding a system means intervening in it, that observation without intervention is incomplete data. They took the name **Praxis**. *(See [The Praxis Circuit](The%20Praxis%20Circuit.md), "Founding Context.")*

## V. The Undeclared War and the Concordat of Rhodes

Now named, the two orders were rivals before they were allies — "the ones who burned and the ones who kept," incompatible by instinct, overlapping by claim. Canon calls what followed exactly what it was: **a low and undeclared war**, with skirmishes neither side much discusses today. It ended not in friendship but in **the Concordat of Rhodes** — an armistice, two complementary survival operations with aligned interests and no formal unity. The Inquisition needed Praxis's doctrine; Praxis needed the Inquisition's field data.

**They met on Rhodes because there was nowhere in Christendom either order could stand without the other reading it as a claim.** The island was Ottoman by then, and had been since the Knights of St. John were starved off it — which made it the one ground in reach that belonged to neither of them and answered to neither of their host institutions. There is no small irony in the choice, and both orders saw it: they ended their war in the ruin of a military order that had held a fortress until holding it killed them. The Inquisition took the lesson as vindication. Praxis took it as data. Neither wrote the other's reading down.

The Concordat's price was transparency, and Praxis did not pay it honestly. What the Inquisition got was a curated disclosure; what Praxis kept was **the Black Archive** — the pre-truce record of what the Circuit did when it answered to no one, burned from paper and encoded instead into a handful of living minds. The Inquisition has never believed the "yes" it was given and has never been able to prove otherwise. *(See [The Praxis Circuit](The%20Praxis%20Circuit.md), "The Black Archive.")*

## VI. The Ivan Incident (1560s) — the Doctrine Shift

**This happens inside the bilateral period, and that is the whole reason it went the way it did.** There is no lattice yet, no Panopticon, no Maintainers — only two orders holding an armistice a few years old and calling it, optimistically, a council. What the sources call the *proto-MetaConcert* is exactly that: the Inquisition and Praxis in one room, not yet a government, already arguing.

The single event most responsible for the shape of *modern* Inquisition doctrine. Alexei Voronov, the finest Inquisitor of his generation, spent two decades on a slow, careful encirclement of the Old One — sound doctrine that had worked for generations. Cornered for the first time in possibly centuries, the Old One panicked and fled into Tsar Ivan IV, freshly destabilized by grief. It spent the next two decades using the Oprichnina to dismantle the Inquisition's Russian infrastructure and kill Voronov himself. Two generations of institutional capacity, destroyed — and the doctrine that patient encirclement denies a cornered psidolon nothing was proven false in the worst possible way. *(See [Psidolons — The Untethered](Psidolons%20-%20The%20Untethered.md), "The Old One.")*


**And Praxis argued the other way, and lost, and was right.** They held that Ivan proved nothing about patience — that the Old One fled because it was *cornered*, which is what encirclement is *for*, and that the lesson was about endgames rather than method. The Inquisition, having just buried two generations of its Russian capacity and the finest man it ever fielded, was in no condition to hear it. The fast-brutal doctrine that governs the Circuit to this day was adopted in grief, over the objection of the people who did the thinking, and the Old One — the specific creature it was designed to stop — is still out there. Praxis has never raised it again. It is not clear whether that is tact or filing.

## VII. The Lattice and the Birth of the MetaConcert as a Government

Praxis's greatest invention was the lattice. Having built it, Praxis refused to run it — maintenance was beneath their institutional attention, and the interesting intellectual work was already done. Someone had to be created to do the job Praxis wouldn't: **the Maintainers**, the third Circuit.

**This is the moment the bilateral model became obsolete — and not, yet, the moment anything was done about it.** Two allied operations did not need governance. The Concordat of Rhodes had divided the world in two, and two is a number that can be settled by negotiation between the parties. A third body broke that arithmetic. But the Maintainers were not a *peer*: they had been created by the founders, to run the founders' infrastructure, and an order that stands up a technical service does not thereby become a federation. It becomes two ancient institutions and a utility. The Concordat limped on for a generation, because institutions never reform at the moment they become obsolete. They reform when the improvised arrangement fails somewhere everyone can see it.

**What finished it was the Panopticon.** The fourth Circuit was the first that was nobody's subsidiary — it did work neither founder had ever done, it produced its own intelligence rather than servicing someone else's, and it therefore had interests of its own and the leverage to press them. At three bodies the Concordat was strained. At four, with two of them created after the pact that was supposed to govern everything, *"whichever of the Inquisition or Praxis gets to it"* had stopped being a division of labour and become a description of the confusion. **That is where the formal government begins**, and where the argument over what to call it happened. Praxis built the infrastructure that made formalization necessary, then watched the organization it had accidentally caused develop the authority to constrain it. *(See [The Maintainers Circuit](The%20Maintainers%20Circuit.md) and [The Panopticon Circuit](The%20Panopticon%20Circuit.md).)*

### Who named it

The thing needed a name, and the question of who supplied it is more revealing than it looks.

**It was not Praxis, and their reason was not oversight.** Asked to name the government their invention had just made necessary, Praxis declined to care. They would concede in the abstract that some governing structure was probably required; what it was *called* was implementation, and implementation is what one delegates. The order that refused to run the lattice refused, by the identical reflex, to name the thing that would run it. There is a Praxis minute somewhere, or there was before the Black Archive got to it, recording the second-greatest institution of the Gifted world responding to the naming of its own civilization with the sixteenth-century equivalent of *fine, whatever you like.*

**And then they were pressed, and they submitted something.** Every party was to enter a candidate; Praxis was informed that abstention was not available; and what Praxis entered was this:

> **The Standing Joint Apparatus for the Administration of Noetic Populations, the Containment of Untethered Phenomena, and the Maintenance of Lattice-Mediated Oversight, With Provision for Subsequent Revision**

It is not a joke, or it is not *only* a joke, and four hundred years later nobody can establish which. Praxis has never clarified. The reading their own scholars prefer is the sincere one — that the submission is exactly what it appears to be, a **definition mistaken for a name**, produced by an institution that does not distinguish the two and never has. Ask Praxis what a thing is called and they will tell you what it is, at whatever length accuracy demands, and consider the question answered. The trailing *With Provision for Subsequent Revision* is the tell that argues hardest for sincerity: no one being merely obstructive remembers to leave room for new data.

**It is also, and this is the uncomfortable part, the only honest candidate anybody entered.** *The Bulwark* was aspirational — it described a duty rather than an institution. *MetaConcert* was self-flattering, and remains so. Praxis's monstrosity is the only one of the three that says what the enterprise actually does: it **administers populations**, it **contains** what those populations turn into, and it **maintains oversight** of them. Every noun is accurate. That is very likely why it lost. A name with *the Administration of Noetic Populations* in it can be printed on a door, and then people have to walk past the door.

**And it is the earliest documented instance of the blindness Heterodox would organize itself around three centuries later.** Praxis believed — believes — that a name is a neutral description, that words are windows, that the only failure available to a term is inaccuracy. The proposition that naming is an *act*, that a word makes a claim and takes a side and does political work in the mouth of whoever says it, was not rejected by Praxis at that meeting. It never occurred to them. The whole counter-vocabulary that a defected Praxis researcher would one day forge — *the power, Triggered, technique* — exists because that assumption was wrong, and the first place it can be caught being wrong is a naming committee where Praxis turned in a paragraph.

**The pattern is the thing to notice.** They built the lattice and would not run it. They caused the government and would not name it. They invented the taxonomy that became a cage and consider the cage somebody else's discipline. **Praxis has never once wanted the thing it made** — and every time it has set one down, whoever picked it up got to decide what it was for.

**The Inquisition proposed something else, and lost.** Their candidate was **the Bulwark** — martial, plain, and entirely honest about what they understood the enterprise to be: a line held against psidolons, by people whose job was standing on it. It is a good name. It is arguably the more accurate one.

**The Panopticon was in the room and had no standing.** Newly created, junior to everyone, and not remotely positioned to propose a name for the whole enterprise. What it had instead was an *interest*, and it is worth being precise about what that interest was.

**Which leaves the Maintainers.** *MetaConcert* is their word, and it is a maintenance engineer's word rather than a philosopher's: a **concert** is many parts acting in coordination, which is precisely and literally what a lattice is, and **meta-** is the systems term for the layer above — the whole that the parts add up to and that has to be tended as its own object. It describes their work, and then extends outward until it describes everyone. That is what a technician's abstraction does.

**And it was a bid, which nobody minuted and everybody understood.** The Maintainers had spent a generation being the utility company: created to perform labour a prestigious order had declined as beneath it, indispensable, and not a party to the pact that governed anything. A *concert* has no servants in it. Every part of a concert is a voice. Proposing that word was an argument that the enterprise was **one organism rather than a treaty between two old orders** — and in a treaty the Maintainers are staff, while in an organism they are an organ.

**Which is exactly why the Panopticon backed them,** and why the Inquisition lost. Both post-Concordat Circuits had been created *into* an arrangement that predated them and had no room for them, and both stood to be permanently junior under any name that framed the thing as the Inquisition and Praxis plus help. The two newest bodies at the table wanted the same sentence to be true, and between them they carried it. The Inquisition was not out-argued. It was **outvoted, by institutions whose existence it had agreed to**, which is how that usually goes.

**And this is the part worth stopping on.** The Inquisition wanted a name that pointed *outward*, at the monsters. The Maintainers wanted one that pointed *inward*, at the organization. The organization chose to be named after itself — and that, rather than any charter or vote, is the moment it stopped being a coalition against psidolons and started being a government. Everything the MetaConcert later became is prefigured in a naming argument that the pragmatists won and nobody thought was important.

**The irony is not lost on anyone who notices it, which is nearly nobody.** The most benign word in the entire vocabulary of the Gifted — a *harmony*, an ensemble, many voices in deliberate accord — was coined by the Circuit that installs binding constructs in the minds of children, and it was coined **sincerely.** The Maintainers meant it. In the year it was proposed it was even true. The name is a fossil of the season in which this was still four institutions negotiating the terms on which they would cooperate, and it has been sitting on the front of an authoritarian state ever since, because renaming it would require somebody to say out loud what it turned into.

## VIII. The Remaining Four Circuits — Filling the Gaps the First Three Left

Each of the last four Circuits exists because the first three were handling something badly, part-time, or not at all:

- **The Panopticon (fourth).** Once the lattice existed, someone needed dedicated, sustained visibility into it — Inquisitors and Praxis analysts taking shifts had proven inadequate almost immediately. Before the Panopticon, "detection" meant rumor, town criers, and dispatching the Inquisition on hearsay.
- **The Integrators (fifth).** Before them, newly Triggered children were handled by whichever of the Inquisition or Praxis happened to notice them first — about as well as either institution's temperament would suggest.
- **The Preservers (sixth).** By this point the MetaConcert had five Circuits, a lattice, and formal governance, and no unified institutional memory — each Circuit had been keeping its own records its own way. The Preservers were built to solve continuity. They inherited an archive that was already missing its worst chapter — the hole Praxis cut before the Preservers existed to notice it was missing.
- **The Operatives (seventh, last).** External relations are a luxury of stability. By the time the MetaConcert had the confidence to formalize a diplomatic corps, it had been managing the Camarilla, the Technocracy, and the Garou through ad hoc personal relationships for generations — one personality clash from collapse.
