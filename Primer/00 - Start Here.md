# Start Here

*What this collection is, what it deliberately leaves out, and the order to read it in.*

---

## What this is

**Psion: The Fraying** is a fan-made splatbook for the Classic World of Darkness — the setting of *Vampire: The Masquerade*, *Werewolf: The Apocalypse*, *Mage: The Ascension*, *Wraith: The Oblivion* and *Changeling: The Dreaming*. It adds one more kind of person to that world: **the Gifted**, who can reach into reality with their minds, and who damage themselves permanently every time they do it.

This folder is not the book. It is a **primer**: the minimum set of documents needed to understand the world, plus a general outline of how it plays. It exists so that an assistant given these files and nothing else can discuss the setting, brainstorm in it, and write in its voice without inventing things that contradict it.

## What is deliberately not here

**All of the crunch.** No dice, no dice pools, no difficulties, no numeric costs, no character creation, no combat rules, no stat blocks. The full game uses the Classic World of Darkness Storyteller System (ten-siders, difficulty 6, count successes) with three additions — the seven **Domains** in place of Disciplines, a **Tether** trait, and a **Tether Track** where the horror lives. None of that is reproduced here, because none of it is needed to understand the world, and it would crowd out what is.

Where a rule matters to comprehension, it appears as a fact about reality rather than a mechanic. *Using the power damages you. The damage accumulates. The deepest of it never heals. Nothing repairs a tether — no drug, no therapy, no ritual, no expenditure of will. Rest works. Time works. Nothing else does.* That is the truth of the setting. The dice are elsewhere.

## Read in this order

**The frame** — read these first, in order. They are short and everything else assumes them.
- **01 - The Premise** — what the game is about and why it is not Mage.
- **02 - Terminology and Register** — the two-vocabulary system. *Read this before anything else.* Almost every important thing has an institutional name and a human one, and which one a character uses tells you who they are. Getting this wrong makes the rest read as a different game.
- **18 - Voice and Tone** — who is narrating, and how to write in the world without breaking it.

**The world** — the metaphysics, then the institutions, then the monsters.
- **03 - The Tether** — the organ everything hangs on: what it is, what it's made of, how it breaks.
- **04 - Psionics and the Seven Domains** — what the power actually does.
- **05 - The MetaConcert** — the institution that rules the Gifted, and is not optional.
- **06 - The Seven Circuits** — its organs. A Gifted's Circuit is the Psion equivalent of a clan or a tribe.
- **07 - The Projects** — the resistance, each paired against the Circuit it opposes.
- **08 - Psidolons** — the central monster, and the justification for everything.
- **09 - The Cult of the Unbound** — the people who tell you the monster is transcendence.
- **15 - A Short History** — how old this is, and in what order it grew.

**The neighbours** — Psion in the shared universe.
- **10 - The Wider World of Darkness** — Kindred, Garou, mages, wraiths.
- **11 - The Interred** — the first psidolon that ever survived, and where vampiric Dominate came from.
- **14 - The Dreambound** — the changeling-Gifted, and the one real cure nobody can reach.

**The people**
- **12 - Eve** — the anomaly. Read *The Tether* first or she will not land.
- **13 - Marcus Thiel** — the exemplar: unaffiliated, off-lattice, squishy, effective.

**Playing it**
- **16 - Baltimore** — the sample city.
- **17 - How It Plays** — chronicle shapes and the texture of a scene. Still no dice.
- **19 - Secrets and GM Truths** — the omniscient voice. What the setting knows that the characters don't.

## The four sentences that carry the most

If you retain nothing else:

1. **The Gifted are not special because they have something others lack.** They are people whose tether happens to be resilient enough to survive a Trigger. Everyone has a tether. Theirs is made of different stuff.

2. **The villains are right about the facts.** Fraying is real, psidolons are real, the threat is not manufactured. They are wrong about what follows. The resistance knows this, which is why the resistance is tragic and not triumphant.

3. **Cost and exposure are two different axes** — *dirty/clean* is who noticed; *consonant/dissonant* is what you forced. Paradox bites when they notice; the tether bites when you force. Never collapse them.

4. **The real weapon is the operation, not the blow.** A Gifted's power fantasy is not winning the fight — it is never being in one. If you are in combat, the plan already failed.

## A note on the vocabulary you will see

The book's terminology is **inconsistent on purpose**, and the inconsistency is characterization. Its author is a Praxis defector writing for Heterodox; they refuse the institution's euphemisms and keep its science, because there are no other words for the science. When *the power* appears, that is a refusal. When *Noesis* appears, that is the refusal failing, and the author lets you watch. See **02** and **18**.
