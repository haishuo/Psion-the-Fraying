# The Projects

*The seven resistance movements outside MetaConcert authority — what each does, which Circuit it mirrors, and what it costs to belong.*

---

## What the Projects Are

The Projects are not an organization. They are a loose coalition united by one principle — rejection of the lattice and everything it represents — and divided about everything else. Some rescue, some philosophize, some wage open war, some work from inside. All understand that their existence validates the MetaConcert's paranoia, and all accept that cost.

Each Project is paired against a Circuit as its structural opposite — noted in the headings below. Not by design, but by inevitability: an institution's functions create the space for the movement that inverts them, and defectors are the only people who know a house well enough to fight it.

There is an older shape underneath. The MetaConcert was founded by two Circuits — the Inquisition and Praxis, the one that kills and the one that knows — and every Circuit since inherited one of those two temperaments. The resistance inherited the same pair, because its founders were runaways. **Rebel is the Inquisition's child:** ex-Inquisitors who broke, and who never learned another verb. **Fractal is Praxis's child:** researchers who fled while they still had a self to run with, and who, having helped build the model, began to wonder whether the whole edifice was wrong from the first page. Every later Project sorts toward the soldier or the scholar.

Here is what makes the resistance tragic rather than triumphant, and it must not be softened. **The Projects cannot argue that fraying isn't real.** It is real. Unmonitored Gifted do fray, do snap, and do become psidolons that kill people. Without the lattice the Projects cannot detect when one of their own reaches critical fraying — and when the MetaConcert points at the bodies, it is not lying. What the Projects can argue is that the response is disproportionate: that the cure is worse than the disease. The argument is defensible. It is not comforting.

Victory is also foreclosed. The lattice is a mutual-assured-destruction device; destroying MetaConcert infrastructure risks the release of the MAD trigger network — already installed, already waiting — which shatters the Masquerade and kills everyone. The MetaConcert cannot be reformed from outside, because it is authoritarian, and cannot be ignored, because it hunts. Resistance is necessary. Victory is impossible. Surrender means death. They resist anyway, because what else is there.

Both sides have blood on their hands. Both believe they are saving lives. Both might be right.

## Project Railroad (↔ Integrators)

*"We save who we can, when we can, because someone has to."*

Railroad intercepts newly Triggered children before the Integrators reach them. Their mission is not rescue but prevention, and it is a footrace measured in minutes: the Panopticon detects a signature, Integrators are dispatched, a Parasyte source leaks the address and the ETA, and whoever arrives first decides that child's entire future. Integration is permanent. There is no second attempt. They are former Integrators who couldn't stomach the system and Gifted who lost someone to a White Room, in decentralized cells under coordinators called Conductors. They see themselves as medics, not warriors — exhausted, overworked, constantly grieving.

Their primary ally is the Children of Gaia, and the alliance is spiritual rather than tactical. To a CoG mystic the lattice is the Weaver's web made literal: minds bound into permanent pattern, individual chaos suppressed, perfect order without balance. Sheltering Railroad's refugees is not politics but a correction to the Triat. They provide sanctuary on tribal land, wards against Detection, training through meditation rather than obedience, and monitoring for fraying that is less precise than the Panopticon's and vastly more respectful. The MetaConcert despises the arrangement and will not attack it, because war with the Garou Nation would be catastrophic.

The cost: everything Railroad does, it does blind. Children they free sometimes snap; it has happened inside a caern, and people died. The elders reaffirmed the policy anyway — *"The Weaver would have us choose safety over mercy. We choose mercy."* And Railroad tells a family their daughter is about to be taken, gets twenty minutes to convince them, and leaves the parents believing they were robbed. *"So that girl is free, and her parents think we kidnapped her. Both things are true."*

## Project Rebel (↔ Inquisition)

*"They will not reform. They must be fought."*

Rebel is the blunt instrument. They storm White Rooms to grab Gifted mid-Correction, ambush Integration transports, attack Maintainer hubs and Panopticon stations, assassinate Inquisitors, raid Preserver archives and leak what they find. Rebel kicks in the door — and then hands the liberated person to Railroad, because Rebel does not teach bracing and does not run safe houses. They create the violent opening. Someone else turns it into a life. Former Inquisitors who broke, Gifted who lost everyone, Kinesis specialists with nothing left to lose, in cells under Commanders. Their allies are the Virtual Adepts, who understand asymmetric war and supply the encryption that keeps Rebel off the lattice.

Their problem is arithmetic and it does not improve. Every attack triggers Inquisition response. Every assassinated Councilor is replaced by a hardliner. Every act of violence is cited as proof that dissent enables terrorism, which justifies the next tightening, which radicalizes the next recruit. The Chicago Lattice Disruption of 2021 destabilized a regional lattice; hundreds suffered agonizing disconnection, several died of severance trauma, the MetaConcert rebuilt stronger, and Rebel split over whether the dead counted as acceptable. Some cells have gone past the line — torturing captured Inquisitors, attacking Integrators — and been disavowed, and continue anyway.

The cost of belonging is the contradiction you carry: Rebel rejects the MetaConcert's utilitarian murder while committing its own, and knows it. *"I've killed seventeen Inquisitors. The MetaConcert has executed thousands of us. The math is clear: we're losing. But we're making them bleed."* Nobody can answer whether Rebel makes things better or worse. They fight anyway.

## Project Parasyte (↔ Operatives)

*"We change the system from within."*

Parasyte operatives pass as loyal MetaConcert members. They are the most dangerous Project because nobody knows who they are — including, sometimes, themselves. They solved the surveillance problem by not hiding.

The **identity overlay** suppresses the operative's true self below conscious access and runs a fabricated identity in the active layer: beliefs, loyalties, emotional responses, all genuine. The overlay does not pretend to be a loyal Inquisitor. It *is* one. Surface thoughts read clean because they are clean. It is built on the operative's own history, changing only what must change — lies are better with truth in them. The irony is exquisite: Praxis derived the technique from psidolon occupation mechanics and gave it to the Operatives for infiltrating outsiders. Some went native and brought it out, and the Panopticon cannot reliably detect overlays because its systems were built to accommodate them. A handler holds the **restoration trigger**, a psionic signature transmitted from outside, so the overlay genuinely does not know it exists and interrogation cannot find it. If the handler dies, the operative is trapped in the cover identity forever, doing the MetaConcert's work with sincere conviction, their sacrifice never known. This has happened. There is no way to count how often.

The cost is slow and total. The overlay executes flagged Gifted because it believes in the doctrine; the original self must live with what their other self did in earnest. Worse: a consciousness suppressed beneath another for twenty years erodes — not into incoherence, but measurably. Edges softened. Convictions that felt absolute now negotiable. Handlers are trained to recommend extraction past acceptable parameters; in practice the parameters remain acceptable for longer than they should. And some operatives simply look at the overlay's life and decide it was the better one.

*"I integrate children into slavery so that other children might stay free. The math has to work. Please, let the math work."*

## Project Fractal (↔ Preservers)

*"The lattice is slavery. Freedom means accepting risk."*

Fractal argues that the MetaConcert's framework is illegitimate — not just its methods but its foundational assumptions. Mental sovereignty is absolute. Surveillance cannot coexist with freedom. Some Gifted will fray, and that is tragic, and it is not a justification for tyranny. Their radical position: even if the utilitarian math is correct, even if preemptive execution saves more lives, it is still wrong.

But it is a mistake to define Fractal by its opposition. Strip the politics away and what remains is **curiosity unconstrained by utility** — people who hold that a question worth asking does not have to lead anywhere, only to be interesting. This makes them the mirror of Praxis, their own parent — which raises the obvious question of why the ledger sets them against the **Preservers** instead. The answer is that the Projects are paired by *what they oppose*, not by *who bore them*, and Fractal is the only one where those differ. Heterodox is Praxis's other child and stayed home to argue with its parent. Fractal walked out and kept walking, and when it stopped it had found a different enemy: the Circuit that decides what happened. A lie in a Praxis file ruins one subject. A lie in a Preserver archive ruins everyone downstream of it, forever, including the people who would otherwise have known to be angry. Fractal ran from the laboratory and turned around to face the record. It is the only Project that chose its enemy rather than inheriting it. The Preservers, for their part, do not consider Fractal an opponent at all. They consider it a filing error.

Praxis asks *what happens if we do this;* Fractal asks *what happens if we think about this.* The same hunger; the only difference is method. Praxis builds a laboratory. Fractal books an auditorium. Pursued to its end, Praxis's curiosity takes a captive mind apart by percentages and records the number where the person stopped being one. Fractal's costs an afternoon and some folding chairs. One produces wights. The other produces a standing ovation and no action items.

The briefings write the joke themselves. Railroad: *we extracted seventeen people.* Rebel: *we sabotaged a Panopticon relay.* Fractal: *we held a four-hour symposium on whether silence can be lonely.* Nobody cuts them loose anyway, because without one corner of the movement permitted to spend three hours on whether the color blue has feelings, the resistance slowly begins to think exactly like the MetaConcert — efficient, instrumental, contemptuous of any question that doesn't pay — only with different goals. Fractal is the antibody against the resistance becoming its enemy by degrees.

The cost: they live entirely outside monitoring, so no one sees the snap coming, and Fractal unravels at a higher rate than anyone. They accept it. Everyone else notes that this is easy to say when it wasn't your loved one the psidolon killed.

## Project Iconoclast (↔ Maintainers)

*"They built it. We can take it apart."*

Rebel breaks things. Iconoclast takes them apart. Where Rebel's attacks produce temporary disruption and permanent escalation, Iconoclast's work produces permanent liberation one person at a time — because their core function is **removing people from the lattice safely.**

The Maintainers know clean severance; they perform it routinely in White Rooms as part of termination. They sever people and process them; they do not sever people and release them. The technique exists. The institutional will to use it for liberation does not. So a Gifted who wants out has three options: find an Iconoclast; do it alone, which frequently leaves cognitive damage, because ripping out a binding construct tears something important loose; or stay connected behind Barriers and live in permanent fear. Most successful apostates found an Iconoclast. Marcus Thiel did it himself. He survived. Not everyone does.

They are defected Maintainers and self-taught engineers, patient and obsessive, organized as research labs rather than cells — fewer members than any other Project, because the skill floor is brutal. Binding architecture is not standardized; each Maintainer's installation varies, so every severance is a custom operation. Their allies are the Glass Walkers, to whom every dissolved binding is a strand of the Pattern Web literally unwoven.

The cost is written in their own files. They have killed people — miscalculated integration depth, cascades the operator couldn't contain, survivors who came out damaged. They document failures with the same precision as successes; the documentation is harrowing and it is the basis of every improvement. The Maintainers consider Iconoclast the only Project that is an existential threat — not because they can destroy the lattice, but because they can make it *optional.* Destruction can be rebuilt from. Obsolescence cannot.

## Project Lacuna (↔ Panopticon)

*"If they can't see you, they can't control you."*

Lacuna is the Project the Panopticon was built to prevent, and the proof that prevention is incomplete. They don't rescue, philosophize, fight, or infiltrate. They create the conditions under which all of that becomes possible: the infrastructure of resistance, not the resistance itself.

Their insight is that the lattice is a constructed system, and constructed systems have tolerances. The Panopticon compensates with redundancy and algorithms, but compensation is not elimination. **Dead zones** are the product: areas where lattice monitoring degrades to functional blindness, sustained by an operative actively holding an interference pattern. Not shielding — jamming. Barriers protect a mind; a dead zone protects a space, so a Gifted inside doesn't have to hold their own Thoughtshield at maximum to disappear. They close when the operative stops. They are the coalition's operational backbone: Railroad briefs Conductors in one, Heterodox meets in one, Rebel plans in one, Iconoclast performs severance in one because the procedure is psionically loud. Lacuna provides the space and withdraws — deliberately never learning what happens inside, so a captured operative can truthfully say they don't know.

Former Panopticon analysts who know the algorithms from inside; Barriers specialists working past standard technique; people with a talent for negative space. No central leadership, because a counter-surveillance organization cannot afford anyone who knows its full scope. Their allies are the Nosferatu, for whom being unseen is not a tactic but the condition of existence.

The cost is invisibility all the way down. When Lacuna works, nothing happens and nobody notices. A dead zone is detectable from outside as an anomalous silence, so they must be small, brief, and sited where coverage is already thin. Lacuna adapts; the Panopticon adapts; neither gains lasting advantage; both know it; both continue. *"We find the places where completeness fails… It's not much space. But it's enough."*

## Project Heterodox (↔ Praxis)

*"What if they're not wrong about everything — just wrong about the map?"*

Heterodox is the Project Praxis takes personally.

Praxis built the Domain taxonomy — the seven-category framework every Gifted uses to understand their own mind. Their map became the territory. Heterodox asks whether the map is wrong. Not fabricated: the taxonomy describes real phenomena. But a framework is not the thing it describes. It carries its builders' assumptions — which Gifted they had access to, which edge cases they forced into a box rather than allowing to suggest the system was incomplete.

**The claim is not that the taxonomy is wrong. It is that the taxonomy is *limiting.*** A Gifted trained in the Domain system develops *within* it — learns what it teaches, grows along the paths it provides, conceives of their potential in the terms it defines. Teach a child to name her own mind in Praxis's Greek and you have decided what she can afterward think with it. Gifted who develop outside the system sometimes develop differently. Not better. Differently, in ways the taxonomy can't account for. The framework does not merely describe the power; it shapes it — into whatever serves institutional need. This is why the vernacular says **"the power"** rather than "the Noesis." Most Project members were raised in the lattice; they know the word perfectly well. Declining to use it is not ignorance. It is Heterodox's argument made in two syllables.

Their people: ex-Praxis researchers told to stop asking; self-taught Gifted whose experience never matched the taxonomy; defected Preserver archivists who broke over the editing and who *could not walk out empty-handed if they tried* — a Preserver carries the unedited originals in their own memory, the living receipts behind their own eyes. Research collectives rather than cells, organized around questions rather than objectives; more academic conference than resistance movement. Their primary ally is the Order of Hermes, whose Mind Sphere theory and Praxis's Domain theory describe overlapping phenomena through incompatible lenses. The gaps are where the work happens.

The cost of belonging is that the work may be structurally impossible, and the best of them know it. *"The categories they are trying to transcend are the categories they think with… you cannot draw a new map without using the cartographic tools the old map gave you."* So Heterodox's contribution may not be an alternative framework at all. **It may be the documented proof that the existing one is incomplete** — valuable even if the replacement never arrives. Praxis finds that possibility more disturbing than a rival theory. An alternative theory can be evaluated, tested, refuted. A documented gap that nobody can fill is permanent institutional uncertainty.

*"We're collecting the anomalies. We're mapping the gaps. And the gaps are not small."*
