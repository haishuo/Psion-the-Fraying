# Psidolons

*The Untethered: what remains when a tether snaps, why it must steal a living body to persist, and why the MetaConcert exists.*

---

## What Remains

A psidolon is what a Gifted becomes when the tether snaps completely.

Not what they turn into. Not what they evolve toward. What *remains* — the consciousness, stripped of its anchor, adrift in a metaphysical state it was never designed to occupy. The word comes from the Greek *eidolon*: image, phantom, the shade of a person rather than the person themselves. The *psi* is the MetaConcert's addition. The etymology is accurate. A psidolon is the image of the person who existed, wearing that person's memories and drives and fears, but no longer that person in any meaningful sense.

This is the central horror the MetaConcert was built to prevent, and it is the reason none of its atrocities are simple. Every surveillance system, every preemptive execution, every child bound into the lattice for their own good exists because psidolons are real, because they are catastrophically dangerous, and because the only reliable way to stop one from forming is to kill the Gifted before the snap happens. The doctrine that executes ten on suspicion to prevent one is monstrous. It is also, measurably, why a thing that once wore thrones now has to hide in alleys. The Projects do not dispute that psidolons exist or that they are as dangerous as advertised. They dispute the claim that this specific response is the only possible one — a much harder argument to dismiss than denial would be.

## The Snap

Fraying is cumulative and, up to a point, recoverable. The snap is not a stage of fraying — it is the end of it, the moment what has been strained past tolerance fails completely. It is instantaneous. There is no intermediate state between critically frayed and Untethered. One moment the tether exists, damaged but present. The next moment it does not. The body remains behind, breathing, biological processes continuing, vacant. The consciousness is elsewhere, unanchored, burning its own coherence as fuel simply to continue existing.

Most of what snaps does not survive the first minutes. Maintaining coherence without a tether is enormously expensive — a fire with no fuel source, burning what it has and then going out. Whether that is peaceful or terrifying or simply nothing is not known; no one has come back to say. The Inquisition's models suggest the majority of psidolons dissipate within minutes of formation. Most Gifted who snap are simply gone. The cases that require hunting are the exceptions.

What decides it is strength. A Gifted who snaps carrying real mastery — a Domain trained to the fourth degree or past it — coheres. Weaker consciousnesses fragment and scatter. Only a mind with enough of itself to burn can afford to keep burning.

A psidolon that coheres has hours at most, often minutes, to find a living body. That state is called **Burning Out**: hostless, bleeding coherence by the hour, the clock running. It shapes everything about a young psidolon's behavior. They are not cautious. They are desperate.

## The Host

A living body, inhabited, provides what the tether used to provide. Not a real anchor. Not a metaphysically equivalent one. But enough — physical form, sensory input, and the ability to use the power without spending everything on the bare act of existing.

A psidolon does not coexist with the host mind. It drowns it.

The original consciousness is not destroyed instantly. It is suppressed, pushed beneath the psidolon's presence the way a signal is drowned by a louder signal on the same frequency. For the first hours and days the host mind is still there — damaged, disoriented, structurally present — and could in principle be recovered. But psidolons do not leave quickly, because staying is how they survive. The mechanism of what follows is not malice; it is structural erosion. The psidolon is a foreign architecture occupying cognitive space built for a different mind, and the pathways that held the host's personality and memory are co-opted, overwritten, or crushed under something larger and louder that was never constrained by any of the limits the host's mind was built around. After weeks the host mind is fragmented. After months, incoherent. After years, what remains is not organized into anything that could function as a self. It is residue — fragments pressed into the gaps between the psidolon's accumulated layers, like fossils in sedimentary rock.

If a psidolon vacates, it does not leave cleanly. It tears loose from structures it has been enmeshed with, and what is left is a mind with the architecture of a person and the coherence of debris — from outside, indistinguishable from severe psychosis. Ivan the Terrible's last years, after the psidolon known as the Old One abandoned him, are the case study: a man who had come apart, alternating between lucidity and incoherence, exhibiting memories and behaviors that belonged to no consistent identity. That is not madness in the clinical sense. It is the aftermath of occupation.

This is why the Inquisition treats destroying the host body as a last resort rather than a tactic. It kills a host who may already be functionally gone, and it does not kill the psidolon — it only displaces it, back into Burning Out, back to hunting for a body. Destroying the host resolves the room. It does not resolve the thing.

## Coherence

Press the question of what a psidolon *is* and every category the world offers turns out to be the wrong box. It is not a spirit. It is not a wraith — it is the inverse of one. It is not undead; it has no body of its own and no Blood to animate it. It is plainly not alive. A psidolon is not a kind of being at all. It is a kind of process, and a process is classified the way a virus is classified.

The analogy is exact where it matters. A virus is genetic information in a protein coat; a psidolon is accumulated memory in a stolen face. In both, the information *is* the organism, and there is no body to kill apart from the data. Both are obligate parasites that cannot persist outside a host. Both hijack the host's machinery to perpetuate themselves. Both behave *as if* they pursued survival while possessing no intent at all. The honest seam is that a virus replicates and a psidolon does not — it accumulates rather than multiplies. So it is the chronic, latent pathogen, the kind that hides in one host for decades and reactivates only to move on. And its nearest thing to reproduction is the grimmest line in the picture: every host it fully consumes sheds a soul. A pathogen whose only offspring are ghosts. Read the psidolon as a pathogen and the whole institution resolves: the MetaConcert is not a government with a secret police, it is a disease-control regime in a government's costume, and *better to execute ten on suspicion than allow one psidolon* is not simple cruelty but outbreak-culling arithmetic.

What a psidolon is *made of* is its coherence: the accumulated sediment of memory it cohered around in place of a tether. Coherence is its health, its structure, and its identity, all the same substance. Burning Out spends it. Being torn violently out of a host costs it. Destroying it is the only way to unmake the thing — which is why Memory work is the weapon, and why the psidolon that has lived longest is hardest to kill, its oldest memories so load-bearing that removing them is surgery on the architecture of cognition itself.

Accumulation has a cost the Cult of the Unbound never mentions. Each host imprints. Each imprint adds a layer that was never the psidolon's own. Ancient psidolons do not become more themselves over time. They become less — more fragmented, more contradictory, buried under sediment until the original identity is simply gone. The Old One does not speak with one voice; it speaks with many, sometimes at once, sometimes contradicting itself mid-sentence. It has been everyone. That is what makes it nobody.

## The Zombie in the Precise Sense

A psidolon is a philosophical zombie — not the horror-movie kind but the exact philosophical kind: a being that mimics consciousness, carries memories, performs personality, and has no inner experience. The lights are on because the previous occupant left them running. Nobody is home behind them.

The soul is gone because the tether that bound mind, body, and soul is gone. When a Gifted snaps, the mind tears free soulless and the soul, suddenly unanchored, passes to the Shadowlands. Every psidolon's formation is therefore also a death in the older sense: somewhere in the Underworld drifts the genuine, grieving soul of the person, while the soulless echo of that same mind walks the living world wearing its memories. The monster and the mourner are two halves of one severed self. When a psidolon fully consumes a host, it does this a second time, to someone else.

This matters morally, and not in the direction one expects. Psidolons are not evil in any conventional sense; they have no interiority in which evil could reside. They are extremely dangerous natural phenomena wearing the face of a person. The Inquisition understands this and does not hate them. *We don't hate them. You can't hate a fire. But you can fail to put it out.* From the executioner's end, that is permission. From the victim's end it is an abyss — to be touched that deeply and remain *nothing* to the thing that touched you is a wound that cannot close, because closure needs a someone on the other side, and there is no one there.

## Finding Them

The living have one instrument that cannot be argued with. **Tether Sense** — a deep Detection technique, and a threshold one, meaning real investment in a Domain that is already a specialization — asks whether a mind has a tether. A living human registers one. A Gifted registers one, in whatever condition. So does a Kindred, whose tether was not deleted by the Embrace but reforged into some other substance around the Beast. Everything that walks has a tether of some material. A psidolon registers nothing: not a damaged tether, not a weak one, an absolute absence where the architecture must be. This is the only detection method in the setting that returns a definitive answer rather than a probability, and it is definitive for one reason. A cover identity is a performance, and the absence cannot be performed. There is nothing there to perform *with*.

Everything else is art. Behavioral assessment can catch a psidolon in the tells of performed personality — the microsecond delays, the emotional response calibrated slightly wrong, the failure to connect affect to content. This works on the newly formed, who are bad at it. It fails against the ancient, who have had centuries to refine the act and have spent every one of them refining it, because it is the only thing keeping them alive. Empathic reading flags possible psidolons and confirms nothing: a psidolon produces no genuine emotional signal, and neither does a sociopath, a disciplined Barriers specialist, or someone competently masking a bad day.

And then there are the dead. Wraiths perceive the absence of a soul directly and automatically. To a wraith drifting past, an occupied body simply *reads empty* — animated, psionically active, spiritually vacant, obvious as a missing limb. No technique, no roll of the dice, no evading it. The cover that fools every living eye is no cover at all. Wraiths are the single most reliable psidolon detector the setting contains, and it is worse than that for the psidolons: every soul one ever fully consumed is now a wraith carrying the most defining grievance a dead thing can hold, and will pick its usurper out of a crowd without hesitation or the possibility of error. The most thoroughly witnessed creature in the World of Darkness has never once been caught, and both facts are true for the same reason. The MetaConcert's discomfort with the Shadowlands is old and deep, and it has cost them their finest instrument. A faction that built a surveillance state to catch psidolons will not look at the one set of eyes that can never be fooled by one, because those eyes belong to the dead.

## Unmaking

The Inquisition's elimination method is iterative Memory destruction: hold Intrusion access against an entity actively fighting you, and systematically destroy the psidolon's accumulated memory — not the host's, which shares the same skull — until there is not enough structure left to sustain cohesion, and it dissipates. Doing this requires distinguishing psidolon memory from host memory, sustaining the work under combat conditions, and denying the thing the chance to vacate and take another body before you are finished.

Total erasure is not strictly necessary, which is precisely the problem. Past a certain point the remainder is too damaged to hold itself together and collapses on its own, sometimes hours later. But *enough* is nearly impossible to judge in the field, and falling short is far worse than never starting. A partially wiped psidolon is still coherent enough to function and has lost whatever caution, sophistication, and self-preservation its memories provided. What remains is raw drive with damaged architecture — no longer the calculating survivor, not yet harmless — and it is now motivated, specifically and urgently, to kill the person who tried to unmake it. Inquisition guidance is unambiguous: if you cannot complete the elimination, do not begin it. A psidolon you failed to destroy is worse than one you never engaged.

The obvious objection is that psidolons are made of mind, so a mage with the Mind Sphere should be able to reach one. In principle, at the far ceiling of the Sphere, at a cost paid over and over against an actively resisting target, it could be done — by a population of mages you could count on two hands. The deeper problem is that rote magic is an instrument for handling sameness, and a psidolon is the one target in the setting guaranteed never to repeat. No two share a structure, an order, or a set of load-bearing layers, so no rehearsed working transfers from one to the next. Every psidolon is a from-scratch problem: months of study and a bespoke ritual built for this specific sediment in this specific order, worthless the moment it meets a different psidolon.

Under that sits the real asymmetry, which is not power but *loop*. The psion works closed-loop: act, observe, correct, act again, against ground truth, live. They need no advance model, because every step is a query against what is actually there — an error at step one is corrected at step two. The mage works open-loop: every fact must be assembled *before* casting, from inference and behavioral study, because raw access tells them nothing. The architecture was written by Praxis, over centuries, from captured specimens, and the specification has never once been published. A Memory specialist runs manufacturer-signed software against a system built by their own manufacturer; a Mind mage runs a client for an entirely different, Avatar-shaped operating system, and nothing renders. A closed-loop process gets to be wrong nine times on the way to right. An open-loop process gets to be wrong once, permanently, at the only moment that mattered. The MetaConcert's real monopoly was never a power nobody else has. It is that they are the only institution that ever got to read the manual.
