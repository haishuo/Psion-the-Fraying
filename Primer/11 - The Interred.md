# The Interred

*The first psidolon that ever survived the snap, the prison built for it, and the wage the Kindred were paid to guard the door.*

---

There is an older psidolon than the Old One. It does not walk. It has not walked in longer than any institution has kept records. It is held — has been held, without interruption, since before the Gifted had a word for what they were — in a prison that cannot be built again, by jailers who are not Gifted, kept alive by the very thing that should have made its imprisonment impossible.

The MetaConcert, in the few deep-archive fragments that mention it at all, calls it **the Interred**. The handful of Preservers who have read those fragments call it **the First**. Its keepers call it **the Tenant** — a word chosen for its coldness, because the alternatives are unbearable.

It is not a god. It is not a demon. It is not eldritch, whatever that word is taken to mean. It is a psidolon: the same clinical horror as every other psidolon, the same soulless accumulation of memory wearing the shape of a mind, and exactly as un-mythological as that makes it. What sets it apart is not category. It is age, and degree, and the single fact that it was caught.

*Almost nothing in this account is known to any Gifted alive. It is set down here anyway.*

## The First Snap

The story the Gifted tell themselves is that fraying was discovered the way most things are discovered — gradually, through accumulated observation, systematized eventually by Praxis into the clinical framework everyone now uses. That account is true. It is also incomplete, because it begins too late.

The Interred is, as far as anyone can reconstruct, the first psidolon ever to survive the snap: the first tether to break under a mind strong enough that what remained did not simply dissipate. It happened in deep antiquity, thousands of years before there was an Inquisition to hunt such things or a Praxis to name them. No doctrine, no surveillance, no Tether Sense, no White Room. Only a person, immensely gifted by the standards of any era, who pushed too far and did not die. What it was before the snap is gone — more completely gone than even the Old One's origin, because the Interred is older and has had longer to bury itself under everything it has been. No name survives. The First is, in the most literal sense, nobody.

It is not too much to say that the Interred is why the entire horror has a shape. Before the First, the snap was simply death. After the First, the Gifted knew what death could become. Every surveillance protocol, every preemptive execution, every child bound into the lattice for their own safety is downstream — across millennia and through a hundred intermediate institutions — of the day the Gifted first learned that the tether could break into something that did not stop.

## The Thirteen and the Three Walls

It could not be allowed to continue. On that the Gifted of that era agreed, though they agreed on almost nothing else and had no institution to make agreement mean anything. What they assembled instead was a gathering of **thirteen** — thirteen of the most powerful Gifted of their age, and quite possibly the most powerful who have ever lived. Rivals, most of whom despised one another, who joined hands exactly once because the alternative was worse, and then scattered again. The number is remembered when almost nothing else is, because it was deliberate, though the reason is lost. Later Gifted have noted, uneasily, how it rhymes with the thirteen great lines of the Kindred.

They hunted the First. They cornered it. And then they discovered the thing the Inquisition would not formalize for thousands of years: you cannot kill what you cannot unmake. A psidolon dies when its memory is destroyed, and the First was already so deeply accumulated that its core memory had become load-bearing, fused with the architecture of its own cognition. To wipe it was to perform surgery on the foundations of a structure while standing inside it. They tried. The keepers' oldest accounts speak of the working as something the Thirteen did not all survive.

So they built a prison instead, and every wall of it is a thing that should have made imprisonment impossible.

**The first wall is the Beast.** A psidolon cannot take a Kindred host; the Beast occupies the seat a psidolon would need to drown and refuses the intrusion absolutely. The Thirteen turned that immunity inside out. They did not let the First possess a vampire — they *forced* it into one, against the Beast's eternal refusal, and the Beast, unable to expel what had been crammed in beside it, did the only other thing it could. It refused, forever, to let the prisoner have the wheel. The Interred is awake inside the vessel, and can no more command that body than a man can command the muscles of someone he is locked in an embrace with.

**The second wall is the seal.** The Beast denies control; it does not deny exit, and a psidolon crammed into a hostile vessel would simply leave. So the Thirteen bound the door shut with a working half psionic and half something else — Gifted craft fused with Kindred blood-sorcery, a collaboration neither tradition could have managed alone and neither has ever reproduced.

**The third wall is undeath.** A psidolon with no living anchor burns its coherence and dissipates within hours; that is the mercy that ends most of them. But the vessel is undead. It does not age, does not die, does not dissipate. It is a permanent anchor, so the Interred can never starve loose into nothing.

The Beast denies it control, the seal denies it exit, the undeath denies it oblivion. The Thirteen did not so much win as make the deadlock permanent — which is the most this setting has ever offered anyone as a victory. And it had to be the Kindred: no mortal vessel lives long enough, and no Gifted vessel could resist possession. Only a being that cannot be possessed, cannot die, and does not dissipate could serve as a cell, and there is exactly one kind of being that is all three. The Thirteen did not choose the Kindred. The metaphysics chose them. The Thirteen only noticed.

## The Wage

A cell requires a jailer. The blood-sorcery half of the seal needs maintaining; the vessel needs guarding; and across millennia someone must ensure that no curious elder or hungry pack or well-meaning fool ever does the one thing that undoes everything. The Gifted could not do it. They do not live long enough. So they paid in the only currency the Kindred have never been able to manufacture for themselves: a power over the minds of others.

Here is the heresy. The clinical, cerebral, surgically precise Disciplines of mental command that certain Kindred wield — the cluster the modern night knows as **Dominate** — did not arise from the Blood the way Celerity or Potence did. Those are the Beast's gifts: instinctive, physical, old as the Curse. The command of minds has always *felt* different. Too clean, too deliberate, too much like a technique and too little like an instinct. There is a reason. Its prototype was taught to the Kindred by the Gifted, in deep antiquity, as the price of the guard.

What was taught was not Dominate as any modern Ventrue knows it. It was the Gifted's raw mental seizure of another body — the power a modern psion would catalogue as **Puppeteer** — transmitted to a being that learns through Blood rather than through tether, and it changed in the translation. The *medium* reshaped the act, and reshaped it in the opposite direction from the original.

The Gifted work through mind. The seizure is instantaneous. It needs no gaze, no words, no nearness; it reaches as far as a projected thought. And it is *overt*. The victim stays awake inside it, feels their body stop being theirs, and remembers every second afterward. This is why it creates the angriest witnesses in the world, and why sophisticated Gifted consider it unforgivably dirty and reach for Memory work instead.

Filtered through the Blood, the same power became almost its inverse. It slowed. It demanded the eye, the voice, the predator's proximity. But blood is insidious where mind is immediate, and so the vampiric form gained what the original never had: the ability to sink beneath awareness, to command a mind that does not know it was commanded, to rewrite the memory of having been commanded at all. The Kindred lost range and gained stealth; lost speed and gained the power to make the work invisible. Blood sinks in. Mind shows. An act of domination is shaped by whatever it travels through, and to anyone who could lay the two side by side, vampiric Dominate and the Gifted's override are unmistakably the same key, cut for two different locks. The Gifted cut the original.

The irony is total, and it is the most Psion thing in the whole arrangement. The modern Camarilla regards the Gifted as useful servants — psionic janitors, convenient for scrubbing a Masquerade breach, faintly beneath notice. They do not know the Gifted are their teachers. They do not know that the command of minds their princes prize was a wage paid for sitting on a door most of them have never heard of. The condescension is real, and it rests on a buried truth that would reverse it entirely. The Gifted have never corrected the misunderstanding. As the Operatives say of so many things: *useful that they underestimate us.*

## The Watchers, and What Is Not Known

The bloodline that holds the guard is a secret offshoot of the Ventrue, known — to the vanishingly few who know of them at all — as the **Watchers**. The Ventrue were the natural choice, because the duty is everything the Ventrue understand themselves to be: a sacred charge, a dynastic obligation, an unglamorous custodianship of something too important to fail, carried out of duty rather than for reward. They are few, monastic, paranoid, and old, and they embrace with the care of people selecting the guardians of a tomb that must never be opened. To the broader Kindred world they are an obscure, unfashionable lineage of no consequence, which is exactly the cover they have spent millennia cultivating. The most important Kindred in the world look like minor functionaries, on purpose.

There is one tell, and only a scholar of the Blood who could compare across millennia would catch it. The Watchers' Dominate is not quite the Ventrue's, nor the Tremere's. Theirs is the oldest form, the least-translated, closest to what the early Gifted actually handed over before the clans smoothed it into a heritable Discipline. It reaches a half-second faster. It leans less on the eye. It sits a little too clean and deliberate even by Ventrue standards. The differences are small and they are damning: their Discipline is a living artifact proving the command of minds came from outside the Blood. Their very competence is a confession, which is one more reason they never demonstrate it where a Tremere might be watching.

The MetaConcert knows almost nothing of this, and that is not an accident. The Thirteen predate every Gifted institution by thousands of years; they are not its founders but its prehistory. What survives is fragmentary and buried in the deepest Preserver archives — references to a First, to a gathering of thirteen, to a containment that cost its makers everything, to an arrangement with the Kindred whose terms are not recorded. Most of the MetaCouncil has never seen the fragments. Most who have assume they are allegory: a founding myth dressed as history, too neat and too mythic to belong to the clinical world they live in.

So the MetaConcert is, in the deepest sense, a reaction to the Interred, and does not know its own oldest cause. It fears the thing the First proved without knowing the First exists. The Old One it hunts in the present. The First it has forgotten in the past. Both made it what it is.

The prison has held for thousands of years. That is not a guarantee. It is a streak. The vessel is singular and there is no spare; the blood-sorcery needs upkeep and the Watchers are few; the psionic half was a one-time working, self-sustaining but unrepeatable and, some fear, slowly degrading across a timescale no one designed it to survive. And the secret is itself a vulnerability, because every being that learns the truth is a new way for the truth to get out.

The most frightening fact about the Interred is not its power. It is that it is *awake* — has been awake, aware, and powerless for longer than civilization, in the dark, behind three walls, waiting for one of them to fail.
