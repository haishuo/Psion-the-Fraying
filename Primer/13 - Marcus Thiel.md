# Marcus Thiel

*The apostate Memory specialist, the sister he keeps, and what ten years off the lattice makes of a person.*

---

## Elena

Marcus Thiel is twenty-two years old and has been hunting the same psidolon for ten years. He will not stop until one of them is gone.

He was twelve when his power began to Trigger — not fully; it surfaces gradually, like pressure building behind a wall — but enough to be *different*, and enough to attract attention from things that wanted what he represented. A mid-tier psidolon recognized his latent signature as a desirable vessel: a Gifted host is more resilient, more hospitable, more useful than an ordinary human. But latent psionics carry instinctive resistance, and it could not take him cleanly.

So it took the nearest person he loved instead. **Elena Thiel was eight years old** — bright, cheerful, devoted to her big brother with the uncomplicated worship only a younger sibling manages. She was gone before Marcus understood what was happening. The thing took her face, her body, her biological existence, and left behind something wearing her like a costume.

He has never forgiven himself. In his darkest moments he knows the arithmetic with brutal clarity: it wanted *him.* If it had taken him, Elena would have lived. She would be eighteen now.

---

## What the Institution Did

A grieving child with nowhere to turn went to the institution that was supposed to protect the Gifted. He told the MetaConcert what happened and asked for help. They integrated him into the lattice, assigned him to Integrator training, and told him the psidolon was their problem to handle on their timeline.

Then, with clinical efficiency, they dispatched Operative Memory specialists to correct his parents. Standard protocol: newly integrated Gifted cannot have civilian family who know too much. His mother and father are alive somewhere, still breathing — but the son named Marcus and the daughter named Elena have been quietly removed from their minds. They don't grieve Elena because they don't remember Elena. They don't wonder where Marcus went because Marcus never existed to them. They didn't do it to hurt him. That is almost worse. They did it because it is simply *what they do*, filed beside Integration paperwork and tether monitoring reports.

Marcus was now the only person in the world who remembered that Elena Thiel had ever lived.

He was never fraying. He was uncooperative — reasonably, even magnificently so, a boy whose sister had been eaten and whose grief had been filed as a scheduling matter. That is not a tether problem. But Praxis has never required damage:

> *"So what was your plan? Keep him in the Integrators forever?"*
> *"That would be a gross misuse of resources. Should the specimen continue to refuse cooperation, Corrective measures would be enforced to ensure stable psychology."*
> *"The* specimen *is a* person*. He has a* name*."*
> *"…We are revising standard protocol to include incompatible specimens and units that cannot be properly managed."*

Correction — the rebuilding of a mind from the inside — had been a procedure for the critically frayed and the criminal. Praxis was *expanding* it to cover the merely inconvenient: not *he is breaking,* but *he will not behave.* And nothing protected him. A Gifted who is a *member* of a Circuit is shielded by that Circuit's sovereignty; being *under* the Integrators — a ward, a trainee, a case — is not the same as *being* one, and Marcus was a member of nothing. No Circuit, no standing; no standing, no rights; and to Praxis, a Gifted with no rights is simply a specimen.

He bolted first, fleeing not merely the leash but the quiet erasure of everything in him that refused the leash. Before he went he made sure to learn one thing: how to kill a psidolon. The answer is Memory, specifically total erasure. A psidolon *is* its memories in a fundamental metaphysical sense; without them it cannot cohere. It dissipates. The same technique the MetaConcert used on his parents is the only weapon that can end this. He made his cold peace with that, then cut the lattice out of his own skull — painful, dangerous, alone in the dark, with no one to tell him whether it would work — and walked away.

He had taken far more than his track required in the years they held him: enough Panopticon method to slip their sweeps ever since, enough of the Inquisition's own elimination doctrine to know that total erasure is the only real kill. They taught the apostate everything he would need. They only thought they were raising a teacher. The Inquisition has never forgiven Praxis for it — the Circuit that exists to *kill* compromised Gifted looked at Marcus and saw a grieving young man with a name and a reason; the Circuit that exists to *study and repair* looked at the same boy and saw a unit that would not manage. In the MetaConcert, the executioners are the ones who still see you.

---

## The Ritual, and the Discipline

Every night without exception, Marcus performs deliberate Memory recall of Elena. Her face, her voice, the specific way she laughed, the unremarkable details of ordinary life together that would have faded in anyone else years ago. He has held every one of them perfectly in place for ten years.

This is not grief work in any therapeutic sense. It is love and defiance at once. The world took Elena and then took the people who remembered her; Marcus refuses to complete the erasure. It is also a corrective: every lead leaves him new memories of the thing wearing her face, doing what Elena never did, and the ritual keeps that boundary clear.

Ten years of that, unsupervised, with no institution watching the meter. His tether is in better condition than it has any right to be after ten years of unsupervised use — partly natural resilience, mostly discipline. He does not use his power casually; every application is purposeful and measured. His Thoughtshield is exceptional and held near-maximum whenever he suspects observation, and not merely for operational security: on the lattice, Panopticon monitors could reach in at any time and *see* Elena. He was not going to let the MetaConcert have her too. His mental walls are the walls of the only place Elena still exists.

The fraying that has happened to him over ten years is not metaphysical. It is the ordinary human kind — the slow erosion of reasons to stay, the calcification of purpose into architecture, the quiet replacement of a life with a mission. No monitor can measure that. It is real anyway.

---

## The Hunt

The psidolon has stayed in Elena's body the entire ten years, which is unusual; most burn through hosts faster as memory fragmentation forces them into fresher vessels. That it stayed is what makes the hunt possible at all — a host-hopper would be nearly untraceable, since detecting a signature requires a Gifted with the right technique in physical proximity, and nobody can glance at a photograph and confirm possession. **Because it stayed, he isn't hunting a psidolon. He's hunting a woman** — a genuinely living one. The soul is gone; the flesh is not undead and not preserved. It ages, eats, and bleeds, and has spent ten years growing into an adult, with nobody left inside who used to call it home.

The harder problem is that no image of that face exists anywhere but inside his own head: the erasure protocol scrubbed the paper trail too — birth records, school photos, the ordinary residue of a life. So he taught himself to draw, badly at first, years ago, specifically so the face in his head could exist somewhere outside it. By now it is exact: the last portrait of Elena Thiel in existence, redrawn and refined across ten years of a discipline no institution taught him. Without it there is nothing to hand anyone.

He sells Memory work for cash — a Prince who needs a witness's evening lifted clean, a Virtual Adept who needs a hack scrubbed — because rent does not care what he is mourning. He takes cash and never blood or loyalty; a debt like that has no clean edge, and clean edges are the whole point. Standing behind every job, paid or not, is the portrait and the same question: *have you seen this girl.* Some contacts price a lead knowing exactly what it is worth to him. He pays every time, without negotiating.

**And he corrects people the instant they call it Elena** — every time, sharply enough that new contacts learn not to do it twice. His network only has her face to go on, so they reach for her name; it costs them nothing to say and costs Marcus everything to let slide. The nightly ritual keeps the line clear inside his own head; a contact saying *"we found Elena"* is that same line being erased from outside by someone who doesn't know they're doing it.

> *"That's not Elena. Let's get that straight. But yes — please keep an eye out for a woman matching this description. I will pay whatever I need to."*

## Isolde, and the Refusal

Marcus deals with Kindred the way he deals with everyone: honest about what he wants, reliable in what he promises, careful about what he extends. He is wary of the Camarilla's structural relationship with the MetaConcert — he knows about the cleanup arrangements and is careful never to become something they would classify as a loose end. He will not go back to the institution. That is not negotiable.

**Isolde is the exception:** a Ventrue four centuries dead, and the one person he lets *know* him. The same enemy carved both. Years before it took Elena, in an earlier host, the psidolon was fed upon by Isolde — and as her fangs killed its anchor it tore open her oldest memory, gave her back the sun and her living human self, then made her watch her own Embrace happen again. While she drowned in it, it *spent* her: drained the control out of her grip and made her finish a man she had meant only to sip. She spent a decade calling that corpse her own lapse. Marcus, hired once to erase the witness without knowing what he was burying, later read it and could prove what she could not grant herself — the thing spent her; her discipline never broke. That absolution is most of why she is the one person he permits to see him.

**The offer.** Early, before she understood what he was, Isolde offered him the Embrace. Her logic was sound from where she stood: a vampire is *immune* to possession where a living Gifted is merely resistant, and undeath would make him tireless and proof against the fate that already cost him Elena.

He told her no, and told her why, because he does not lie to her. The Embrace would not sharpen his hunt; it would *end* it. A psidolon is killed exactly one way — total Memory erasure — and the Embrace destroys the power utterly. The tether is a feature of *living* tethered existence; undeath and the Beast override it, and there is no psychic vampire. He would gain the hunt and lose the kill.

What that revealed is among the truest things about him. He did not refuse out of horror at becoming a monster. **He would take the Embrace tonight** — walk willingly into undeath, the Beast, the loss of the sun and every human thing left in him — if it bought a single better chance at the creature wearing Elena's face. His own humanity is not sacred to him; it is one more resource, and he has already spent most of it. The line is not *I refuse to become a monster.* It is *I refuse to lose the one thing that can end this.*

He does not plan past the hunt — a conscious architectural decision, not denial. If he seriously imagined a life after, he would have to confront that he does not know who Marcus Thiel is without this purpose. He is not suicidal and does not seek death. He considers it an acceptable outcome and has never pretended otherwise to anyone who asked. *"Either the monster dies, or I do. Or both. Either way, the story ends."*

---

## Why He Never Says "the Noesis"

He was trained inside the vocabulary and could use it perfectly. He does not. He is unaffiliated and off-lattice, and refusing the word is the tell: the institution's name for the thing is a claim of ownership over it, and Marcus concedes them nothing — not his sister, not his parents, not the word for what he can do.

He is the setting's exemplar of what a player character looks like. Not a hero, not a soldier, not a true believer in any cause. Squishy, mortal, ordinary in every respect a supernatural world counts — he eats, he pays rent, he sleeps in cheap rooms, and anything with claws could kill him. He wins by knowing how everyone thinks, by being unremarkable enough not to stick in memory, and by being already gone before they arrive. The Inquisition has sent people after him twice. Both times he was gone before they got there.

Trained Gifted who catch a glimpse past his Thoughtshield find something that isn't rage — rage would be easier. It's closer to a candle in a very dark room. Small, steady, and absolutely refusing to go out.

---

*"She was eight years old. She thought I was the coolest person in the world. She deserved sixty more years of being Elena. I'm going to make sure the last thing that happened to her wasn't the last word on her life. Then we'll see about after."*
— **Marcus Thiel**
