# The Dreambound

*The changeling-Gifted: two whole natures in one body, the fray-free road that is not psionics, the one tether-mend in the setting, and the culture that exists to remember everyone else.*

A changeling-Gifted is far more viable than most cross-splat collisions, and the reason is simple: a changeling is not undead, not a spirit, not a thing the tether struggles to read. A changeling is a *living mortal* — one who ages, bleeds, and dies — who also carries a reincarnated fae soul, bound to the mortal self by the Changeling Way. They are alive in every sense the tether requires, and they do not have *less* soul than a baseline human. They have **more:** a doubled soul, mortal and fae, in one body. A changeling has a tether like anyone living, and can be Gifted like anyone living.

The MetaConcert has no real name for this, only a clinical drawer to file it in: an *anomaly,* an incompatible specimen, a unit that will not integrate. The name that stuck came from elsewhere. It is the Projects' word, and the changelings' word — **the Dreambound:** bound by a tether like any Gifted, bound to the Dreaming by the soul they carry, and never entirely sure which of the two bindings is the truer one.

**Do not confuse them with Eve.** Eve is one thing the taxonomy cannot place — born a fusion, carrying a tether made of something that does not fray. **A Dreambound is two things in one body**, each doing its own thing correctly: an ordinary Gifted who is also an ordinary changeling. Nothing about their tether is exotic. It is a tether. Nothing is partial about their fae soul and nothing is partial about their tether — not half of anything, wholly both.

And unlike Eve, they need no birth lottery — but there is exactly **one road in, and it is a birth.** A Dreambound is **fae-souled:** a full changeling who is also Gifted. Under the Changeling Way a returning fae soul takes an *infant* and then *sleeps* inside an ordinary-seeming child, until the **Chrysalis** — which is not an arrival but **a waking.** Nothing ever lands on a grown Gifted from outside. Whatever wakes in a Dreambound was in them the whole time.

Which is where the numbers come from, because **a fae soul choosing a cradle is not choosing blind, and the reaching, vivid mind of a Gifted is precisely the kind of brightness it is drawn to.** Gifted capacity is present from birth; the Trigger only crosses a threshold that was always there, and a mind built to project, to reach outward, to impose will on the world *dreams loud* long before it ever reaches. Some corner of the Dreaming half-knows the Gifted exist, the way a moth knows a window — and knows it about the cradle, not the adult. So the Dreambound are not singular the way Eve is: not common, but real in numbers.

**The strangeness is in the timing.** A Dreambound has two thresholds to cross and one adolescence to cross them in, and the same things trigger both — terror, grief, wonder, a night that will not fit into the life before it. So the Trigger and the Chrysalis land in the same year, sometimes the same week, each a plausible cause of the other. That child is **Raw and newly-Chrysalised at once**, drowning in two directions from two natures neither of which has a name yet, while an Integrator team drives toward the house because the Panopticon heard the loud half. The setting's own definition of a Trigger says it without meaning to: *nothing arrives that was not already there; a threshold is simply crossed.* That is the Trigger. It is also, word for word, the Chrysalis. A Dreambound is someone to whom that sentence is true twice.

## Stranger, never stronger

Their tether is **ordinary.** They fray exactly as any Gifted does — *and worse.*

When a Dreambound reaches for the power the ordinary, instrumental way — clean remote-viewing, precise Intrusion, the mind imposing itself on reality like a tool — the cool, will-driven, reality-as-machine use of the mind is itself a **Banal act.** It abrades the fae soul even as the tether-work frays the tether. Done the textbook way, the power costs them *twice.* The orthodox mode is therefore their **worst** one, not their best. There is no buffer and no discount; over a life the ledger comes due exactly as it does for everyone but Eve.

What they have instead is a **second road**, and the whole point of it is that it does not run through the tether at all.

Take their version of Projection. An ordinary Gifted projecting is doing remote viewing: a psionic security camera trained on a distant room, cool and exact — *report what you see.* A Dreambound reaches a *real* place, a room with no one sleeping in it, no dream and no Dreaming present. But they do not view that room; they *go* to it, the way one goes somewhere in a dream, and what they bring back is dreamlike: distorted, charged, symbol-heavy, the place rendered as a self experienced it rather than as a lens recorded it. It is dreamwalking to a place that isn't dreaming. It runs through the fae soul, for which participatory dream-reaching is simply natural, and so it **frays nothing.** The intelligence it yields is unreliable and hard to act on — a dream of the truth instead of a photograph of it.

The same door opens inward, into a sleeper's dream: walk it, shape it, speak through it — become the bird that keeps pace with a flying dreamer and says something cryptic and true, so the sleeper wakes knowing a thing they cannot account for. All of that is real, and fray-free, and **none of it is psionic.** They can do it because they are, fully and in their own right, a changeling; walking and working dreams is the changeling birthright, older than any tether, and a pure fae does every bit of it, usually more gracefully. The power adds *nothing* to the dreamwork. The only way it could intrude is as a temptation to refuse — they *could* drop the dream-craft and pulse a flat psionic message instead, which, being the banal instrument, would fray them. So they don't. They let the message be the dream.

## The one door that is not locked

The Gifted half contributes exactly one thing, and almost no Dreambound ever does it. **In a sleeper's dream, a sufficiently powerful Dreambound can see the tether** — sees the dream *and the tether running through it,* where a pure changeling sees only a dream, because they carry one themselves and know from the inside what they are looking at. It is the apex of what they can become, both natures at full stretch, neither remotely sufficient alone.

Weigh what that sight costs everyone else. Most Gifted cannot perceive a tether at all; the rare Detection specialist who can pays a steep fray price, will not spend it often, and — the crux — having paid it, can do *nothing* about what they have seen. Seeing was never fixing. A Dreambound sees it for free, standing in the dream where it lives, and then, alone in all the world, can reach out and *work* on it.

The reason is the thing the grey apparatus has buried so deep that even the Gifted forget it: **the tether is not Banal.** The lattice is Banal, Praxis is Banal, the White Room is Banality given walls, and the instrumental use of the power is a Banal act. But the tether itself — the living bond tying a mind to a body to a soul, making of the three one whole person — is nothing of the kind. It is one of the oldest fairy tales there is: *the thread that holds a self together.* The Banality was always in the cage, never in the bird.

What a Dreambound mends in the Dreaming stays mended when the sleeper wakes: **the one and only exception to the iron law that nothing accelerates a tether's healing.** But it is not a technique and never becomes one — no procedure, no one-two-three that mends any tether twice. Even the one who managed it last night may not manage it tonight.

The hardest part is not the mending. It is *finding* the tether. Something in the dream stands for it; it must, and it always does. What is not certain is whether the Dreambound can find which thing it is. A dream goes on forever and is crowded with everything, none of it labelled, and the tether could be wearing any face in it: a wounded bird, a broken bridge, a tree, a stranger met on a road. Nothing announces itself as *this is the self; this is the cord that holds it.* Their doubled nature buys a crude sense of warmer and colder, better than nothing and a long way from certain. When they find it they know it the way one knows things in dreams — wordless certainty, no proof and no need of any. A Dreambound can walk a dream the night through and never once lay a hand on the cord running under all of it.

Then the second problem: repairing the thing entirely within the dream's own logic — splice the rope, ease the rot from the tree, gentle the serpent — slow and roundabout, and the only way the work can be done. And it is bounded on every side. It cannot lift a tether above the baseline it was born with; this heals damage, it does not add capacity. It cannot touch the scars — the deepest fraying and the damage earned at the very brink are permanent here as everywhere. It cannot reach the snapped, because a torn-free mind leaves no dreamer and no dream to enter. It is dangerous in exact proportion to the need: the dream of a critically frayed mind is a collapsing nightmare, and mending a tether at the lip of the Snap means standing inside a structure as it comes down. And hardest of all, **it does not work on themselves.** One cannot be a guest in one's own dream, nor see one's own tether with the outside eye the mending needs. A Dreambound can pull others back from the brink and still fray to death alone, untended — the one gift that might have saved them forever facing the wrong way. They are healers of every tether but the one they carry.

**Unreliability does not shrink the size of the fact.** Praxis's doctrine holds, flatly, that nothing repairs a tether. A Dreambound does not refute it reliably, or on demand, or even often — but a law either holds always or it was never absolute. Every time it happens, the doctrine is not bent. It is broken. And for a Gifted staring at their own fraying, a Dreambound is not a plan; it is hope in the specific sense that word takes on once every ordinary option has already failed — the reach a person makes for an experimental therapy after standard treatment has nothing left to try. Most who make that reach will not be mended. Some will. Nobody, including the Dreambound trying, can say in advance which night is which. That the cure is real and unreachable is its own horror, and a different one from having no cure at all.

**And the near-miss is not a lesser Dreambound; it is a different thing.** A **kinain** is fae-*blooded* — descended from a changeling/mortal union, carrying a trace of the Dreaming in the blood rather than a second self in the seat. Real, and not the same: no Chrysalis to wait for, no told story, no fray-free dream-road, and **no Seeing the Thread and no mend, permanently.** Blood is not a smaller soul. The Dreambound call them **cousins** and mean it warmly; the MetaConcert has no word for them at all, files them as ordinary Gifted, and is for once entirely right. Note the trade runs both ways — a cousin is safer in every respect, pays for the power only once instead of twice, and has nothing the White Room can dissolve. **The reason they are safe is that they are not carrying anything.**

## The told story

Every Gifted has an innate floor beneath the Domains: before a single dot, a psion *receives* the surface cognition of every unshielded mind nearby — real thoughts, real feelings, indiscriminate, unattributed, simultaneous, contextless, a firehose with no valve. **A Dreambound has that same floor, and the same input.** But it passes through the fae soul on the way in, and arrives as **story** — not the deep one, which is not available at the surface to anyone, but the surface one, the story a person is telling the world. *I'm a good person. I've got it together. I'm competent. I'm fine. None of this is my fault.*

**None of which has to be true.** What arrives is the story someone is telling, or the story they badly want told about them, and it does not announce which. **It is not a lie detector.** A confident liar reads as a clean untroubled story, because his presentation is the one part of him that is working; a decent person mid-collapse reads as *I am falling apart,* which is true and tells you nothing about whether to trust them. The sense reports **presentation, never veracity.** Everyone else in the setting reads what a person is thinking; a Dreambound reads what a person is *asking to be believed*, and learns nothing from it about whether to believe them. Their characteristic error is the one the sense is built to produce: **mistaking a mask for a face.**

**Raw, it drowns them like anyone — differently.** A hundred simultaneous minds is a flood of facts. A hundred people **insisting on themselves at once** is a hundred arguments pointed at a child who has no way to reach a verdict on any of them, and Dreambound children in that state do not go quietly overloaded, they go **paranoid.** And they broadcast their own story outward as loudly as any Unshielded psion — which a Panopticon sweep reads as an ordinary Unshielded signature, the lattice having no category for what is actually leaking and needing none to send a car.

**Bracing resolves it, and it is still bracing** — free, standing, below the scale, gating the flood and containing the broadcast in one act. What differs is who teaches it. The **Integrator way** draws the skin and holds the door shut; it works perfectly, and it is also the first Banal act of that child's life, administered at twelve by someone genuinely saving them who has no idea what else is in the room. The **fae-native way**, taught in a freehold by another Dreambound, is not a wall but an attention: *keep your place* — hold your finger on the line you are actually reading, and let the rest go on being none of your business. Same result, opposite direction, and it costs the fae half nothing.

Neither switches the sense off. Which is why a **MetaConcert campus** is, to a Dreambound, a city of the mute: a braced psion leaks nothing, so they cross a quad among several hundred people **with no stories at all.** That silence is not the psidolon silence and no Dreambound past their first year confuses them — a braced mind is a **closed door**, a story present and held; a psidolon is an **open door with nothing behind it.**

**Depth is what the Dreaming is for.** The surface is all a waking room offers, to them as to any psion. Inside a dream the story stops being a claim and becomes the thing itself — longer, deeper, and far likelier to be true, because a dreamer is presenting to nobody. What it is not is straightforward: dream logic holds all the way down, and what is true arrives dressed as a wolf. **Nearer to true and further from plain speech are the same movement.** So: one sense at three depths — the told story, awake and free; the lived story, in the Dreaming, earned and interpreted; and beneath both, in the rarest hands, the thread the whole story hangs from.

**And a last cruelty in the arithmetic.** A Dreambound can buy **Detection** dots, and they work — the clean, directed, attributed read every other psion has. But that attention is the *instrumental* attention: it works by setting the fae lens aside, and every dot walks their Banality floor up. The stories get quieter and the words get louder, until one day a Dreambound walks into a room and hears **what people are thinking** — clean, accurate, attributed, exactly what every Gifted in the world has always had and considers a gift. They will never be able to explain to anyone why it is the worst thing that has ever happened to them.

## No story to garble

A Dreambound does not sort people into categories. They read them. Marcus is not *human, Gifted, Railroad;* he is a man carrying a candle through a hurricane because he refuses to let his sister disappear. Even a sad life is still, unmistakably, a *story:* a throughline, a self that has stayed continuously itself.

A psidolon is not a sadder story, or a garbled one. **There is no story to garble.** It remembers. It plans. It speaks convincingly, sometimes perfectly. Those are all *events* — and a narrative is not a sequence of events. A narrative requires a continuing self the events are happening *to.* A psidolon has the chapters and no author. Ask a Dreambound what meeting one felt like and the honest answer sits closer to *reading dialogue with no speaker,* or *things kept happening, but nothing was about anything.*

This costs nothing and requires no Domain — free and instinctive, what happens automatically when a mind built to read every soul-intact person as a story meets the one thing that was never a mind. It is the same absence the setting already measures as Coherence, read through fae eyes. A young Dreambound often assumes this is how everyone perceives the world, and is startled the first time someone asks *what do you mean, no story.* It also makes them nearly impossible for a psidolon to fool, and it has nothing to do with strength: the thing being imitated was never what a Dreambound is reading. *Marcus's story is still about Elena. This isn't about anything.* A Detection specialist reports *no tether;* a wraith says *there's nobody in there;* a Dreambound simply notices there is no story. Three senses, one absence, none of them wrong.

## What they treasure

Ask a changeling what stories matter and most answer in the key the Dreaming taught them: the myths, the archetypes, the courtly epics, the Hero, the Fall, the Return of the King. A Dreambound does not deny those are stories, or that they are beautiful. They simply do not believe they are the greatest ones. To a Dreambound the greatest story in any room is never the famous one. **It is whichever life in that room no one else will remember** — and there is always one, and usually most of them.

**They are storytellers, not historians, and the distinction is a moral one.** A historian keeps the record that matters to the future: the reign, the battle, the treaty, the name worth carving. A Dreambound keeps the record that will otherwise simply *stop* — the life that ends and closes and is gone, as if it had never happened. Kings have chroniclers already. It is the baker who fed a lane for forty years, the servant who left no name in any ledger, the traveller no one on the road will recall by morning whose story a Dreambound will cross a kingdom to hear, because those are the ones about to vanish, and vanishing is the only thing a Dreambound counts as death.

**And a story belongs to the one who lived it.** The MetaConcert takes memory by force — lifts it, reads it, edits it, erases it, and never once asks. A Dreambound will not so much as *retell* a story without the leave of the person whose story it is. Held in trust, never owned. In a setting where minds are surveilled, extracted, and Corrected as routine, the people who will not repeat even a secret freely given are quietly among its few uncorrupted ethics.

Their treasure is what you would expect of the fae, and nothing like it: no gold, no relics, but a vault full of *lives* — every remembered person a jewel, and every ordinary life the rarest gem, because it is the kind the world keeps no copies of. It is why they are so taken with *things:* a worn copper pot, a scratched wristwatch, a doorstep hollowed by a century of feet. Not for what any of it would fetch. For what it has *witnessed.*

And so, the one sentence that holds the rest. A Dreambound does not believe stories belong to heroes. **They believe heroes are only the rare people whose stories everyone happens to remember — and that their own calling is to remember everyone else.**

## What the institution would do to them

**The whole of MetaConcert pedagogy *is* the instrumental mode:** precision, control, the clean read, reality handled like an apparatus. Taught to a Dreambound, that curriculum is a slow injury. It drills them in the one way of using the power that frays them hardest *and* corrodes the fae soul, while dismissing their fae-native dream-reaching as undisciplined psionics to be corrected out. The White Room is the sharp end: to an ordinary Gifted, Correction is a cell and a procedure; to a Dreambound it is a **solvent**, severing them from the fae-native road — no dream survives that air — and dissolving the fae soul itself.

But that is only the sharp end. **The MetaConcert is, in plain metaphysical fact, one of the most Banal things the setting contains** — a vast clinical apparatus of lattice and ledger and rote, built to *manage* wonder out of existence. The White Room kills the fae soul in an afternoon; the institution does it slowly, just by holding someone inside it. A Dreambound taken into custody, integrated, processed, made to live the grey clinical life of a managed asset does not need to be Corrected to lose their second self. The Banality alone starves it. The fae soul dims, withdraws, and goes home to the Dreaming the way it would at the Snap — except no Snap is needed and no one is watching for it. What is left is a hollowed-out *ordinary* Gifted, who has lost the back door, lost the part of themselves that never frayed, lost the wonder they were carrying, and can from then on only use the power the one fraying way. **The first thing to go is the quietest:** the told stories thin out year by year until one morning they do not arrive at all, and there are only thoughts there instead — plain, clean, correctly attributed. Nobody schedules that morning. They simply wake up perceiving other people the way the institution has always perceived them.

**This is the saddest thing that can happen to one of them** — sadder than the clean horror of a White Room, because it is not done with intent or even with notice. The institution does not know what it killed. It files a successfully integrated unit and moves on.

The net is centrifugal: the Dreambound are pushed *away* from campus and clinic and *toward* freeholds and the Dreaming, where their gift works and the air does not hurt — off-grid by metaphysical gravity, the one population the Panopticon's habitat-map points in exactly the wrong direction to find. And the same gravity that dooms them inside the MetaConcert saves them outside it: a Project bolthole was built by people who refused the lattice, the ledger, and the management of minds, which makes it, almost incidentally, a place where Banality does not rule. A Dreambound there does not merely hide. They can be whole — in exactly the way the MetaConcert would have called impossible, and then quietly erased.

## Not psionics done differently

**None of this is psionics done differently.** There is no eighth Domain hiding in what a Dreambound does, no "instinctual Memory" or "passive Projection" extending the seven-Domain taxonomy to cover them. What they do in a sleeper's dream — including the tether-mend — is not psionics wearing a fae costume, and it is not pure changeling Dreaming either. It is a third thing, born only where both natures meet at full strength, irreducible to either parent. **The told story is not that third thing** — it is the plain psionic innate floor every Gifted has, merely translated on the way in by the organ it passes through. The third thing is rare, earned and apex; the translation is free and arrives with the person. Sight is not a strange form of hearing; smell is not a weak form of taste. Even Project Heterodox, whose whole thesis is that the map is smaller than the territory, assumes the missing territory is still *psionic* territory, merely uncharted. It isn't.

Nor is *story*, here, this setting's Consensus. The tether is real and objective whether or not anyone narrates it; it frays and holds by the same hard rules the rest of the setting obeys, witnessed or not. What the fae half provides is not the power to make a thing true by telling it. It is the only vocabulary capable of *perceiving and touching* something this abstract at all. Story is the lens a fae mind sees the tether through. It is not the material the tether is made of.

Which marks the edge of what they can change: **their reach stops at minds.** There is no Dreambound version of Kinesis, and never will be — matter has no dream to enter, no story for fae perception to grip. And even inside a mind, the change has to be earned in the dream's own terms. A Dreambound cannot narrate *and then she believed him* into a sleeper's head the way a Gifted might force a Compulsion. The wolf in the dream has to be beaten the way that dream's own logic says wolves get beaten. They are not issuing a command. They are the rare guest clever enough to find the one true answer the story was already, secretly, asking for.
