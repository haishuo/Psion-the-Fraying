# A Short History

*From the age of titans to the seven-Circuit institution: how old this is, and in what order it accreted.*

---

Most of what follows has no date. This is not an oversight. The MetaConcert's deep past lives in its own mythologized, hard-to-verify record. Where hard dates exist — Torquemada, Ivan — they are kept. Where they do not, nothing is invented to fill the gap.

## The Age of Heroes

No Inquisition. No Praxis. No MetaConcert. A scattering of powerful, mostly solitary Gifted — an age of titans who answered to no one, remembered where remembered at all as wizards, oracles, monsters, saints.

Because no one hunted them, psidolons ran rampant. A psidolon could wear a life for decades. It could wear a throne. Some of history's famous sudden personality-replacements — rulers and beloved figures turned overnight into strangers — may have been an Untethered consciousness wearing a crown.

The emblem of the era is the sealing of the Interred: thirteen of the mightiest Gifted who ever lived, most of whom despised each other, joined hands exactly once, because the alternative was worse, to contain a psidolon they could not kill — then scattered again. Not an institution. A one-time armistice among rivals.

## Two Convictions, No Names

What ended the age of titans was organization, and organization began as two convictions rather than two institutions.

**The hunters' conviction:** these things ran wild for want of anyone whose job it was to end them.

**The scholars' conviction:** these things had never been properly understood.

The hunters became the first true order the Gifted ever forged — not a name yet, just *the ones who burned.* The scholars gathered in the great houses of learning of the old world, the University of Sankoré at Timbuktu chief among them — also not a name yet, just *the ones who kept.* Both carried informal, shifting designations for centuries. Neither was called what we call them now.

This is the part worth holding onto: **the orders predate their own names by a very long time, and they did not receive those names at the same moment or for related reasons.** Whatever you know about the words *Inquisition* and *Praxis* tells you nothing about when the things themselves began.

## The Hunters Become "the Inquisition" (~1478–1530s)

Centuries into its existence, still unnamed, the order of hunters embedded itself in the Catholic Church's apparatus under Tomás de Torquemada — using the real Spanish Inquisition's institutional reach, cross-border authority, and legal cover to hunt psidolons and eliminate compromised Gifted under the pretext of heresy trials. The reputation for religious persecution is not fabricated; the cover required real executions to be credible. But the primary targets were always psidolons, and Torquemada was a senior member of the order, not a churchman who happened to share its methods.

The infiltration ran so deep, and so long, that when the operation ended the order never took the mask off. It kept the name. To this day the Inquisition wears the Spanish Inquisition the way a psidolon wears a host — and, like a psidolon that overstays, it never got its own name back.

## The Scholars Become "Praxis"

The scholars' order waited far longer for its name — not at the hour history handed the hunters theirs, but generations afterward, around the 1550s, and **not in Europe.** While the hunters were burying themselves in Catholic Spain, the scholars had simply gone where the learning was, as they always had: Sankoré, then Cairo, then Constantinople. By this point the two orders were not merely diverging in method. They were not living in the same world, and had not been for a long time.

They took their name in the **coffeehouses of Constantinople** — rooms that had existed barely a generation and were already notorious, where men sat up all night arguing and the authorities kept trying to close the doors on the grounds that nothing good is decided after dark. It was the first institution in the world built for an argument to outlast the night, and an order that had been having one argument for six hundred years walked in and recognized the house. There it finally named the conviction it had always acted on: that understanding a system means intervening in it, that observation without intervention is incomplete data. It took the name **Praxis**.

## The Undeclared War and the Concordat of Rhodes

Now named, the two orders were rivals long before they were allies. The ones who burned and the ones who kept: incompatible by instinct, overlapping by claim. What followed was a low and undeclared war, with skirmishes neither side much discusses today.

It ended not in friendship but in the **Concordat of Rhodes** — an armistice between two complementary survival operations with aligned interests and no formal unity. The Inquisition needed Praxis's doctrine. Praxis needed the Inquisition's field data.

They met on Rhodes because there was nowhere in Christendom either order could stand without the other reading it as a claim. The island was Ottoman by then, and had been since the Knights of St. John were starved off it — which made it the one ground in reach that belonged to neither of them and answered to neither of their host institutions. Both orders saw the irony and neither wrote it down: they ended their war in the ruin of a military order that had held a fortress until holding it killed them. The Inquisition took the lesson as vindication. Praxis took it as data.

The Concordat's price was transparency, and Praxis did not pay it honestly. What the Inquisition received was a curated disclosure. What Praxis kept was the Black Archive — the pre-truce record of what the Circuit did when it answered to no one, burned from paper and encoded instead into a handful of living minds. Then the only question that mattered was asked and answered: *Is this everything?* *Yes.* The oldest alliance in the MetaConcert rests on that word. The Inquisition never believed it, has never been able to prove otherwise, and has never forgiven being made to swallow it with open eyes.

Because Praxis came so early, when organizational culture was still forming, its epistemology became the institution's. The cold clinical rationalism is not a tone the MetaConcert adopted. It is the tone Praxis established, and everything after grew inside it.

## The Ivan Incident (1560s)

This happens *inside* the bilateral period, and that is the whole reason it went the way it did. There is no lattice yet, no Panopticon, no Maintainers — only two orders holding an armistice a few years old and calling it, optimistically, a council.

Alexei Voronov, the finest Inquisitor of his generation, spent two decades on a slow, careful encirclement of the psidolon called the Old One — sound doctrine that had worked for generations. Cornered for the first time in possibly centuries, the Old One panicked and fled into Tsar Ivan IV, freshly destabilized by grief, then spent two decades using the Oprichnina to dismantle the Inquisition's Russian infrastructure and kill Voronov himself.

Two generations of institutional capacity, destroyed. Doctrine shifted to fast-brutal: immediate engagement on confirmed identification, no observation period, higher collateral damage accepted in exchange for denying the psidolon any window. That doctrine has governed the Inquisition ever since. It has not caught the Old One in four centuries.

Praxis argued the other way at the time, and lost. Their case was that Ivan proved nothing about patience — that the Old One fled precisely *because* it was cornered, which is what encirclement is for, and that the real lesson was about endgames rather than method. The Inquisition, having just buried two generations of its Russian capacity and the finest man it ever fielded, was in no condition to hear it. The doctrine that governs the Circuit to this day was adopted in grief, over the objection of the people who did the thinking. Praxis has never raised it again.

## The Lattice, and a Government

Praxis's greatest invention was the lattice. Having built it and proved it worked, Praxis made a characteristic decision: the interesting intellectual work was done, and maintenance was beneath its institutional attention. It documented the architecture and stepped back.

Someone had to be created to do the job Praxis would not. The **Maintainers** were the third Circuit, and their existence is the actual founding of the MetaConcert as a government rather than an alliance. Two allied operations did not need governance. Three interdependent institutions did. Praxis built the infrastructure that made formalization inevitable, then watched the organization it had accidentally created develop the authority to constrain it. Its most significant achievement was not intellectual. It was political.


## The Remaining Four

Each of the last four Circuits exists because the first three were handling something badly, part-time, or not at all.

**The Panopticon (fourth).** Once the lattice existed, someone needed dedicated, sustained visibility into it; Inquisitors and Praxis analysts taking shifts had proven inadequate almost immediately. Before the Panopticon, detection meant rumor, town criers, and dispatching the Inquisition on hearsay.

**The Integrators (fifth).** Before them, newly Triggered children were handled by whichever of the Inquisition or Praxis noticed them first — about as well as either institution's temperament would suggest. Said out loud, the arrangement produced a unanimous reaction from every other Circuit, and that reaction was the mandate.

**The Preservers (sixth).** Five Circuits, a lattice, formal governance, and no unified institutional memory — each Circuit keeping its own records its own way. The Preservers were built to solve continuity. They inherited an archive already missing its worst chapter: the hole Praxis cut before the Preservers existed to notice it was gone. Insofar as they know, they hold the complete record of the MetaConcert.

**The Operatives (seventh, last).** External relations are a luxury of stability. By the time the MetaConcert had the confidence to formalize a diplomatic corps, it had been managing the Camarilla, the Technocracy, and the Garou through ad hoc personal relationships for generations, one personality clash from collapse.

---

The founding sequence reads: *survive → understand → build → watch → care → remember → engage.* A coherent institutional biography in seven steps. The organization did not plan itself. It accreted — each Circuit a need it could not have anticipated until the absence became a crisis.
