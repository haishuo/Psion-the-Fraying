# Baltimore: The Cloister and the Clinic

*The default chronicle setting — why this city, its two campuses, and the people who hold the appointments.*

---

## Why Baltimore

Every game needs a place to start. Psion's is Baltimore, because no American city embodies the setting's themes more completely, and few embody them by accident.

**It invented the American research university.** Johns Hopkins opened in 1876 as the first American university built on the German research model — the prototype every American research university copied. To the Gifted this is not trivia; it is lineage. Praxis and American academic medicine grew up as twins, and Baltimore is where that twinship is oldest and deepest. The Baltimore Seat is a Praxis stronghold, one of the founding chapters of the MetaConcert in North America and still one of its most important. If the American Gifted have a spiritual capital, it is here — in the place that taught the country how to turn a university into an instrument.

**Its clinic has a past.** Johns Hopkins Hospital is one of the great hospitals of the world, and its real history includes the kind of research the MetaConcert embodies — work done on bodies and minds whose owners never agreed and were never told. The fiction does not have to manufacture a consent-violating research institution for the Gifted to hide inside. Baltimore handed it one. The Phipps Psychiatric Clinic — among the first psychiatric units inside a general hospital — is a particular gift: an entire tradition of the institutional management of "disordered" minds, the perfect cover for what the MetaConcert does to the Unshielded, the frayed, and the inconvenient.

**It is a watched city.** Baltimore has been a live testbed for mass surveillance — a dense municipal camera network, predictive policing, and, for several months in 2016, a private aircraft secretly photographing the entire city from the sky. The Panopticon did not need to build its apparatus here. It only had to plug into the one the city already had.

**And its map is divided.** Endowed chairs and marble hospital lobbies sit a few blocks from disinvested neighborhoods that have been surveilled, policed, and abandoned in turn. The MetaConcert's institutional power and the Projects' work among the people that power discards are not abstractions here. They are *addresses,* a ten-minute walk apart.

It is Edgar Allan Poe's city. It was always going to be ours.

## The Two Poles

The Baltimore Seat is organized, like the city, around two campuses that are nothing alike.

**Homewood — the Cloister.** The undergraduate campus: red brick and Georgian quads in leafy North Baltimore, ringed by Charles Village row houses. This is where the power *surfaces* — in students, far from home, sleepless and stressed and primed for the trauma that pulls the trigger. Homewood is the recruiting ground. The Integrators work it like a sinister honor society, and Project Railroad works it right back, both racing to reach a newly Triggered nineteen-year-old before the other does. It is also where dissent lives: Heterodox faculty teaching in the same departments as the orthodoxy they have broken with.

**East Baltimore — the Clinic.** The medical campus: the hospital, the dome, the School of Medicine, the research towers — and, hidden among them, the things the Seat does not advertise. The local White Room is here, behind a door in a restricted research wing or a locked Phipps ward, where a frayed Gifted brought in for "treatment" raises no questions in a building full of them. Praxis's real laboratories are here; so, it is whispered, are the ones that are not on any floor plan. And the medical campus sits in the middle of neighborhoods the institution has loomed over for a century — which is exactly where the Projects, the Cult, and the desperate all are. The clinic gleams. The blocks around it do not. Both belong to the story.

**The rest of the board.** The University of Maryland's medical campus and law school sit downtown — a second academic-medical pole, contested ground. The Peabody conservatory in genteel Mount Vernon is where the Seat's senior figures keep their respectable social lives. The Inner Harbor is neutral ground where the Operatives meet Baltimore's Kindred. And out past the Beltway, the university's classified applied-research arm does work with no public clearance — the obvious front for Praxis projects that officially do not exist.

## The Seat

Power here wears a faculty ID. The Seat runs on appointments, directorships, grant committees, and hospital privileges — and the people who hold them.

**Dr. Vivian Ashford-Cole** *(Praxis — the Seat's effective head).* An endowed professor and department chair in the neurosciences, internationally published, beloved by her graduate students, and the coldest mind in the city. She runs the Seat the way she runs her lab: by data, by procedure, and without the comfort of guilt. She is the smallpox-repository argument made flesh — *you cannot defeat what you refuse to study* — and the persistent rumor among the few who know her is that somewhere under the East Baltimore campus, in a room with no number, she keeps a **specimen.** Whether it is true is the kind of question that gets people Sterilized for asking.

**Dr. Asha Iyer** *(Inquisition — the White Room).* An attending psychiatrist at Phipps, gentle with her living patients, who also runs the Seat's White Room and has performed more terminations than anyone in the city. She is on her last permitted rotation; everyone can see what the work has done to her, and no one says so. She believes, completely, that she is the most merciful person in the building.

**"Wren"** *(Panopticon — the watcher).* No one uses the analyst's name. They work from an unremarkable office wired into both the lattice and the city's real surveillance — the cameras, the license readers, the data brokers, and the institutional memory of a place that has always watched its poor. Wren is how a flag becomes an address in Baltimore. Never seen in the field.

**Dean Walter Adler** *(Integrators — the recruiter).* The warm, rumpled dean-of-students figure every undergraduate trusts, which is the entire point. When a student Triggers, Adler is the kind hand on the shoulder, the discreet referral to "a program that can help" — the smile at the front of the trap. He genuinely believes he is saving them. He is usually not wrong, which is what makes him unbearable.

Beneath them: a Maintainer who keeps the Seat's binding constructs and the White Room's isolation; a small Preserver staff buried in the medical library and the archives; and Operatives who manage the wary arrangement with Baltimore's Kindred.

## The Resistance

The same two campuses, read from the other side.

**Theo Calloway** *(Railroad — the conductor).* A former Integrator who could not keep doing to children what Adler does. He runs Baltimore's Railroad out of East Baltimore — intercepting newly Triggered students and frayed runaways, and moving them out to Children of Gaia sanctuary in the Maryland hills along the Patapsco. He knows Adler personally. They trained together. Neither has forgiven the other for the choice the other made.

**"Static"** *(Lacuna — the privacy engineer).* In the most surveilled city in the country, someone has to build the spaces the Panopticon and the cameras both cannot see. Half privacy-tech, half psionic dead-zone craft; makes the Railroad's safe houses possible, and lives in terror of the day Wren finally notices the *shape* of all the places that have gone quiet.

**Dr. Elaine Voss** *(Heterodox — the apostate scholar).* Once Praxis, now the most dangerous kind of dissident: a tenured professor in Ashford-Cole's own department who argues, in seminars and unpublishable papers, that the Domain framework is *wrong* — incomplete, self-serving, a cage the MetaConcert mistook for a map. She is watched constantly and Sterilized never, because removing a tenured Hopkins professor cleanly is harder than letting her talk. The schism between Praxis and Heterodox, in Baltimore, is a faculty feud between two people who share a hallway.

**Brother Ezra Pell** *(the Cult of the Unbound).* The Cult works the margins the institutions abandoned — the support groups, the shelters, the frayed and the failing. Ezra is soft-spoken, genuinely kind, and catastrophically wrong, offering the snap as a door and "transcendence" to people the MetaConcert would simply Sterilize. He has watched it kill before. He keeps preaching.

## Flashpoints

- **The Tap.** A brilliant Homewood sophomore Triggers during finals — loud, Unshielded, terrified. Adler is already drafting the referral; Calloway already has a car. The party has hours, and whichever way they move, they make an enemy who used to be a friend.
- **The Room With No Number.** Someone the party trusts goes into the East Baltimore campus "for tests" and comes out *calmer.*
- **The Eye.** Static finds proof that Wren has tapped the city's real surveillance apparatus and routed it through the lattice. Exposing it could break the Seat. Reaching the proof means going somewhere the Eye is already looking.
- **The Apostate's Problem.** Voss has found real evidence the framework is wrong, and the only reason she is still breathing is that no one is sure how much she has written down. Protect her, debunk her, deliver her, or finish what she started — Praxis is watching all four.
- **The Wake.** A psidolon is hunting in East Baltimore, and the wraiths of its consumed hosts are trying, in the only ways the dead can, to point the living at it. The Inquisition will not work with the Shadowlands. The party might have to.

## Running Baltimore

**Use the geography.** Keep asking which pole a scene belongs to — the cloister or the clinic — and let the ten-minute walk between the gleaming institution and the abandoned block do what no amount of exposition can.

**Power wears a lanyard.** In Baltimore the MetaConcert looks like a faculty meeting, a grant decision, a referral to a specialist, a kindly dean. The horror is that it is *respectable,* and that everyone in the building thinks they are helping. The scariest villain here is a beloved professor with tenure and a clean conscience.

**Honor the real city.** Some of what makes Baltimore *fit* is real harm done to real people: research conducted without consent, decades of surveillance and disinvestment, communities treated as material. Henrietta Lacks was a real woman; the cells taken from her without her knowledge are real; her family's grief is real and recent. Use this history as resonance — the true thing the fiction stands beside — never as a plot trophy or a villain's set-piece, and never fictionalize real victims of medical exploitation. Do not turn struggling neighborhoods into a backdrop. It should hurt because it's true, not because it's lurid.

**And let it breathe.** Baltimore is also crab houses and marble stoops and the Ravens and a particular gallows humor and a fierce local love. A city that is *only* grim is a lie about Baltimore, and a chronicle that is only grim burns its players out. Let there be charm in Charm City. It makes the dark land harder.
