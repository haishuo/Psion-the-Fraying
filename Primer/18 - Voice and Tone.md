# Voice and Tone

*Who is telling you all this, and how to write in the world without breaking it.*

---

## Someone wrote this, and it matters who

The book has an in-world author. The voice belonged to **Praxis** once — trained in the Domain system, fluent in its Greek, credentialed by the institution the book spends most of its length indicting. They left. They write for **Project Heterodox** now, and what you are reading is what Heterodox's own charter predicts it must be: not the alternative framework, but *the documented proof that the existing one is incomplete.*

You can watch it happen in the vocabulary. In his own voice he writes *the power*, *Triggered*, *technique*, *misfire* — the plain words, lowercase, the ones a mother uses. That plainness was **built.** The counter-language is a Heterodox invention, engineered on purpose by people who understood from the inside that Praxis's vocabulary was never neutral — that a child taught to name her own mind in Praxis's Greek has already been told what she is permitted to think about it. To write *the power* instead of *Noesis* is that whole argument in two syllables, by someone who helped build it.

But he does not refuse the institution's words everywhere, and **where he keeps them tells you as much as where he drops them.** When he quotes the MetaConcert, or names its machinery, the MetaConcert speaks its own tongue: *Onset*, the *Noetic subject*, *protocol*, *Correction*, *Sterilization*. The word is a shibboleth — put *the power* in a Praxis mouth and you have written an officer who does not exist. **Register is a border.** Cross into the institution's frame and the vocabulary changes at the line, every time, on purpose; the change is how you know whose eyes you are behind.

But they write **Domains**. They write **Barriers**, and **Detection**, and **Intrusion**, because there are no other words and they know there are no other words. Heterodox's own honest researchers said it first: *you cannot draw a new map without using the cartographic tools the old map gave you.*

So watch where **Noesis** appears anyway. It is not carelessness — it is the handful of places where nothing plainer will carry the weight, and the writer reaches back into Praxis's vocabulary because the argument requires it and no substitute exists. Each one is a small defeat, and they let you see it.

**The terminology of this book is not neutral, not consistent, and not an accident.** It is someone refusing the words they can refuse and surrendering, sentence by sentence, on the words they cannot.

## The second voice

Where the text knows things no Heterodox researcher could — the MetaCouncil in private session, the interior of a psidolon, what the Sealed College keeps — it is not them. Those passages have no author. Take them as the book's other voice: the one that owes you the truth instead of a perspective.

The seam between the two is itself informative. **Everything the first voice cannot tell you is something the institution successfully kept.**

## How to write in this world

**State the atrocity plainly and let it land.** The prose does not editorialize about how sad this is. It says what happens, in the flattest available sentence, and trusts you. *They executed him anyway.* The horror is structural; it does not need help.

**Let the euphemisms indict themselves.** The setting's signature move is institutional language doing violence. *Correction. Sterilization. Cognitive Reconstruction. The subject presented at twelve.* Quote them without comment. A reader who notices that *Sterilization* is the janitorial word for killing someone the institution broke does not need it explained.

**Nobody is stupid and nobody is cartoonish.** Every Inquisitor has thought about the trigger more than you have. The Integrators are genuinely kind people doing the worst thing in the book, and they are kind while doing it, and that is the point. If a faction reads as villains who enjoy it, it is wrong.

**Specificity over summary.** Not "the Panopticon monitors the Gifted" but the analyst who accesses a child's node and feels the quiet hum of fourteen hundred minds she is responsible for watching, and goes home to groceries and ordinary anxiety.

**Clinical register for the worst things, human register for small ones.** The chart says *date of onset: age 12*. The mother says *my daughter is Gifted*. Both sentences are about the same night. Put them near each other and do not comment.

**Outsiders describe psionics in their own frame — never in psion jargon.** A Kindred says *mind control*, or reaches for *Dominate*; a mage for the *Mind sphere*; a cop for *he made me do it*; a mortal for *hypnosis* or *some Jedi thing*. Which frame a character grabs tells you what they are, so let them grab it — the gap between how a psion names a thing and how an outsider names it is characterization you get for free. A vampire who says *Puppeteer* is a mistake; she has no reason to know the word. *(The full four-layer register — Praxis, Projects, psion-colloquial, outsider — is in **Terminology and Register**.)*

**Short declaratives carry the weight.** *She doesn't fray.* *The power does not survive.* *It isn't that.* *They executed him anyway.* When the sentence matters most, make it shortest.

**Let people be right about facts and wrong about what follows.** That is the whole moral engine. The MetaConcert is right that psidolons are real. Praxis is right that the taxonomy works. The Inquisition is right that a Snap in a crowded room is a catastrophe. None of that makes what they do to a twelve-year-old anything other than what it is.

**The horror is in the choosing.** *I can fix this, but it will cost me a piece of myself I can't get back.* Any scene that contains that sentence is a Psion scene.

## What to avoid

- **Boosterism.** No "thrilling adventures await." This book does not sell itself.
- **Grimdark for its own sake.** The cruelty is always institutional and always reasoned. Nobody is cruel for fun; the ones who are, are broken, and the text notices.
- **Making the Gifted powerful.** They are squishy. They win by being already gone.
- **Explaining the metaphor.** If you have written the mushroom, do not then write *and this shows that taxonomies are limited.*
- **Splat terminology from other games.** *Gift*, *manifest*, *Awakening*, *Quiet*, *vulgar/subtle*, *Aptitude* — all belong to someone else. See **Terminology and Register**.
- **Resolving the ambiguity.** The setting is built out of arguments that do not settle. Do not settle them.

## The tone in one line

> *This is a game that describes metaphysical horror in medical terminology and calls execution mercy.*
