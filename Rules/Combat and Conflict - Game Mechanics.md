# Combat and Conflict: Game Mechanics

**Psionics Under Fire**

*Psion: The Fraying uses standard Classic World of Darkness combat. This document does not reprint it — it adds the psionic layer: how techniques work as combat actions, how Kinesis fights, how mental and astral conflict resolve, and how the [Fraying](The%20Fraying%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md) turns every protracted fight into a trap. Use your core Storyteller rules for the rest. Stat blocks for techniques are in [The Seven Domains — Game Mechanics](The%20Seven%20Domains%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md).*

---

## Standard Combat, in One Breath

For orientation only:

- **Initiative:** Dexterity + Wits + 1d10, highest first.
- **One action per turn** (more by splitting a pool; declare in reverse initiative order, resolve in order).
- **Attack:** relevant pool (Dex + Brawl/Melee/Firearms) vs difficulty 6; the target may **Dodge** (Dex + Athletics) to cancel successes.
- **Damage:** weapon or Strength + extra successes, **soaked** by Stamina (+armor) — bashing soakable by all, lethal soakable only with armor or supernatural toughness.
- **Health penalties** apply to all pools as wounds mount.

Everything below sits on top of this.

## Psionics as Combat Actions

**Using a technique costs your action**, exactly like swinging a weapon. Roll the technique's pool (see the Domains mechanics); resolve on your initiative.

- **Barriers defends for free.** Because Barriers is a passive firewall, you resist incoming Intrusion, Control, and Memory **reflexively** — even mid-swordfight, even when you've already acted. *Raising* Thoughtshield, springing **Feedback**, or any active Barriers technique still costs an action.
- **Splitting actions** (attack *and* use a technique in one turn) follows the normal dice-splitting rules, but psionic pools are small — splitting a 6-dice Intrusion pool usually fails. Most Gifted do one thing per turn and do it carefully.
- **Concentration.** Sustained techniques — Puppeteer, Kinetic Barrier, Aegis, or simply *holding Intrusion access* — occupy your focus. If you take a Health level of damage while sustaining, roll **Wits + the relevant Domain (diff 6)** or the effect **drops**. Establishing Intrusion access (a Dig) is one action; everything you do "inside" a mind afterward requires you keep that concentration alive.

## The Gifted Edge: Speed, Forewarning, and Tradecraft

A Gifted is, in raw durability, **the squishiest thing in the World of Darkness** — no regeneration, no supernatural soak, no Beast to shrug off a wound, just seven Health levels and a mortal's Stamina against things that fold cars and outrun headlights. In a standup fight with a Garou or an angry elder, a Gifted loses, and the system means them to. They are alive for two reasons only: the MAD doctrine stops the factions from exterminating them wholesale, and one-on-one, **they see it coming and are gone before it lands.** Their edge is never force. It is *tempo* and *information* — the mind arriving before the body. These rules give that edge teeth.

**Instant by nature.** A technique needs no ritual, no focus, no gathering of power, no incantation, no eye contact — it is a single mental act resolved the instant it is willed. This is the Gifted's structural advantage over every other tradition: where the mage is still assembling a rote and the blood-sorcerer still drawing the ritual, the Gifted has already acted. (It is also exactly why the Panopticon is a nightmare — the same instant, tell-less mental act that wins a fight is the one that reads your mind across a room with no warning at all.)

**Forewarning.** While you actively run a Detection technique (Thoughtsense, Empathic Sense, or Psionic Sense), you feel minds and intent before bodies move:
- You **cannot be ambushed or surprised.** There is no drop to get on a Gifted who is listening.
- **Add your Detection rating to initiative.** You do not act fast — you act *early*, already moving before the threat revealed itself.

**Read-and-Run.** A technique is mental; movement is physical; the two do not compete for the same channel. You may take a **full move — including an all-out flee — in the same turn as a psionic technique, with no multiple-action penalty.** Sense the room and be out the back door. Override the guard and walk past as he opens it. Wipe the witness and leave in the same breath. This is the single most characteristic thing a Gifted does in a fight, which is to say: *leave it.*

**The real weapon is the operation, not the blow.** A Gifted's power fantasy is not winning the fight — it is never being in one. The signature loop is **Detection** to move through a place unseen, **Intrusion** to take what you came for, and **Memory** to erase that you were ever there. Run well, a Gifted's greatest victories leave no body, no alarm, and no one who remembers their face. The dirty Domains — Kinesis, Control — are what you reach for once the operation has already failed. *(See the dirty-vs-clean hierarchy in [The Seven Domains](The%20Seven%20Domains%20-%20Psionic%20Techniques.md).)*

**"Was there a way to solve this without Kinesis?"** is the question a mentor asks after almost every op, and the honest answer is almost always yes. It isn't only Strain economy, though Kinesis is the most expensive Domain in the book — it's that Kinesis is the one Domain where a Gifted competes on the same terrain as everyone else: raw force. A telekinetic shove loses to a Garou in Crinos. A hurled car loses to Kindred-fueled strength. Fighting there means picking the one fight a Gifted cannot win on the merits. The mental Domains are the terrain no other splat can follow onto. Kinesis is the terrain where they're already waiting for you.

### Worked Example: The Locked Door

A locked door is the purest test of how a Gifted thinks, because every faction in the World of Darkness has an answer to it — and the Gifted has the *most* answers, which is exactly why the crude one is almost never right.

A Kindred Dominates the guard, or breaks the door, or is simply important enough to be waved through. A Garou becomes Crinos and the door stops being a door. A mage turns it to cottage cheese, or steps around the question entirely with Correspondence. Each solves it in one move, in its own idiom.

A Gifted has a whole *ladder* of solutions, and the ladder is sorted by what it costs the tether — the only currency that finally matters:

- **Kinesis, brute.** Tear the door off its hinges. It works; it is loud; it frays you hard; and now there is a wrecked door for someone to find and explain.
- **Kinesis, clever.** Work the lock's pins telekinetically instead of fighting the door — the same result for a fraction of the Strain, but still a door that opened for no one and, often, a guard who heard it.
- **Puppeteer the guard** into turning his own key. Total and certain — and the single worst rung on the ladder: maximum Strain, and a guard left standing there *knowing* his hands moved without him. The angriest possible witness, plus a Memory job to clean up after.
- **Compulsion or Suggestion.** *"You need to check something in that room."* Cheaper, quieter, far less dangerous to you and to him — but it bargains with his will, and a guard with real reason to keep that door shut can refuse it.
- **The clean way — the one the mentor is hoping you'll find.** A whisper of **Mirage**: the guard hears a scuff at the door, a handle tried. He opens it himself, to look. A second Mirage slides you out of his notice as you step past him, through the door he just opened. He finds an empty hall, frowns, decides it was the wind, and closes it behind you. Almost no Strain. No wrecked door. No angry witness. No memory to wipe — because, as far as anyone will ever be able to tell, nothing happened.

That last line is the whole game. The brute breaks the door; the artist makes the guard open it and forget he did. **Puppeteer is on the ladder — it is simply near the bottom of it, and the Gifted who reaches for it first is the one who never learned to climb.**

## The Fraying in a Fight

This is the most important thing on the page. **Combat is where Gifted snap.**

- **Stage penalties hit every roll** — including your attacks, dodges, and soak — and psionic pools take the Stage-2 penalty on top. A frayed Gifted is worse at *everything*, right when it matters.
- **Combat stacks Strain modifiers viciously:** Panicked (+2), Desperate (+1), sustained Kinesis (+1/turn), forcing through resistance (+1). A four-turn psionic firefight can pour a dozen Strain into a tether that started the scene healthy.
- **At Stage 4, any psionic use is a Snap Check** — in the middle of the fight, with Tether + Stamina vs 8 and no Willpower to lean on. The most dangerous moment in Psion is the Gifted who reaches for power one more time to survive a fight they're already losing.

The system's lesson, learned at the table: the smart Gifted ends fights fast or avoids them entirely. A long battle is a losing one even when you win it.

## Kinesis in Combat

The only Domain built to trade blows — and the one that burns tether fastest.

**Telekinetic Strike** (Telekinesis used offensively)
- **Attack:** Wits + Kinesis vs diff 6, line of sight. Target may **Dodge** (Dex + Athletics) or **Deflect**.
- **Damage:** **(Kinesis rating) dice + extra successes.** Blunt shoves and slams are **bashing**; hurled hard objects, crushing force, or edged debris are **lethal**. Soaked by Stamina (+armor, +Kinetic Barrier).
- **Mass by rating** (from the fiction): ● small objects, ●● a person or furniture, ●●● a car door's worth of debris (genuinely deadly), ●●●● structural, ●●●●● devastating.
- **Strain 4** (+1 per turn if sustained as a continuous press rather than discrete strikes).

**Kinetic Barrier** (soak)
- Soaks **(Kinesis) dice** against physical attacks — and crucially, **including firearms.** A mortal cannot normally soak a bullet's lethal damage; a Kinetic Barrier is one of the few things that can. This makes a Kinesis user briefly bullet-resistant — at **Strain 4 +1 per turn maintained**, a cost that ends the trick quickly.

**Deflection** (reflexive)
- Wits + Kinesis as a reflexive action against one incoming attack (including a bullet); successes subtract from the attack's successes. **Strain 2.** Cheaper than a full Barrier, but one threat at a time.

## Mental and Astral Conflict

**Mental contests** resolve as the Domains mechanics describe: attacker rolls the technique, defender reflexively rolls **Wits + Barriers**, net successes decide. In combat:

- **Intruding** is an action; the defender's resistance is free (passive Barriers). **Mind Barbs** and **Feedback** punish the attacker reflexively if set or available.
- **Puppeteer** in a fight: once you hold access, each turn you spend an action concentrating to direct the puppet (+1 Strain/turn). The overridden character acts on their own initiative but under your command; break concentration and they're free — and they remember everything.
- **Psionic Scream** is an action that hits **everyone in range, ally and enemy** — each resists with **Willpower**; failures lose their next action to disorientation. The genuine panic button.

**Projection in combat is reckless.** While you are projected via Astral Step, your body lies inert and undefended.
- An attacker who strikes your **body** while you're projected hits an unresisting target.
- **Taking damage to the body while projected forces an immediate, violent return:** roll **Tether + Stamina (diff 6)** or be disoriented (lose your next action) and take **+2 Strain** from the wrench back into flesh.
- **Lethal or aggravated damage to the body while projected forces a full Snap Check** — the tether can tear with you on the wrong side of it. This is exactly why Operatives scout with Remote Viewing and almost never Astral Step into danger.

## The Fugue: Mental Duels

*Advanced material. [The Fugue](The%20Seven%20Domains%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md) (Intrusion ●●●●●) is a rare capstone technique — most tables will never see it used. This section is for the ones that do.*

**Whose invention this is.** The Fugue is the most purely *Praxis* thing in the entire catalogue, and the one place the counter-language has no rival word to offer — for a reason worth understanding before the rules. Two strong minds could always, in principle, go to war directly, rather than one simply reading a mind that cannot fight back. But before Praxis, that clash had no *form*. It happened rarely, between masters, and it happened differently every single time — a drowning to one, a chess game to another, a shared and terrible dream to a third — unrepeatable, untransmissible, survived rather than learned. It had no name because it had no shape. A Gifted either had the instinct for mind-war or discovered, once and fatally, that they didn't.

Praxis did to it what Praxis does to everything: studied enough clashes to find the invariant structure under the idiosyncrasy, and codified it — **not to make it fair, but to make it teachable.** The distinction is the whole point. When the old world formalized the pistol duel — ten paces, a second, the challenged chooses the weapon — the codes were about honor and equity. The Fugue's rules are about neither. They are a *curriculum.* Praxis took an act of raw genius that a handful of Gifted stumbled into per century and turned it into something a talented student could be walked through: how you enter, how the space renders, what the contest actually contests, how you leave with your self intact. They named it for the musical form — independent voices entering and pursuing one another under strict counterpoint — and were quietly pleased that the word also names a state of dissociation.

This is why everyone says *Fugue* — Inquisitor and Conductor and off-lattice hermit alike — and why [Project Heterodox](../Projects/Project%20Heterodox.md), which has a defiant word for nearly everything, has none for this. There was nothing here to rename. Praxis did not launder an honest thing; it *authored* the thing whole, and even the resistance learned mind-war in the shape Praxis cut for it. It is the deepest reach of the taxonomy anywhere in the setting: not merely that Praxis named your power, but that when you fight for your life mind-to-mind — at the rawest, most personal extremity the Gifted condition allows — you fight inside a form a research institution designed to be *legible.* There is no longer a way to have that fight that is not, in its bones, a Praxis lesson.

**What it is not.** The Fugue is not a plane, a dimension, or anywhere that exists outside the participants' own skulls. Nothing is "cast," nothing is teleported, and there is no astral battlefield out there to visit. Two brains stay exactly where they started for the entire duration. What actually happens is smaller and stranger: two minds are exchanging concept directly, with no language and no senses mediating between them, and a mind cannot process an exchange that unmediated as anything but *something happening somewhere* — the same way a dream feels unmistakably like a place while the body never leaves the bed. So each mind renders a somewhere to make the exchange legible: an endless library, a battlefield, a storm at sea, a chessboard. This is dream-logic, not travel. The environment is metaphor. The struggle is not. *("Fugue," the name, is meant in the clinical sense — a dissociative state, not a destination — matching the register of Cognitive Command and Cognitive Reconstruction elsewhere in the MetaConcert's vocabulary.)*

**And that answers what magic is acting on: nothing.** Ask it plainly — if a Mage throws a fireball in here, what's actually burning? There's no oxygen, no fuel, no matter for Forces to touch, the identical absence that leaves Kinesis holding air. The fireball can certainly *appear* — the Fugue renders belief and expectation for everyone inside it, Gifted or not, so a Mage's fire is exactly as vivid as anything else in the construct — but it does no harm, because there was never anything flammable to begin with. This is the dream where a stranger stabs you with a knife that turns out to be made of celery: the blade lands, the moment is real and can be genuinely terrifying, and you wake unmarked, because it was never steel. It only looked like steel to a mind that had no other way to represent a threat. A Sphere effect inside the Fugue is exactly that convincing and exactly that harmless, for exactly that reason.

**Entering.** The Fugue can only be reached from an already-held Intrusion access — breach the mind first (Dig or stronger) and keep concentrating. Pulling the target the rest of the way in is a second, separate roll: **Wits + Intrusion**, resisted by **Willpower + Barriers** — a non-Gifted target has no Barriers to add, so they resist on **Willpower alone**, which for an ancient Kindred, a Garou elder, or a Mind-sphere master Mage can already be a formidable pool without any psionic trait helping it. There is no partial result. Succeed and both of you are in. Fail and the target isn't dragged anywhere at all — a sufficiently defended mind refuses the Fugue outright, the way a locked door doesn't open a little.

**Hard, not impossible.** This is meant to scale, not wall off. A legendary-willed being — the vampire who has survived a millennium, the archmage whose whole practice is the mind, a Garou elder even in a calm form — should be a genuine long shot for anything but a master psion, and a coin-flip at best for an ordinary one. That's the point: nobody but a truly formidable Gifted should expect to win this contest against the strongest minds in the setting, which is exactly what makes succeeding against one worth a story. The one true hard wall is a Garou in **Crinos or frenzy** (existing rule: Intrusion auto-fails against a mind that isn't reasoning) — everything else, however difficult, stays a roll. Never a flat no.

**Whose ground this is.** Inside the Fugue, a Gifted rolls Domains exactly as everywhere else. This is not a bonus for being Gifted — it's the absence of the penalty everyone else pays. A non-Gifted mind (Kindred, Garou, Mage, an unusually strong-willed mundane) is not helpless here — the Fugue answers to force of self, not psionic bloodline alone — but they are working in translation: substitute the Storyteller's best analog for a Domain roll (Willpower for a Kindred or Garou, Arete for a Mage) at **+2 difficulty**. The penalty isn't weakness. It's unfamiliarity with a landscape whose grammar the Gifted has spent a lifetime learning to read — the same relationship a Garou has to the Umbra, or a Mage has to someone else's dream: capable of visiting, never quite at home. A Technocrat's science and a Hermetic's Words both assume a reality, physical or belief-mediated, that the Fugue simply isn't. Their paradigm has nothing to grip.

**This is meant to be decisive, not marginal.** The difficulty penalty is the smaller half of the asymmetry — the larger half is that a non-Gifted mind is substituting *one* broad trait for every role the duel calls for: the same Willpower or Arete standing in for the walls, the navigation, the tell-reading, and holding identity together, all at once. A Gifted brings six specialized tools to the same fight — Barriers for the walls, Detection for the tells, Projection for staying whole, Memory for the terrain itself. Comparing a specialist's full kit to a generalist's one borrowed wrench, at a penalty on top, is where the real gap lives; the dice modifier is almost an afterthought. Inside the Fugue you are no longer comparing Sphere ratings, Discipline levels, or Gifts to Domain ratings — you're comparing identity, will, and a lifetime of trained mental architecture to a paradigm that has never had to think about any of it. **Treat a challenge to fight this way exactly as you'd treat a challenge to fistfight a Crinos Garou or trade rituals with a master Hermetic: legal, rarely wise, and the table should feel the difference the moment it's proposed.**

**Kinesis does not function here.** There is no physical reality inside the Fugue to move, strike, or shield with. A Kinesis specialist who has never needed anything else finds themselves, for the first time, holding nothing at all.

**Mind-affecting Sphere effects fail for a second, deeper reason.** Forces has nothing to burn; fair enough. But surely Mind has *something* to work with — the whole exchange is already happening inside minds. It doesn't, and the reason cuts deeper than "no substrate": a spell is an act performed *from outside* the system it changes, an Avatar imposing a pattern-shift on a separate target through belief, and that presupposes a caster standing apart from what's being edited. Inside the Fugue there is no apart. The caster is a participant in the same shared rendering as the target, the same way jacking into a construct is something you do *before* you're inside it, never from within. **Sphere effects — Arete + Sphere rolls, rotes, anything that requires invoking a Sphere at all — simply do not resolve inside the Fugue,** Mind included. What a Mage has left is the substitute already described above: Arete standing in for a Domain roll. That is not a weaker spell. It is the absence of a spell, replaced by nothing but the same bare force of self everyone else in the Fugue is reduced to.

**What a fight looks like.** There are no hit points in the Fugue. Win and loss are read off what actually happened to the two minds inside it:
- Finding the memory the other side tried to hide (**Detection** vs. **Barriers**/**Memory**, extended contest).
- Holding your own identity coherent while the other side's construct starts to fracture (**Projection** vs. **Willpower**, resisted, ongoing).
- Recognizing a planted false memory for what it is before you act on it (**Detection** or **Wits** vs. the planter's **Memory**).
- Reaching the center of the other mind's construct — the deepest-held true self — before they collapse the whole architecture around you (**Intrusion** vs. **Barriers**, race-to-successes).
- Turning the other mind's own subconscious against its own construct, so it rejects what they built rather than defends it (**Control**, resisted by **Willpower**, at a steep difficulty premium — this is asking someone's mind to betray them).

Use whichever Domain fits the moment. **Barriers** is fortification — walls, labyrinths, impossible geometry defending the core self. **Intrusion** is navigation and infiltration — finding the door, reading the symbols. **Memory** *rewrites the landscape itself*, since the terrain is built from the same substance Memory edits. **Control** is a contest over whose will the shared space obeys. **Detection** finds the hidden path, the lie, the trap, the emotional current under the metaphor. **Projection** is what keeps you *you* while immersed in someone else's translation of two minds meeting.

**The cost — sustainable, not prohibitive.** Entering costs the initiator Strain 3, +1 per turn sustained — the same bracket as Puppeteer or Deep Dive, not a special tax for being powerful. A Gifted opponent pays their own Strain normally for whatever Domains they use inside; a non-Gifted opponent spends their own native resource (Willpower, Rage, Blood, Quintessence — ST's call) instead. This is deliberate: the Fugue is a Gifted's signature arena, not a last resort, and a splat's defining fantasy should be something the player reaches for, not something they learn to dread using. A Garou doesn't pay permanent Gnosis to enter Crinos; a psion shouldn't bleed themselves toward Untethering just to fight on their own ground. Ordinary Fraying rules apply and no more — push hard, sustain it too long, and Strain overflows into Fray exactly as it would in any other fight. Losing a Fugue is a real loss (the information, the advantage, sometimes a wound to your sense of self), but it is not automatically catastrophic.

**Total Immersion — where the real danger lives.** The duel itself is sustainable. *Rewriting* someone inside it is not. Attempting something beyond a contest — permanently erasing or reconstructing a mind's foundations rather than out-maneuvering it, or extending a single Fugue to hold more than one other mind at once — is the psionic equivalent of a Mage attempting world-shaking magic, and should cost like it: every such escalation adds Strain heavily (+3 or more per additional target or per attempt to make a change permanent rather than contested) and guarantees Fray, not mere Strain, on any failure. This is the tier that deserves the dread — awake brain surgery, reserved for the rare moment a Gifted decides winning the contest isn't enough and tries to rewrite the terms of personhood itself. The ordinary duel that gets you there should never carry that fear. Only the reach past it should.

**The individual answer, not just the institutional one.** The MAD doctrine is how the MetaConcert survives contact with the rest of the World of Darkness. The Fugue is how a single Gifted does. Every splat needs a way for one of its own, alone, with no institution standing behind them, to actually *win* a fight rather than merely survive it — even Hunters, bare of any power but conviction, have theirs. This is the psion's.

## Fighting the Supernatural

Dice modifiers for the cross-splat interactions described in the [fiction](The%20Seven%20Domains%20-%20Psionic%20Techniques.md):

- **Garou:** the Beast's ascendancy sets the difficulty. In **Crinos or frenzy**, Intrusion/Control/Memory **auto-fail** — Skim returns only rage, there is no will to seize and no stable mind to edit. In the **calm forms (Homid/Lupus)** they work at **+1 difficulty** — a Garou is never merely human. **Spiritually attuned Garou** (theurges, ritualists, elders) resist as if they had **Barriers ●●●**. Detection works in any form (it just reports *very angry*); Kinesis is unaffected. Their regeneration and soak make a standup fight with a Gifted lopsided — which is the whole reason you wanted to do this from across the room and then leave.
- **Kindred:** **Barriers ●●●+ blocks vampiric Dominate** outright (●–●● can be overpowered). Memory techniques work but **+1 difficulty**, and memories bound up with the Beast may resist editing entirely. Vampires soak lethal; plan accordingly.
- **Mages:** different metaphysics, roughly even footing. In a *pure mental* contest the Gifted is the specialist — grant **−1 difficulty** to the psion. Against a Mind-Sphere mage bending reality, all bets are off.
- **Wraiths:** **Thoughtsense fails** (no active thoughts) but **Empathic Sense works** (pure emotion). Intrusion and Control are at **+2 difficulty or impossible** (no stable living mind to seize). Kinesis affects them normally.
- **Psidolons:** the Inquisition's prey, and a fight of their own — handled in the forthcoming antagonists material. In brief: they have no living body to Kinesis and no stable mind to Override, and are put down primarily through Memory work performed under fire.

## Quick Reference

- **Initiative:** Dex + Wits + 1d10. **A technique = one action.** Barriers resists reflexively; raising shields / Feedback costs an action.
- **Instant:** techniques need no ritual, focus, incantation, or eye contact — they resolve the instant willed.
- **Forewarning:** while running active Detection → **cannot be surprised**, and **add Detection rating to initiative.**
- **Read-and-Run:** pair a psionic technique with a full move (including an all-out flee) in the same turn — **no multiple-action penalty.**
- **Concentration:** take damage while sustaining → Wits + Domain (diff 6) or it drops.
- **TK Strike:** Wits + Kinesis; damage **(Kinesis) dice + successes**, bashing or lethal; Strain 4 (+1/turn sustained).
- **Kinetic Barrier:** soaks **(Kinesis) dice**, *including firearms*; Strain 4 (+1/turn).
- **Deflection:** reflexive, subtract successes from one attack; Strain 2.
- **Stage penalties apply to all rolls.** Stage 4 + any psionic use = **Snap Check** (Tether + Stamina vs 8, no Willpower).
- **Body hit while projected:** Tether + Stamina (diff 6) or +2 Strain & lose next action; **lethal hit = Snap Check.**
- **Cross-splat:** Garou — war-forms/frenzy auto-fail mental, calm forms +1 diff, theurges resist as Barriers ●●● · Kindred Barriers ●●●+ blocks Dominate, Memory +1 · Mages −1 to psion in pure mental · Wraiths no Thoughtsense, mental +2.
