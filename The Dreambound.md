# The Dreambound

**The Changeling-Gifted**

*This document was split out of [Cross-Splat Edge Cases](Cross-Splat%20Edge%20Cases.md) once the Dreambound outgrew being an edge case — they now have their own chargen support ([Character Creation — Building a Gifted](Character%20Creation%20-%20Building%20a%20Gifted.md), "Building a Dreambound") and their own progression, the Dreambound Tropes tree, described there. This document is the lore and identity: what they are, why they exist, and what it costs them.*

---

A changeling-Gifted hybrid is far more viable than most cross-splat collisions in this setting — a Garou-Gifted birth, for instance, needs a once-in-a-thousand roll of the dice, and then a second lottery on top of it. This one needs no such luck, for a simple reason: a changeling is not undead, not a spirit, not a thing the tether struggles to read. A changeling is a *living mortal* — one who ages, bleeds, and dies — who also carries a reincarnated fae soul, bound to the mortal self by the Changeling Way. They are alive in every sense the tether requires, and they do not have *less* soul than a baseline human. They have **more:** a doubled soul, mortal and fae, housed in one body. A changeling has a tether like anyone living, and can be Gifted like anyone living. Nothing about being fae-souled forecloses the power.

**A word for them, from the people who can bear to look.** The MetaConcert has no real name for this — only a clinical drawer to file it in: an *anomaly,* an incompatible specimen, a unit that will not integrate. The name that stuck came from elsewhere; it is the Projects' word, and the changelings' word, and it is **the Dreambound** — bound by a tether like any Gifted, and bound to the Dreaming by the soul they carry, and never entirely sure which of the two bindings is the truer one. This document uses *Dreambound* and *Changeling-Gifted* interchangeably: the first is what they are called by those who love them, the second is the plain description of what they are.

**A note on the word *hybrid.*** It is convenient and slightly wrong. A changeling is not a crossbreed; it is a living human being who carries, fused into their own, the reincarnated soul of an ancient fae — which is why two perfectly ordinary mortals can bear a changeling child. By that logic a Dreambound is not *half* of anything. There is nothing partial about their fae soul and nothing partial about their tether: they are a full changeling and a full Gifted at once, two complete natures in one person. *Dual-souled* is nearer the truth than *hybrid,* and the latter is kept here only because it is the word people reach for. The one genuinely hybrid-like thing about them is the ordinary cost of trying to be two things at once — most divide their attention and end up a little worse at each than a single-souled specialist would be. But that is a matter of practice, not of nature; no metaphysical ceiling forces it, and the rare Dreambound who lives long enough, or burns bright enough, to raise *both* natures to their height is precisely the apex adept described later in this entry. Not half-and-half. Wholly both.

This is also the cleanest contrast the setting can draw with [Mage](Cross-Splat%20Edge%20Cases.md#a-gifted-who-awakens). A Gifted cannot Awaken not because the tether and the Avatar are fighting over the same seat, but because there's no seat left empty for an Avatar to fill: the Avatar is an interface a *weaker* tether grows to bridge the gap between a mind and a reality it can't reach unassisted, and a Gifted's tether already reaches — nothing to bridge, nothing for the Avatar to attach to. The Dreaming makes no such claim on that seat at all. It does not argue about the nature of consciousness or offer another way to touch reality; it asserts that dreams, stories, and wonder are *real* and have a substance, and that substance is **Glamour.** That is orthogonal to the tether, not a rival solution to the same problem. The two natures do not compete for anything. They simply share a skull — which is exactly why they can coexist where the Avatar cannot even form.

**Two routes in, and only one is a birth.** This is the structural break from [Eve](Eve.md). The Garou are *born;* a Garou-Gifted hybrid requires a once-in-a-thousand birth and then a second lottery atop it. But a changeling is not, at root, a bloodline — it is a *fae soul incarnating into a living body,* the Chrysalis, and that can happen to a person already grown. So the hybrid comes two ways:

- **Heritable, mild.** A **kinain** — fae-blooded, descended from a changeling/mortal union, carrying a trace of the Dreaming — who is also born Gifted. (A sidhe-descended kinain is the closest thing the World of Darkness has to a "half-Sidhe.") A touch of fae laid over the power; rare, like any double inheritance, and the gentler of the two.
- **Acquired, dramatic.** A fae soul reincarnates — Chrysalises — into someone who is *already living and already tethered:* a Gifted. This needs no birth lottery at all. It needs only a fae soul to find a Gifted host. And it does, more readily than chance would predict, because **the reaching, vivid mind of a Gifted is precisely the kind of brightness a wandering fae soul is drawn to.** A consciousness built to project, to reach outward, to impose will on the world — that mind *dreams loud.* Some corner of the Dreaming half-knows the Gifted exist, the way a moth knows a window. The result is that Changeling-Gifted are almost certainly **more numerous than Eve-style hybrids** — not common, but not singular. The cost of that frequency is instability: two souls drawn together by a brightness neither fully governs do not always settle quietly. They are **more common *and* stranger.**

**Stranger, never stronger — and the difference is in *how* they use the power, not how much.** Eve's Rage is braided into the weave of her tether itself, and what it made carries a psionic load without fraying; she is the one being in the setting whose tether is made of something that does not fray. Not a discount — a different material. The Changeling-Gifted is nothing like that. When they reach for the power **the ordinary, instrumental way** — clean remote-viewing, precise Intrusion, the mind imposing itself on reality like a tool — they fray exactly as any Gifted does, *and worse,* because that instrumental mode is itself a Banal act: the cool, will-driven, reality-as-machine use of the mind abrades the fae soul even as the tether-work frays the tether. Done the textbook way, the power costs them *twice,* and the orthodox way is therefore their **worst** mode, not their best. They still fray; over a life the ledger comes due exactly as it does for everyone but Eve. There is no general buffer and no shifting map that lets them off. What they have instead is a *second road.*

**The fae-native road bypasses the tether entirely — which is the whole point, and the reason it does not fray.** Take their version of Projection. An ordinary Gifted projecting is doing remote viewing: a psionic security camera trained on a distant room, cool and exact — *report what you see.* A Dreambound does something the Domain framework cannot cleanly name, because it is neither projection nor [Dreamwalk](The%20Seven%20Domains%20-%20Psionic%20Techniques.md) and somehow both. They reach a *real* place — a room with no one sleeping in it, no dream and no Dreaming present — so in *where it goes* the act behaves like projection. But they do not *view* that room; they *go* to it, the way one goes somewhere in a dream, and what they bring back is dreamlike — distorted, charged, symbol-heavy, the place rendered as a self experienced it rather than as a lens recorded it. It is dreamwalking to a place that isn't dreaming. And it **does not run through the tether at all** — it runs through the fae soul, for which this kind of participatory dream-reaching is simply *natural* — so it costs no Strain and frays nothing. This is the exact sense in which they are *stranger, not stronger:* not a tether that cannot fray (that is Eve, and Eve alone), but a fray-free *back door* around a tether that frays exactly like anyone else's — trading a clean instrument for dream-logic. The intelligence it yields is unreliable, uncanny, and hard to act on — a dream of the truth instead of a photograph of it — and a disciplined Dreambound learns to read their own dreams the way an analyst reads a bad satellite feed.

**The same back door opens inward — into another sleeper's dream — and here a distinction matters more than anywhere else in this entry.** A Dreambound can step into the dream of someone sleeping nearby, walk it, shape it, and speak through it — become the bird that keeps pace with a flying dreamer and lets it say something cryptic and true, so the sleeper wakes knowing a thing they cannot quite account for. All of that is real, and fray-free, and **none of it is psionic.** They can do it because they are, fully and in their own right, a changeling, and walking and working dreams is the changeling birthright, older than any tether; a pure fae does every bit of it, and usually more gracefully. The power adds *nothing* to the dreamwork itself. The only way it could intrude is as a temptation to be refused — a Dreambound *could* drop the dream-craft and simply pulse a flat psionic [Mindspeech](The%20Seven%20Domains%20-%20Psionic%20Techniques.md), and that, being the banal instrument, would fray them as orthodox psionics always does. So they don't. They let the message be the dream, exactly as their fae half always could.

The Gifted half contributes one thing, and one thing only — but it is the single thing here that no other being in the World of Darkness can do. And almost no Dreambound ever does it.

The overwhelming majority, slipping into a sleeper's dream, do precisely what any fae does — visit, talk, nudge the dream a little — on Glamour alone, with nothing psionic in it anywhere; the tether never enters the matter, and they would not know how to look for it if it did. Tether-sight, and the mending that follows, are the *apex* of what a Dreambound can become, and they demand a genuinely powerful one: the changeling-and-psionic equal of an elder vampire centuries deep — not necessarily *old,* but that order of raw strength, both natures grown great and grown into each other. It is the most intensely hybrid thing they ever do, fully fae and fully Gifted at once, each half at full stretch and neither remotely sufficient alone. Most Dreambound never come near it. The few who can are rare even among the rare.

**In a sleeper's dream, such a Dreambound can see the tether.** A pure changeling walking that same dream sees only a dream; a Dreambound sees the dream *and the tether running through it,* because they carry one themselves and know from the inside what they are looking at. Weigh what that sight costs everyone else. Most Gifted cannot perceive a tether at all: [Tether Sense](The%20Seven%20Domains%20-%20Psionic%20Techniques.md) is a **four-dot Detection discipline,** the mark of an outright specialist, and the ordinary Gifted holds one or two dots of Detection at most — nowhere near enough to see one. The rare specialist who *can* read a tether pays a steep fray price to do it, will not spend that price often, and — this is the crux — having paid it, can do *nothing* about what they have seen. They can tell whether a tether exists at all (and so whether they face a psidolon), and they can see that one is badly frayed, and there it stops. Seeing was never fixing. A Dreambound sees it for free, because they are not reading it from outside but standing in the dream where it lives — and a Dreambound, alone in all the world, can then reach out and *work* on it.

**And here is the thing the grey apparatus has buried so deep that even the Gifted forget it: the tether is not Banal.** The lattice is Banal, Praxis is Banal, the White Room is Banality given walls, and the instrumental use of the power is a Banal act — all of that is true. But the tether *itself,* stripped of the institutions that measure and bind and Correct it, is nothing of the kind. Consider what it actually *is:* the living bond tying a mind to a body to a soul, making of the three one whole person. That is not a spreadsheet and not an apparatus. It is one of the oldest fairy tales there is — *the thread that holds a self together* — and to fae sight it shines exactly as bright as such a thing should. It is why a changeling looking at Marcus Thiel sees a candle cupped against a storm and not a man performing a procedure: they are seeing the tether for what it is. The Banality was always in the cage, never in the bird. And that is the whole secret of the Dreambound's gift — a fae can perceive and tend the tether because the tether is exactly the kind of thing fae perception and dream-logic were made for: not a machine to be engineered, but a story to be continued.

And what a Dreambound mends in the Dreaming stays mended when the sleeper wakes — **the one and only exception to the iron law that nothing accelerates a tether's healing.** But hold the word *exception* lightly, because this is not a technique and never becomes one. Like everything fae, it is not science: there is no procedure, no one-two-three that mends any tether twice. It is dreams and stories, and it behaves the way dreams and stories do — differently every time, sometimes not at all, never quite on command. Even the Dreambound who managed it last night may not manage it tonight. Two hard problems stand in the way, and the first is worse than the second.

**The first is simply finding the tether, and it is the hardest thing a Dreambound ever does — harder, more often than not, than the mending itself.** One thing is certain: the tether is *in there.* Enter a sleeper's dream and something within it stands for the tether; it must, and it always does. What is *not* certain is whether the Dreambound can find which thing it is. A dream goes on forever and is crowded with everything, none of it labelled, and the tether could be wearing any face in it — a wounded bird, a broken bridge, a tree, a stranger met on a road. Nothing announces itself as *this is the self; this is the cord that holds it.* The Dreambound's doubled nature buys them an edge over blind guessing: their changeling fluency in dream-logic, laid over a Gifted's knowledge of what a tether even *is,* gives them a crude internal sense of warmer and colder, a feeling for whether they are near the thing or far from it. But it is crude — nothing like the clean homing of a tracker that pulses faster as you close on it; more a vague pull in the dark, better than nothing and a long way from certain. And when they do find it, they know it the way one knows things in dreams: the wordless certainty by which a dreamer is simply *sure* that the stranger beside them is their oldest friend, with no proof and no need of any. They just know, the instant they touch the right thing. Reaching that instant is the whole labour. The tether is always present and not always findable, and a Dreambound can walk a sleeper's dream the night through and never once lay a hand on the cord that runs under all of it.

**The second problem begins only once the first is solved:** having found the thing, they must work out how to *repair* it — to splice the rope, ease the rot from the tree, gentle the serpent — entirely within the dream's own logic, which is slow and roundabout and inefficient, and is nonetheless the only way the work can be done at all. And it is bounded past that on every side. It cannot lift a tether above the baseline it was born with — this heals damage, it does not add capacity, for the tether still *cannot be strengthened.* It cannot touch the scars: the deepest Fray and the boxes earned at the very brink are permanent here as everywhere, and a tether dreamed as a thing already half-rotted will not be made new. It cannot reach the snapped, for a torn-free mind leaves no dreamer and no dream to enter. And it is dangerous in exact proportion to the need — the dream of a critically frayed mind is a collapsing nightmare, and a Dreambound who goes in to mend a tether at the lip of the Snap is standing inside a structure as it comes down. Hardest of all, **it does not work on themselves:** one cannot be a guest in one's own dream, nor see one's own tether with the outside eye the mending needs. A Dreambound can pull half the resistance back from the brink and still fray to death alone, untended, the one gift that might have saved them forever facing the wrong way. They are healers of every tether but the one they carry. And yet — slow, partial, perilous, never quite enough — the sheer fact that it works *at all* is enormous. It is the one door in the whole edifice of the Fraying that is not locked.

**Unreliability does not shrink the size of the fact.** Imagine someone claimed to have built a machine that, switched on, sometimes — unpredictably, with no fixed odds anyone could ever name — became a genuine perpetual motion machine. Most nights it would do nothing. But even one confirmed instance would rewrite physics, because a law either holds always or it was never absolute to begin with; a perpetual motion machine that only sometimes works is not a weak version of the impossible, it is proof the impossible was never as total as claimed. That is exactly the scale of what a Dreambound's mend represents. Praxis's doctrine holds, flatly, that nothing repairs a tether. A Dreambound does not refute that doctrine reliably, or on demand, or even often — but every time it happens, the doctrine is not bent. It is broken. That it cannot be repeated to order changes nothing about how enormous the exception is.

A Dreambound who has done it will tell you, if pressed, that it was never a repair in the engineer's sense at all. A fraying tether is a story racing toward its one ending; to mend it is to lean into the page and say *not yet — there are more pages to be written.* That is nothing but the oldest fae conviction made flesh, the one a Seelie will cross blades over: that no story has only one ending until the teller says so. The grey world built an entire science to prove some endings cannot be changed. A Dreambound is the rare, quiet, dream-bound proof that it was wrong.

Which is why, to those few who know what they are, such a Dreambound — one of the rare adepts who can find and mend a tether at all — is quietly among the most precious things the resistance has. The Projects' oldest structural wound is that they cannot tend their own fraying — no lattice to watch it, no Correction they would ever accept, only rest and time and the slow private arithmetic of the Snap. A Dreambound is the answer the off-grid were never supposed to get: a way to mend a fraying mind that needs no lattice, no clinic, no surveillance, and asks nothing of the one being healed but a night's sleep. The MetaConcert *monitors* the frayed and *Corrects* the broken. A Dreambound sits down inside your dream, finds the rope wearing thin, and quietly splices it while you fly — and you wake a little further from the edge, and never quite know why.

**For a single Gifted staring at their own fraying, a Dreambound is not a plan. It is hope, in the specific sense that word takes on once every ordinary option has already failed.** There is no institutional path back from serious Fray — rest slows it, nothing reverses it, and past a certain point the MetaConcert's only offer is Correction or a bullet. Seeking out a Dreambound from that place is not choosing a cure over a lesser one. It is the same reach a person makes for an experimental therapy once standard treatment has nothing left to try — not a promise, not even good odds, just the one door that has not yet been proven shut for *this* patient, this once. Most who make that reach will not be mended. Some will. Nobody, including the Dreambound trying, can tell you in advance which night is which. That is not a flaw in the mercy. It is the exact shape hope takes when the alternative is agreeing the story is already over.

**Which is precisely what the MetaConcert would train out of them — and the horror is in that sentence.** The whole of MetaConcert pedagogy *is* the instrumental mode: precision, control, the clean read, reality handled like an apparatus. Taught to a Dreambound, that curriculum is a slow injury — it drills them in the one way of using the power that frays them hardest *and* corrodes the fae soul, while dismissing their fae-native dream-reaching as undisciplined psionics to be corrected out. And it ends, for the unlucky, in the **White Room:** the most Banal place the setting contains, *Banality made into architecture.* To an ordinary Gifted, Correction is a cell and a procedure. To a Dreambound it is a **solvent** — the room's deadening pressure severs them from the fae-native road (no dream survives that air) *and* dissolves the fae soul itself, stripping them down to the bare tether to fray like anyone, or worse, with the very part of them that could have coped erased on the way. The net of all this is centrifugal: the Changeling-Gifted is pushed *away* from the Gifted's native turf of campus and clinic and *toward* freeholds and the Dreaming, where their real gift works and the air does not hurt. They are off-grid by metaphysical gravity — the one population the Panopticon's habitat-map points in exactly the wrong direction to find. (It is also why the fae read a tether so vividly to begin with: fierce will and love look like Glamour to fae sight. The changelings who call Marcus Thiel "a candle someone cups every night" are reading a tether through the only lens they have — see [Marcus Thiel](Marcus_Thiel.md), "The Changelings." One of them is not reading a metaphor at all: the Sluagh who sent him to Vale is herself Dreambound, and has actually *seen* what she is describing.)

**And the White Room is only the sharp end of it.** The MetaConcert is, in plain metaphysical fact, one of the most Banal things the setting contains — a vast clinical apparatus of lattice and ledger and rote, built to *manage* wonder out of existence. The White Room kills the fae soul in an afternoon; the institution does it slowly, just by holding someone inside it. A Dreambound taken into MetaConcert custody, integrated, processed, and made to live the grey clinical life of a managed asset does not need to be Corrected to lose their second self. The Banality alone starves it. The fae soul dims, withdraws, and goes home to the Dreaming the way it would at the Snap — except no Snap is needed and no one is watching for it — and what is left behind is a hollowed-out *ordinary* Gifted: a person who has lost the back door, lost the part of themselves that never frayed, and lost the wonder they were carrying, and who from then on can only use the power the one fraying way. **This is the saddest thing that can happen to one of them,** sadder than the clean horror of a White Room, because it is not done with intent or even with notice. The institution does not know what it killed. It files a successfully integrated unit and moves on.

The upshot is a quiet sorting. The ones who are never caught — most of them, carried off-grid by the gravity already described — go where their nature is survivable. **The survivors surface in the Projects,** among the off-grid and the hunted, in freeholds and Railroad cells and the loose places the lattice does not reach; that is simply where a thing like this *can* live. The rare Dreambound who ends up *inside* the MetaConcert is, almost by definition, trying to get out — running on the animal certainty that this is no place for what they are, that the air itself is killing the truest thing about them. They are usually right, and usually not fast enough.

**The good news — and there is some — is that the same gravity that dooms them inside the MetaConcert saves them outside it.** The Projects are not merely *safer* than the MetaConcert; they are its opposite, and that opposition is the whole point of them. A Project bolthole is a place built by people who refused the lattice, the ledger, and the management of minds — which makes it, almost incidentally, a place where Banality does not rule. For a Dreambound that is not just refuge from the hunt; it is air they can breathe. The fae soul that goes grey and dies inside the institution *lives* here: the fae-native road stays open, the dream-reading works, the wonder they carry is treated as something real instead of a defect to be corrected out. A Dreambound in a Railroad bolthole does not merely hide. They can **thrive** — whole, in exactly the way the MetaConcert would have called impossible and then quietly erased.

**And many of them drift, in the end, to [Project Fractal](Project%20Fractal.md)** — which is the natural home, for a reason deeper than sympathy. Every other Project inherited the MetaConcert's rational-empiricist way of seeing the world even as it rejected the MetaConcert's conclusions; Railroad with its rescue metrics, Rebel with its operational doctrine, the rest with their tradecraft and their success rates — that cold instrumental framework came with them when they fled. **Fractal alone questions the framework rather than the answers,** the one Project that has tried to think outside the apparatus entirely, that embraces wonder, the Wyld, the unprovable, the perpetually open question. To a fae-souled Gifted, the MetaConcert's rationalism *is* the Banality that was killing them; Fractal is the single Project whose very epistemology makes room for what they are. There is even a quiet aptness in the match: Fractal is famously "philosophically necessary and operationally useless," full of irreplaceable truth that is maddening to act on — which is very nearly a description of a Changeling-Gifted themselves, and of the dreamlike, undeniable, unusable intelligence that only they can bring back from a room. In Fractal they do not just find shelter. They find their own shape — and freedom, which is the one thing Fractal has ever really been about.

And there is a final, almost funny mercy in the match. Fractal was the **weird** Project long before any Dreambound walked in — the one the others regard as a roomful of strange philosophers doing who-knows-what, admirable and insufferable in equal measure, forever arguing about everything. So when something genuinely impossible turns up at the door — a Gifted who dreamwalks into waking rooms and carries a fae soul the lattice cannot so much as file — Fractal does not flinch and does not study it. It shrugs, and it makes room. *We're all strange here,* they say. *And you know what? Maybe that's the whole bloody point.*

**And there is one more impossible guest Fractal now shelters — one the Dreambound turns out to be quietly, uniquely suited to tend.** A defected [Preserver](The%20Preservers%20Circuit.md) is a living archive: a wing of the MetaConcert's true memory carried behind their own Barriers, and — cut off from the Circuit's succession, with no one to offload into — a keeper who will slowly [**saturate**](Psidolons%20-%20The%20Untethered.md), the borrowed memory crowding the self until the boundary between *what I keep* and *who I am* wears through, on the long road to an archive-psidolon. A Dreambound cannot fix this the way they mend a tether; the archive is Memory-Domain work, encoded and sealed, and no dream-craft lifts a wing of it out of a crowded skull. But saturation is not, at root, a wound to the *archive.* It is a wound to the *self* — and the self is precisely what fae dream-work was made to tend. Walking the keeper's sleep, a Dreambound can find the one door that is *home* amid the endless dreamed library and hold it open: re-anchor *this is you; that is only what you keep,* and stop the tag from wearing through. It does not lighten the load. It keeps the librarian from becoming the library. And it is the hardest work they ever attempt — their single hardest task is finding the tether in a dream crowded with everything, and an archivist's dream is crowded with an *institution;* the one cord that holds the self lies buried under all of it, and some nights it cannot be found at all. So the mercy is partial, unreliable, and never a cure — a hand held against a tide. It is also the only thing standing between a foundering keeper and the worst monster the setting can make. Two of Fractal's strangest exiles, then, folded into one quiet arrangement no other place in the world could hold: a dreamwalker slipping nightly into a walking-library's overcrowded sleep, with no succession to call on and no ending to promise, to help a person remember one more night that they are a person — and to buy the time a Project has no other way to buy: time for someone in the boltholes to be found who can take up a wing of the library before the keeper goes under. It is not a cure. It is a held breath. In Fractal, that is very nearly a creed.

**The Snap, for a being with two souls, is not a doubled loss — it is a parting of ways.** When any Gifted untethers, the mind tears free soulless and the abandoned mortal soul passes to the Shadowlands; the monster and the mourner are born together. For a Changeling-Gifted the mortal half follows that same grim road — the human dies twice, as humans always do here. But the fae soul is a thing *of the Dreaming,* and the Dreaming is its home, not the Underworld. So it does not orphan to the Shadowlands and it is not consumed in the tearing. It **withdraws** — back into the Dreaming, to dream again, to incarnate again in some far season. The human is wholly lost; the fae part *goes home.* It is the one Snap in the setting that is not entirely a death, and it is somehow no less sad for that. What remains afterward can be subtly wrong: a psidolon born of a Chrysalised host sometimes carries a residue of the Dreaming in it — dream-touched, off-key, a parasite that trails fragments of stories that were never its own. Rare, and a fine seed for a Storyteller who wants a psidolon that does not behave like the others.

**And a psidolon cannot easily keep one.** A changeling is not immune to possession the way a Kindred is — there is no Beast to slam the door. But the reincarnating fae soul is *slippery,* a thing that has died and returned and knows the way out, and it does not drown as quietly as a single mortal self. A psidolon can ride a changeling, read it, hurt it — but full subsumption comes hard, and a changeling taken *completely* loses its fae soul not to consumption but to flight: the soul withdraws to the Dreaming, leaving a body that reads as empty to a Wraith as any other fully-taken host. A middle case, then — more than a mortal can resist, less than a vampire — and a genuinely dangerous host to attempt, because the thing that escapes you may remember, and the Dreaming keeps long accounts.

A closing resonance, for those who track the setting's vocabulary. The [Cult of the Unbound](The%20Cult%20of%20the%20Unbound.md) *stole* the whole shape of metamorphosis — caterpillar, Cocoon, Emergence, the promised wings — and hung it on people dissolving into nothing. The fae **Chrysalis is the genuine article** the Cult's liturgy only ever echoes without knowing it: a real awakening, a self that truly comes into something more, wearing the same caterpillar-and-wings imagery the Cult can only counterfeit. That a Changeling-Gifted carries an authentic chrysalis inside the same body the Cult would lie to is one of the quieter cruelties available to a Storyteller who wants it.

**One more thing worth saying plainly, because it keeps almost getting said wrong: none of this is psionics done differently.** There is no eighth Domain quietly hiding in what a Dreambound does, no "instinctual Memory" or "passive Projection" that just extends the seven-Domain taxonomy to cover them. What a Dreambound does in a sleeper's dream — including the one true tether-mend — is not psionics wearing a fae costume, and it is not pure changeling Dreaming either. It is a third thing, born only where both natures meet at full strength, irreducible to either parent. Sight is not a strange form of hearing, and smell is not a weak form of taste, even though a mind can blend them into one impression; a Dreambound's gift is the same kind of category error to flatten. [Project Heterodox](Project%20Heterodox.md) comes closer to seeing this than anyone else in the MetaConcert — their whole thesis is that the map is smaller than the territory — but even Heterodox's furthest reach assumes the missing territory is still *psionic* territory, just uncharted. It isn't, not here. Some of what looks like an anomaly in the Domain taxonomy was never inside that taxonomy's country to begin with.

**What "story" means here, exactly — and what it does not.** A Dreambound's fae-native work gets described, again and again, in the language of story — and it is tempting to read that as this setting's version of a Mage's Consensus, where belief is generative: shape the story well enough and it becomes true, the way shared conviction can turn an apple blue in that other metaphysics. That is not what is meant here, and the difference is load-bearing. The tether is real and objective whether or not anyone narrates it — it frays, and holds, and rarely mends, by the same hard rules the rest of the setting obeys, witnessed or not. What the fae half provides is not the power to make a thing true by telling it. It is the only vocabulary capable of *perceiving and touching* something this abstract at all. Story is the lens a fae mind sees the tether through. It is not the material the tether is made of.

That same boundary marks the edge of what a Dreambound can actually change. **Their reach stops at minds.** There is no Dreambound version of Kinesis, and there never will be one — matter has no dream to enter, no story for fae perception to get a grip on. Even their version of Projection only ever reaches a *place* to experience it strangely; it does not let them so much as move a teacup once they arrive. Whatever a Dreambound changes, they change inside someone's mind, and even there, the change has to be earned in the dream's own terms. A Dreambound cannot simply narrate *and then she believed him* into a sleeper's head the way a Gifted might force a Compulsion. The wolf in the dream has to be beaten the way that dream's own logic says wolves get beaten. A Dreambound working inside someone's sleep is not issuing a command. They are the rare guest clever enough to find the one true answer the story was already, secretly, asking for.

## What They Actually Do: The Hermeneut

This is worth naming properly, because it is the whole of what separates a Dreambound from a "dream wizard" or a "psychic," and it quietly explains everything else in this document at once: **a Dreambound's profession, metaphysically, is not power. It is interpretation.** The old word for that discipline is *hermeneutics* — reading meaning, not applying force — and *hermeneut* sits closer to what a Dreambound actually is than anything either parent splat has a word for.

Take the wolf. A therapist asks what wolves mean to the dreamer in general. A pure changeling asks what kind of dream this is. A Memory specialist asks what traumatic event generated the recurring image, so it can be found and edited. A Dreambound asks something else again: **what role is the wolf playing in the story this soul is telling right now?** Maybe it's the father. Maybe it's grief. Maybe it's the dreamer's own anger, or the one thing in the dream that is actually protecting them. The Dreambound does not know going in. Finding out is the entire gift.

**The wolf has a name too, and it isn't Trope.** A recurring symbolic element encountered inside a dream — the wolf, the locked house, the endless staircase, the child in the red coat, the empty throne — is a **Motif**. A Trope is something a Dreambound *does* (Reading Stories, Following Stories, Holding Stories); a Motif is something a Dreambound *encounters* — a recurring image the dreaming soul reaches for, again and again, to say something it cannot say plainly.

**And because interpretation is never procedure, no two Dreambound read the same Motif the same way — which is exactly the point.** Over a long enough life, a Dreambound accumulates a **Lexicon**: a personal, non-transferable body of interpretive experience about the Motifs they keep meeting. One elder Dreambound might say, after forty years, *"I've seen ravens before. They're almost never about death."* Another, just as experienced, might have met ravens that were always exactly that. Both are right, because a Lexicon is not a key that unlocks every dream — it is one interpreter's history of listening, and it means nothing outside the life that built it. The wolf a soldier dreams is not the wolf a child dreams, and the wisest thing any Dreambound's Lexicon ever teaches is not what a Motif means, but that you cannot know until you have understood the dreamer first.

**And the verb matters as much as the noun.** Praxis built the Domain taxonomy — before Praxis, nobody said "Memory Domain"; there was no such classification, only people doing things to minds. Inside the MetaConcert, the correct verb is to **invoke** a protocol: a word that assumes a catalogue, a codified thing waiting to be summoned to order, exactly the epistemology the taxonomy was built to serve. The Projects refused Praxis's name for the same act — *protocol* becomes *technique* — but notice the verb barely moved: you still broadly *use* a technique. They rejected the label, not the assumption underneath it, that these are things to be deployed. A Dreambound rejects both, at the root, because Tropes were never Praxis's to name in the first place — they don't sit inside the Domain taxonomy at all, any more than a genuinely alien organism would sit inside Linnaeus's kingdoms just because it happens to breathe. So the verb changes too. A Dreambound does not invoke a Trope, and does not quite use one either. **A Dreambound *recognizes* a Trope.** Even Following Stories or Holding Stories, which sound like acts of force, are at bottom acts of discerning correctly — recognizing which thread to follow, recognizing how a particular dream needs to be held. Nothing here is summoned. It is noticed.

This is why no Dreambound Trope is procedural, and why none of them ever could be. A Praxis field manual can read *subject exhibits recurring lupine imagery; apply Memory Technique 4* and mean something. A Dreambound manual could only ever say: *listen. Ask. Do not assume the wolf is the wolf.* That is not a gap in the documentation. It is the whole discipline, and it is exactly as maddening to a Praxis-trained mind as it sounds.

**A Dreambound does not cast. They conduct a conversation** — with a person, sometimes; with a dream, or a symbol, or a place, or, rarest of all, with a tether. Every Trope in this document is a way of listening more carefully. None of them is a way of speaking more loudly.

Which is why *tether-mending* needs restating in these terms too, because "repair" was always the wrong word, even where this document used it for convenience. A Dreambound does not fix a tether the way a Maintainer fixes a construct. **They help the tether tell a different ending than the one it was racing toward.** The rope was already a story about a self coming undone; the whole intervention is understanding that story well enough to see where it does not, in fact, have to end that way — and saying so, inside the dream, in the dream's own language.

It changes what failure means, too, and makes it sadder. An ordinary Gifted who fails a technique failed because they weren't strong enough, or the target resisted. **A Dreambound who fails failed because they misunderstood the story** — read the wolf as the father when it was the grief, or found the true answer a day too late, or never found it at all. Power was never the variable. Understanding was, and understanding can be wrong in ways raw strength cannot.

This is also the cleanest philosophical rupture the setting has from Praxis — sharper, in its way, than Heterodox's own. Praxis's founding conviction, underneath every doctrine it has ever written, is that **anything can eventually become procedure:** enough measurement, enough taxonomy, enough controlled iteration, and the ineffable becomes a technique with a Threshold rating. A Dreambound's whole existence is the quiet, permanent rebuttal to that conviction — **some things can only ever be understood. Not solved.** Not because no one has tried hard enough yet, but because understanding and solving are different verbs aimed at different kinds of things, and a tether is the second kind.

**One naming caution, because the wrong word here would undo the right idea.** It is tempting to call a Dreambound a supernatural therapist or psychiatrist, since so much of what they do resembles a session — but those are the MetaConcert's words, clinical words, Praxis's own vocabulary of diagnosis and intervention, and reaching for them here would quietly hand the Dreambound's identity back to the very epistemology they refute. The truer company they keep is older: myth-interpreters, shamans, Jungian analysts, fairy godmothers, the wise woman in the story who understands exactly why the prince keeps dreaming of ravens. None of those figures "fix" a dream. They help the dreamer understand it — and once the understanding is genuine, the dream changes on its own. That is the whole miracle this document has been describing from the start, finally given its right name.

**And this is also how a Dreambound recognizes the one thing in the world with no story to have.** Dreambound do not sort people into categories the way an institution does — they read them. Marcus is not *human, Gifted, Railroad* to a Dreambound. He is a man carrying a candle through a hurricane because he refuses to let his sister disappear. Isolde is not *elder Ventrue.* She is four centuries of loneliness wound so tightly around duty that she has forgotten where one ends and the other begins. Even a sad story like hers — violation, a cold transaction learned in self-defense, decades of deliberate distance from anything that could hurt her again — is still, unmistakably, a *story*: a throughline, a self that has stayed continuously itself across however much time it took to survive being itself.

A psidolon is not a sadder story, or a garbled one. **There is no story to garble.** Call what a Dreambound perceives "an incoherent story" and the phrase still smuggles in a story, merely a broken one — and something stranger than that is actually happening. A psidolon remembers. It plans. It speaks convincingly, sometimes perfectly. Those are all *events.* A narrative is not the same thing as a sequence of events — a narrative requires a continuing self the events are happening *to,* a protagonist the chapters are about. A psidolon has the chapters and no author. Ask a Dreambound what meeting one felt like, and the honest answer sits closer to *reading dialogue with no speaker* or *things kept happening, but nothing was about anything* than to any description of confusion or damage. There is no soul left to read in the first place — it departed for the Shadowlands in the same instant the tether snapped, born as a wraith beside whatever remained wearing the body — and what's left has no interiority at all: nothing behind the eyes reading the way anyone else's does, no one there for the events to be happening to. (A young Dreambound often assumes this is simply how everyone perceives the world, and is genuinely startled the first time someone asks *what do you mean, no story* — the way a sighted child is startled to learn not everyone can see color.)

**None of this contradicts what anyone else perceives. It is the same fact in three metaphysical languages.** A Detection specialist runs Tether Sense and eventually reports *no tether.* A Wraith looks at the same thing and says *there's nobody in there.* A Dreambound needs neither instrument nor deduction — they simply notice, the way sight notices a missing object in a room, that *there is no story.* Three splats, three native senses, one underlying absence, described three times, and none of them wrong.

**Which is what makes a Dreambound almost impossible to fool by one, and it has nothing to do with strength.** A psidolon can perfectly imitate someone it has consumed — voice, habit, memory, every mannerism intact. A Dreambound looking at it does not need to catch an inconsistency or win a contest, because the thing being imitated was never what a Dreambound is actually reading. *Marcus's story is still about Elena. This isn't about anything.* That is not Tether Sense. It is not soul-sight. It is not telepathy. It is hermeneutics, made supernatural — and because story is a Dreambound's native sense the way sight is native to a person with eyes, the absence is not a difficult deduction reached after study. It is simply obvious, the instant they look.

This ties to the same thing the rest of the setting already measures as **Coherence** (see [Antagonists — Game Mechanics](Antagonists%20-%20Game%20Mechanics.md)) — read through fae eyes instead of Praxis's instruments, and needing no Domain at all to feel. Though note the fae reading does not *track* the number: a psidolon at full Coherence and perfectly stable, wearing a life it has held for thirty years and fooling every instrument the Panopticon owns, still reads as nothing at all. A Dreambound is not detecting damage. They are noticing that there was never anybody there to damage. It isn't a Trope. It's what happens, automatically and without being asked, when a hermeneut built to read every soul-intact mind as a story meets the one thing in the world that was never a mind to begin with.

## What They Treasure: The Remembrancers

**The section above said what a Dreambound *does.* This one says what they do it *for* — and the answer is the whole of their culture, which is smaller and stranger than the Dreaming's grand ones.** Ask a changeling what stories matter and most will answer in the key the Dreaming taught them: the myths, the archetypes, the courtly epics, the great recurring shapes that outlive every teller — the Hero, the Fall, the Return of the King. A Dreambound does not deny that those are stories, or that they are beautiful. They simply, quietly, do not believe they are the *greatest* ones. To a Dreambound the greatest story in any room is never the famous one. **It is whichever life in that room no one else will remember** — and there is always one, and usually most of them.

**They are storytellers, not historians, and the distinction is a moral one.** A historian keeps the record that matters to the future: the reign, the battle, the treaty, the name worth carving. A Dreambound keeps the record that will otherwise simply *stop* — the life that ends and closes and is gone, unwritten, as if it had never happened. Kings and heroes have chroniclers already; they are in no danger of being forgotten. It is the Boggan baker who fed a lane for forty years, the Pooka who made one lonely child laugh on one particular afternoon, the household servant who left no name in any ledger, the traveller no one on the road will recall by morning — *those* are the stories a Dreambound will cross a kingdom to hear, because they are the ones about to vanish, and vanishing is the only thing a Dreambound truly counts as death.

**And a story, to them, belongs to the one who lived it.** This is the sharpest edge of their culture, and it cuts directly against the world they were born half into. The MetaConcert takes memory by force — lifts it, reads it, edits it, erases it, and never once asks. A Dreambound will not so much as *retell* a story without the leave of the person whose story it is. They will keep it, treasure it, carry it to the end of their own life — but it is held in trust, never owned, and to pass it on unpermitted is, to a Dreambound, a small and genuine theft. In a setting where minds are surveilled, extracted, and Corrected as a matter of routine, the people who will not repeat even a secret they were freely given are quietly among its few uncorrupted ethics.

**Their treasure, then, is exactly what you would expect of the fae, and nothing like it.** A fae is drawn to a hoard the way a moth is drawn to a window — but a Dreambound's vault holds no gold and no relics. If they kept a great vault it would be full of *lives:* every remembered person a jewel, catalogued not by worth but by the plain fact that someone must, and every ordinary life the rarest gem of all, because it is the kind the world keeps no copies of. It is why they are so taken with *things* — a worn copper pot, a scratched wristwatch, a doorstep hollowed by a century of feet, an old road, a castle. Not for what any of it would fetch. For what it has *witnessed.* An object is precious to a Dreambound in exact proportion to the number of lives it has quietly stood beside, and a thing that has stood beside a great many is, to them, close to holy.

**Even places, they will tell you, have stories — though not in the way it sounds.** A castle is not a mind and cannot dream; the stone holds no sentience for anyone to read. But a place is *soaked* in the lives that passed through it, saturated with the residue of everyone who worked and loved and died inside its walls, and that residue is precisely the kind of thing fae perception was built to feel. So a Dreambound does not enter a place and decide what it means. They do what they do with a dreamer, or a Motif, or a wolf: they *listen,* and ask what story the place is already, quietly, trying to tell — and take care not to answer for it. To impose a meaning on a place would be, to them, the very error the whole discipline exists to refuse.

**All of which gives them an uneasy, affectionate quarrel with the archetype** — and archetypes are the air the Dreaming breathes. A Dreambound does not reject them; you cannot be fae and reject them. But they distrust, deeply, the moment an archetype is allowed to *finish* a person instead of merely introduce one. Tell a Dreambound the man before them is the Hero and they will nod, entirely sincere, and then lean in and say the thing that defines them better than any other line could: **"Yes. He is the Hero. Now tell me about *him.*"** The archetype is the first sentence of a person. To a Dreambound it is never the last one, and a whole life lived as nothing but the Hero is, to them, a life that was never actually read.

**This quietly sorts them within their own society.** They are far better understood, as a rule, by the Eshu who wander and gather tales, the Sluagh who keep the whispers and the forgotten, the Boggans who honour the small and the homely and the honest, and the commoner kiths at large, than by the Sidhe. The nobility preserve *lineage* — the dynasty, the bloodline, the great continuous name — which is a real and beautiful keeping, and simply not the Dreambound's. Their relationship with the highborn is respectful and slightly sideways: a Dreambound admitted to a palace is every bit as likely to pass the evening with the servants, or alone in the old kitchen listening to the walls, as at the high table with the king. It is not disrespect. It is that the king already has a hundred keepers and the scullion has none, and a Dreambound's attention flows, always, toward the empty side of that ledger. (The setting already has one walking this exact path: the [Sluagh who keeps Marcus Thiel's sentences](Marcus_Thiel.md) and calls him "the little fire you cup every night" is herself Dreambound — a keeper of secrets who took, as one of the secrets she keeps, the entire story of a forgotten man hunting a stolen face. She is what this culture looks like poured into one person.)

**It is also, said in their own voice, the whole of why they can mend a tether at all.** Everything the metaphysics above frames as "helping the tether tell a different ending" is, in a Dreambound's own words, simpler and warmer than the machinery makes it sound. They do not think *I am repairing a damaged structure.* They think *this story does not have to end here.* Pressed to say it aloud to the frayed person in front of them, a Dreambound says something close to: *The tether is itself a story — perhaps the oldest one there is, the thread that holds a self together. If you still believe you have more of it left to tell, then I may be able to help you tell it.* That is not a bedside metaphor laid over a technique. For a Dreambound it is the literal description of the work, because story is the only language in which the thing is visible to them in the first place.

**And it is why they are the truest opposite the setting can set against Praxis** — not Praxis's enemy, which is a smaller thing, but its exact photographic negative. Praxis looks at a life and asks *what universal law does this reveal?* — a noble question, one that has saved lives, one that compresses a thousand people into a principle outlasting them all. A Dreambound looks at the same life and asks the opposite question, the one no procedure can ever be built to answer: **"What makes this one unlike every other?"** Praxis's whole faith is that a life can, with enough measurement, be reduced to something general and true. A Dreambound's whole existence is the standing insistence that it cannot — that the general truth, however real, is never the *whole* of even the smallest person, and that the irreducible remainder is not noise to be averaged out but the most precious thing there is. It is no accident that they drift to [Project Fractal](Project%20Fractal.md), the one corner of this world that prizes the unrepeatable over the reproducible. Fractal was always looking for them. It simply did not have the word.

**And so, the one sentence that holds all the rest.** A Dreambound does not believe stories belong to heroes. **They believe heroes are only the rare people whose stories everyone happens to remember — and that their own calling is to remember everyone else.**

*(The nearest thing the waking world has to a Dreambound manifesto was written in 1949 by a playwright defending a play about a failed salesman. "I believe that the common man is as apt a subject for tragedy in its highest sense as kings were," wrote Arthur Miller in* Tragedy and the Common Man *— and the play it defended,* Death of a Salesman, *turns on the four words that are a Dreambound's whole calling: **attention must be paid.** Not to a hero — to Willy Loman, the low man, the salesman no one would remember, "because he's a human being, and a terrible thing is happening to him." A Dreambound would have crossed a kingdom to hear him out, and would not have needed telling twice why it mattered. The same play names the thing they set themselves against, too, in six words a Praxis auditor should never be allowed to forget: a man is not a piece of fruit.)*
