# The Inquisition Circuit

**The Ones Who Pull the Trigger**

*"Mercy is a bullet before the monster is born."*

---

## Overview

**Function:** Enforcement, threat elimination, and execution of compromised Gifted

**Domain Specialization:** Kinesis

**Philosophy:** *"We do not kill them because we hate them. We kill them because we know what they become if we don't."*

**Position in Circuit founding order:** First

---

## What the Inquisition Actually Is

The Inquisition is the MetaConcert's enforcement arm. They execute critically frayed Gifted. They eliminate psidolons. They suppress Project operations. They staff White Rooms. They extract targets for Correction. They are judge, jury, and executioner, and the MetaConcert's institutional structure is designed so that no other Circuit has to share that weight.

This last point matters more than it appears. The MetaConcert could have distributed enforcement across Circuits — given Panopticon the authority to act on its own flags, or allowed Maintainers to handle threats to lattice stability directly. Instead, it concentrated the killing in one institution. The Inquisition exists so that the Integrator who teaches a child to brace does not also have to be the person who executes that child twenty years later when the tether frays past recovery. The organizational separation is a moral pressure valve. It does not make the killing less real. It makes it possible for the rest of the MetaConcert to function without confronting what is done in its name on a daily basis.

The Inquisition understands this dynamic perfectly. They are not naive about their institutional role. They know they exist partly so that other Circuits can maintain the psychological distance necessary to do gentler work. Some Inquisitors resent this. Most accept it with the grim pragmatism that characterizes everything about the Circuit. A few take a perverse pride in it — someone has to carry this, and they are strong enough to carry it.

## Founding History

The Inquisition is the oldest Circuit — older than the MetaConcert itself, older than any formal organization of the Gifted. Before Praxis, before the lattice, before the MetaCouncil, psidolons needed killing. The Inquisition was the answer. It was a necessity before it was an institution.

It coalesced out of the scattered age that produced the Interred and the rampant Untethered (see [Psidolons — *When the World Was Full of Them*](Psidolons%20-%20The%20Untethered.md)): a conviction, hardening among certain of the old solitary Gifted, that these things had been permitted to run wild for one reason above all others — that *no one's job was to hunt them.* Where the scholars who would become Praxis answered the same horror by studying it, this faction answered by organizing to end it, and they were the first of the Gifted to forge themselves into a true order rather than a mere collection of rivals. For a long age they carried no fixed name at all — *the hunters,* *the ones who burned,* whatever the frightened called them this century or that; the name **Inquisition** would not attach to them until centuries later, and from a source no one inside the order chose. That priority — *kill the thing first; understand it later, if at all* — is in the Inquisition's marrow. It is also the oldest quarrel they have with Praxis: older than the Concordat of Rhodes, older than the MetaConcert, as old as the difference between a sword and a question.

### The Spanish Inquisition

During the bilateral period — before the lattice existed, when the hunters and what would become Praxis were simply two allied survival operations — the order that history would come to know only as **the Inquisition** embedded itself within the Catholic Church's apparatus under Tomás de Torquemada. The historical record remembers Torquemada as a zealot hunting heretics. This is the cover story. The order needed institutional reach, resources, and the authority to move across borders and compel cooperation. The Church provided all of these. Heresy charges provided legal cover for what were actually psidolon hunts and the elimination of Gifted individuals deemed too dangerous or unstable to manage.

The reputation for brutal religious persecution is not entirely fabricated — the cover required real executions to be credible — but the primary targets were always psidolons and compromised Gifted. Torquemada himself was a senior member of the order, not a churchman who happened to share their methods. The infiltration ran so deep, and for so many generations, that when the operation finally ended, the mask did not come off. The order kept the name. There is no cleaner proof of what this Circuit actually is than the fact that its own members, centuries on, still answer to a cover story: the Inquisition wore the Spanish Inquisition the way a psidolon wears a host — and, like a psidolon that stays too long, it never got its own name back.

### The Ivan Incident and the Doctrine Shift

The single event most responsible for the shape of modern Inquisition doctrine is the Old One's occupation of Ivan the Terrible — the full account of which is documented in the psidolon records.

The short version: Alexei Voronov, the finest Inquisitor of his generation, spent two decades developing a slow-careful encirclement methodology against the Old One. He nearly succeeded. The Old One, cornered for the first time in possibly centuries, panicked, fled into the nearest available body — Tsar Ivan IV — and spent the next two decades using the Oprichnina to dismantle the Inquisition's Russian infrastructure and kill Voronov himself. Two generations of institutional capacity destroyed. The slow-careful doctrine that was supposed to deny the psidolon escape had instead given it time to identify its hunter and turn an empire's apparatus against him.

The doctrine shifted to fast-brutal. Immediate engagement upon confirmed identification. No extended observation period. Accept higher collateral damage in exchange for denying the psidolon any window to prepare, flee, or retaliate. This doctrine has governed Inquisition operations ever since.

What the doctrine change did not acknowledge — what Praxis argued unsuccessfully at the time and has not raised since — is that the Ivan incident was evidence of failure against one specific, uniquely capable psidolon, not evidence against slow-careful methodology in general. The fast-brutal doctrine designed to prevent another Ivan has not caught the Old One in four centuries of operation.

The Inquisition is aware of this. They maintain the doctrine anyway, because the alternative — giving any psidolon the window Voronov gave the Old One — is a risk the institution has decided it cannot accept. Whether that decision is correct or whether it is institutional trauma codified as policy is a question nobody in the Inquisition wants to sit with for very long.

## What They Do

### Threat Response

When the Panopticon flags a Gifted for critical fraying — late-stage tether degradation with indicators suggesting Untethering is imminent — the Inquisition responds. The response protocol varies by assessed threat level, but the decision tree is simple: extract to a White Room if possible, eliminate in the field if not.

"If possible" is doing significant work in that sentence. Extraction requires the target to be accessible, cooperative or containable, and located near enough to a White Room to make transport feasible. A critically fraying Gifted in a major city with a White Room on-site might be extracted cleanly. A critically fraying Gifted in a rural area, already exhibiting involuntary projections and body rejection, is a field elimination. The Inquisitor makes the call. The Inquisitor lives with the call.

The false positive rate is the doctrine's most morally corrosive feature. Ten Gifted executed on suspicion for every psidolon successfully prevented — Praxis generated that number, and the Inquisition operates on it. This means Inquisitors know, statistically, that the majority of the people they kill would not have Untethered. They know this and do it anyway, because the math says the alternative is worse.

**And the number cannot be checked.** This is worth going slowly over, because in five hundred years nobody inside the institution appears to have said it aloud. A rate is a ratio, and a ratio requires a denominator: *psidolons successfully prevented.* Count them. Every one is a thing that did not happen, to a person who is not here, leaving a world that produced no evidence in either direction. The Inquisition kills a fraying Gifted and files a prevention — and the entire proof that a monster was averted is that no monster arrived, which is also exactly what you would observe if there had never been one coming.

Ten to one is not a measurement. It is a doctrine wearing a number. You cannot exhume the psidolons that were never born and count them, so there has never been anything to audit, so the figure has stood untouched since the day **Praxis** issued it — the Circuit that had already lied once about what it kept, and was believed anyway, because the alternative was resuming a war. *Nobody has provided better math* is perfectly true. It is true in the way that *nobody has disproved this* is true.

The Inquisition would tell you this changes nothing, and their case is strong: the psidolons they *have* seen are argument enough; a rate you cannot verify is not a rate you may dismiss; someone must act on the best available figure, and there is only one figure. Every word of that is defensible. It is also, precisely, what a person says about the one thing they have decided not to look at — and the Inquisition looks at everything. It is the whole of their pride. They do not sanitize their record, they do not launder their killing, they count their dead and carry them. They have simply never turned that gaze on the arithmetic that tells them who to kill, and the reason is not cowardice. It is that the number came from the only people who could have generated it, and if the number is wrong then the Inquisition is not an institution that makes a terrible necessary choice. It is just an institution that kills people.

Whether the math is right is a question the Inquisition does not ask and Praxis does not revisit.

### Psidolon Elimination

When a psidolon is confirmed — an entity that has fully Untethered and now exists as something other than what it was — the Inquisition's response is immediate and absolute. There is no extraction protocol for psidolons. There is no White Room procedure. There is engagement, as fast and as decisively as possible, before the entity can cause further harm.

Psidolon combat is the most dangerous operational context the Inquisition faces. A psidolon retains the psionic abilities of the Gifted it consumed but is no longer constrained by tether strain — it can use those abilities without limit, without degradation, without the biological feedback mechanisms that prevent a living Gifted from simply burning themselves out. An Inquisitor fighting a psidolon is fighting something that has everything they have and none of their constraints.

Elimination — actual destruction of the psidolon rather than mere displacement — requires Memory Domain techniques. A psidolon coheres around its accumulated memories the way a living mind coheres around its tether. Iterative Memory wipe, systematically destroying that accumulated structure until nothing coherent remains, is the only method that produces a permanent kill. Physical destruction of the host body only displaces the psidolon, forcing it to seek another host or dissipate — a tactical solution, not a resolution.

This means psidolon operations are two-phase engagements. Kinesis-strong Inquisitors contain the threat — suppress the psidolon's psionic output, protect civilians, destroy the host body if necessary to force displacement and buy time. Memory specialists — often Inquisitors with strong Memory capability, sometimes attached specialists — perform the actual elimination under the combat conditions that the Kinesis operators create and hold. Both phases are Inquisition work. Both phases are lethal. The difference is that one is immediate and the other is surgical, and both have to happen under conditions where the psidolon is actively trying to kill everyone in range.

### Project Suppression

The Inquisition handles internal security threats that exceed Panopticon's observational mandate. When Project cells are identified, the Inquisition dismantles them. When apostates flee the lattice, the Inquisition hunts them. When individuals become institutional threats that Correction cannot address — or that the MetaCouncil determines should not be offered Correction — the Inquisition resolves the situation.

This work is less dramatic than psidolon combat and considerably more ethically corrosive. A psidolon is an existential threat that must be destroyed. An apostate is a person who disagreed with the institution and left. An Inquisitor who can maintain the distinction between these two categories while executing both has the specific kind of moral compartmentalization the Circuit requires. An Inquisitor who cannot maintain that distinction either leaves or becomes someone who no longer sees the difference. Both outcomes are common. Neither is discussed openly.

### White Room Staffing

Inquisitors staff White Rooms on rotating assignment. The work is clinical — monitoring subjects brought in for observation, performing terminations when subjects snap, executing preemptive killings when assessment indicates Untethering is imminent. The Maintainer on staff handles lattice isolation and severance. The Inquisitor handles everything else.

Six-month maximum rotation. Mandatory. The psychological toll of performing controlled executions in a sterile medical environment, day after day, on people who sometimes beg, sometimes fight, sometimes simply accept — this breaks people in specific and well-documented ways. The Inquisition tracks the damage. They rotate accordingly. They do not pretend the damage is avoidable.

## Domain Assignment: Why Kinesis

The seven Circuits each align with one of the seven Domains. The Inquisition's Domain is Kinesis.

This is the most straightforward alignment in the entire structure. The Inquisition exists to apply force. Kinesis is the Domain of force — objects moved, barriers shattered, bodies broken by direct application of psionic will. There is no metaphorical subtlety to the pairing. The Inquisition does not dress up what it does. It does not need a metaphor for killing because it does not pretend killing is something other than killing.

The lack of subtlety is itself characterization. Every other Circuit/Domain pairing has layers — the Integrators' Intrusion is invasive in ways that complicate their gentle role; the Operatives' Projection mirrors their existential displacement. The Inquisition's Kinesis is exactly what it looks like. Force. Applied with precision, under pressure, when lives depend on the speed of the response.

That said, Kinesis is not the Inquisition's only tool — and for psidolon elimination specifically, it is not even the finishing weapon. An Inquisitor who could only hit things would be a poor Inquisitor. The actual destruction of a psidolon requires Memory Domain work: iterative dismantling of the entity's accumulated memory structure until it loses coherence and dissipates. Kinesis is what keeps everyone alive long enough for that work to happen — containment, suppression, host destruction to force displacement. The Inquisition produces cross-Domain generalists with strong Memory for the kill and strong Kinesis for the combat conditions under which the kill must happen. The Domain assignment reflects the character of the Circuit, not the totality of its toolkit: when the Inquisition arrives, force arrives. What follows is more surgical, but the first thing you see is Kinesis, and that is not an accident.

## Typical Inquisitor Profile

- Strong Kinesis (primary Domain — combat, containment, immediate threat response)
- Strong Memory (psidolon elimination, information extraction, witness cleanup, field correction)
- Strong Intrusion (interrogation, deception detection, threat assessment; prerequisite for Memory work)
- Strong Barriers (defense against mental assault — non-negotiable for anyone operating against psidolons or hostile Gifted)
- Adequate-to-strong Detection (threat identification, tether assessment in the field)
- Exceptional mental discipline and emotional control
- Willingness to execute other Gifted without hesitation — or more precisely, willingness to execute other Gifted *despite* hesitation, which is not the same thing and is considerably harder
- High tolerance for moral ambiguity sustained over long periods
- Physical fitness (Kinesis supplements but does not replace bodily capability)

## Institutional Character

The Inquisition's culture is built on discipline, certainty, and a specific relationship with guilt that outsiders find difficult to read.

They are not sociopaths. The institutional screening specifically filters for people who understand what they are being asked to do and feel the weight of it. An Inquisitor who feels nothing when they kill is a liability — they lack the judgment that comes from taking the act seriously. An Inquisitor who feels too much is also a liability — they hesitate at the wrong moment and people die. The ideal Inquisitor feels it, files it, and acts anyway. This is not emotional suppression. It is emotional discipline — the ability to experience the full weight of taking a life and not let it slow the hand.

The culture that emerges from this selection pressure is specific. Dark humor is endemic. Gallows jokes are not a coping mechanism; they are the native language of people who deal in death professionally and need to be able to talk about it without drowning. Ritual and protocol are obsessively maintained — not from bureaucratic instinct but because when your job involves making irreversible decisions under extreme pressure, procedure is the scaffolding that keeps judgment from collapsing under its own weight. Personal bonds within the Circuit are intense, private, and rarely discussed with outsiders. Inquisitors trust each other with a completeness that borders on familial, because no one else understands what the work is actually like.

They have a complicated relationship with the concept of mercy. The doctrine says preemptive execution is mercy — better dead than Untethered, better a clean death in a White Room than becoming something that kills dozens. Most Inquisitors believe this. Not because they haven't thought about it, but because they have thought about it extensively, and the alternative — letting critically fraying Gifted live on the chance they might stabilize — has a documented failure rate that produces horrors worse than execution. They have seen those horrors. The belief is not abstract.

The Inquisition's relationship with its own history is unusually honest for a MetaConcert institution. They do not sanitize their record. They do not pretend the false positive rate is acceptable in any moral sense — they maintain it because the actuarial alternative is worse, not because it is good. When an Inquisitor kills someone who would not have Untethered, the institution does not erase that. It files it. The Preservers may edit history elsewhere; within the Inquisition, every kill is documented with unsentimental precision. This is not conscience. It is professional accountability maintained by people who know that losing count would be the beginning of losing themselves.

## Inquisition Doctrine

The Inquisition operates on doctrine that Praxis generated and the MetaCouncil ratified. The Inquisition did not write the justification for what it does. It inherited it.

- **Preemptive execution is mercy.** Better dead than Untethered. A Gifted killed before Untethering dies as themselves. A Gifted who Untethers becomes something else, something that consumes what they were and uses the remains. The execution preserves the person in the only sense that matters — they die as who they are.
- **False positives are acceptable.** Ten Gifted executed on suspicion for every psidolon successfully prevented. The number is Praxis's. The math has been checked. The Inquisition does not like the number. The Inquisition operates on the number.
- **Efficiency over compassion.** Hesitation kills. Not metaphorically — a moment's delay in a White Room or a field engagement produces cascading harm. The doctrine formalizes what Inquisitors learn on their first deployment: that the compassionate impulse to wait, to give someone one more chance, to hope for stabilization, has a body count.
- **The mission supersedes the individual.** An Inquisitor who cannot perform is replaced. An Inquisitor who defects is hunted. An Inquisitor who breaks under the weight is treated with more gentleness than outsiders expect — rotated out, given time, offered support — because the institution knows the weight is real and does not pretend otherwise. But if they cannot return to function, they are retired. The mission continues.

## Inter-Circuit Relationships

**With Praxis:** The oldest relationship in the MetaConcert. Praxis wrote the doctrine the Inquisition operates on, generated the false positive mathematics, developed the tether strain classifications that determine when someone gets flagged. The Inquisition needs Praxis's intellectual framework. Praxis needs the Inquisition's field data. The relationship is functional, mutually dependent, and characterized by a respect that neither Circuit would openly acknowledge. The Inquisition finds Praxis's clinical detachment from the operational consequences of their doctrine frustrating — it is easy to write the math when you are not the one pulling the trigger. Praxis finds the Inquisition's incuriosity about the systems they operate within professionally disappointing. They need each other regardless.

What the functional respect is built over, and never quite buries, is enmity. The oldest relationship in the MetaConcert did not begin as a partnership; it began as a rivalry between the only two powers the Gifted then had — the ones who *burned* and the ones who *kept* — and before the Concordat of Rhodes made allies of them, they were nearer to enemies, with skirmishes that whatever Praxis buried from that era has presumably outlived everyone who could describe them. The Inquisition has not forgotten that it once fought Praxis and survived. It is the only Circuit that can say so, and both Circuits know it.

This is why, when Praxis reaches for a *unit* the Inquisition wants kept whole — an unassigned Gifted slated for "stability" Correction — the Inquisition's countermove is not appeal but *claim:* drag the person formally into Inquisition ranks, where Praxis will not follow, because Inquisition sovereignty is the one boundary Praxis has learned, the hard way, to respect. To be claimed by the Inquisition is the surest shield in the institution against being rewritten by Praxis.

And beneath the professional friction is a contempt the Inquisition would never log in a report. *It is always Praxis's idea* — the lattice they built and abandoned, the doctrine they wrote and never had to pull the trigger on, the elegant solution that becomes someone else's body count. But the deepest part is simpler and colder than grievance. The Inquisition kills people, and *knows* they are people; it is the whole weight that "mercy is a bullet" is meant to carry. Praxis looks at the same Gifted and sees a specimen, a unit, a case — and does not appear to register that there is a person there to lose. The Circuit that pulls the trigger has never once stopped seeing the one it points at. The Circuit that would *save* you stopped seeing you a long time ago. The Inquisition finds that, quietly, the most damning thing it knows about its oldest partner — and the most frightening.

**With the Maintainers:** A working relationship built on shared infrastructure. Inquisitors staff White Rooms that Maintainers designed and operate. The Inquisitor executes; the Maintainer ensures the execution doesn't cascade through the lattice. This produces a specific kind of professional intimacy — two people in a room, one killing and one containing the consequences, over and over. Inquisitors respect Maintainers for their technical precision. Maintainers respect Inquisitors for their discipline. Neither Circuit discusses what the shared work feels like.

**With the Panopticon:** The operational pipeline. Panopticon flags, Inquisition responds. The relationship is efficient and fraught. Panopticon analysts make recommendations from a distance — pattern recognition, tether resonance data, statistical models. Inquisitors act on those recommendations up close, on people with faces and names and fear in their eyes. Inquisitors who staff White Rooms long enough develop a specific resentment toward Panopticon — not for being wrong, but for being remote. For making the call from behind a screen while someone else makes it real.

**With the Integrators:** The most uncomfortable relationship. Before the Integrators existed as a Circuit, the Inquisition handled newly Triggered children. By default. With Inquisition personnel and Inquisition protocols. The Inquisition's response when this was raised — "we've been handling it fine" — is perhaps the most revealing thing they have ever said about themselves. The Integrators now handle what the Inquisition should never have been doing, and every Inquisitor knows this even if the institution has never formally acknowledged it. The Inquisition considers Integrators soft. Integrators consider Inquisitors necessary evils. Both are right. The highest-friction point: when an Inquisitor is dispatched to handle someone an Integrator trained. The Integrator remembers teaching that person to brace. The Inquisitor does not have the luxury of remembering.

**With the Preservers:** Professional and distant. The Preservers archive Inquisition operations with meticulous precision. The Inquisition cooperates because documentation serves institutional accountability. The tension: the Preservers edit records when the MetaCouncil orders it. The Inquisition maintains its own internal records because it does not trust anyone else to preserve the unedited count. Two institutions, both keeping their own books, neither fully confident in the other's version.

**With the Operatives:** Limited operational overlap. Operatives occasionally require Inquisition support for high-risk external operations. The Inquisition provides it professionally and without excessive curiosity about Operative methods. The two Circuits have the cleanest inter-Circuit relationship in the MetaConcert, largely because their operational domains rarely intersect and neither Circuit has reason to resent the other.

## What Other Factions Know

The Inquisition is the Circuit most visible to external factions, though not by name. When the MetaConcert responds to a psidolon with lethal force, or eliminates a Gifted who has become a problem in supernatural territory, other factions see the result. The Camarilla knows the MetaConcert has enforcers. The Technocracy has profiles on Inquisition operational patterns. The Garou have fought Inquisitors and remember it.

What external factions do not fully appreciate is the institutional character. The Camarilla tends to analogize the Inquisition to the Sheriff's office — a crude mapping that misses the doctrinal depth, the moral weight, and the specific psychological culture. The Technocracy models them as paramilitary operators, which is closer but still insufficient. Neither faction grasps that the Inquisition is an institution that has thought carefully about what it does, documented the moral cost in precise detail, and concluded that the cost is justified — not because the Inquisition believes it is good, but because the Inquisition believes the alternatives are measured and worse.

The Projects understand the Inquisition better than anyone outside the MetaConcert, because the Projects are composed of people who left. Rebel in particular has a complex relationship with the Inquisition — many Rebel operatives are former Inquisitors who broke under the weight and channeled the break into resistance rather than collapse. They know the doctrine from the inside. They know why it's wrong. They also know why it's hard to argue with, which is what makes them dangerous to the Inquisition in ways that external critics are not.

## The Uncomfortable Truth

The Inquisition is the MetaConcert's most honest institution. Not most ethical — most honest. They do not pretend that what they do is gentle. They do not launder the killing through euphemism the way the institution launders Correction. They kill people. They know they kill people. They document it, they feel it, and they do it again because the doctrine says the math supports it and nobody has provided better math.

The horror of the Inquisition is not that it contains monsters. It is that it contains people who are doing something monstrous for reasons they have examined carefully and found sufficient. The Inquisitor who kills a critically fraying Gifted is not unthinking. They have thought about it extensively. They have read the actuarial studies. They have seen what psidolons do. They have weighed the false positive rate against the alternative and concluded — not happily, not carelessly, but with the full weight of evidence — that this is what must be done.

That conclusion might be wrong. The Inquisition knows it might be wrong. The doctrine accounts for that possibility and operates anyway, because the cost of hesitation while resolving the uncertainty is measured in bodies.

The people who find the Inquisition most disturbing are not the ones who think Inquisitors are evil. They are the ones who understand the argument, find it difficult to refute, and are horrified by what that implies about the world the argument describes.

## Julian Vire

The Inquisition does not discuss Julian Vire.

This is not because the case is classified. It is because Julian Vire — a barely Triggered civilian with minimal power, hunted by the Inquisition after his mother's defection — resisted sustained Intrusion-based interrogation for weeks. Longer than most trained Inquisitors could manage. And it was not supposed to be *possible.* Raw will does not keep a psion out; only a wall does, and a near-mortal was not supposed to have one. Julian had built one anyway — hunted his whole life, taught mental discipline by an apostate-Inquisitor mother, he had forged from necessity and love the exact defense the entire Gifted art is organized around: untrained, unnamed, and real. His interrogators called it stubbornness because stubbornness was the only word their doctrine had for a thing it insisted could not exist.

He never broke. They never got Eve's location. They never got Lyra's. He took both to his grave.

They executed him anyway.

His resistance has become institutional memory of the most uncomfortable kind: evidence that the qualities the Inquisition values most — unbreakable will, discipline under pressure, commitment to something beyond personal survival — can exist in someone the institution designates as a target. Julian Vire did not merely outlast the Inquisition's interrogators. He defeated them. An untrained civilian with barely any power at all, armed with a wall he had built himself out of nothing but necessity and love, withstood everything the Inquisition could bring to bear and gave them *nothing.* The deepest discomfort is the one they least like to name: the wall that is supposed to be the Gifted's alone was forged, once, by a man who barely was one — the first faint sign of what his daughter would turn out to be.

Julian Vire was, by every measure the Inquisition respects, exactly the kind of person the Inquisition is supposed to produce. And the Inquisition killed him for it.

No formal lesson has been drawn from this. None will be. The lesson is obvious and no one wants to say it out loud.

## Quote

*"I killed my first Gifted when I was nineteen. She was fraying — late stage, involuntary projections, body rejection. She begged me not to. Said she just needed rest. Maybe she was right. Probably she was right. I killed her anyway. Three weeks later, a psidolon killed eleven people in Minneapolis before we contained it. Different psidolon, different case. But I sleep fine. Because I know if I hesitate once, if I give them 'just a little more time,' eventually I'll guess wrong. And eleven people die. Or a hundred. We don't kill them because we hate them. We kill them because we know what they become if we don't."*

**— Inquisition trainer, orientation address**
